# Equity claims vs. the record: PPS rightsizing, 2026

**Purpose:** a media-ready list of places where PPS board members and senior staff describe rightsizing as an equity measure, set against (a) PPS's own data on who is affected, (b) admissions that effects were not modeled, (c) statements that contradict each other, and (d) Board and community questions still unanswered.

**How to read it**
- Quotes are verbatim from YouTube auto-captions or PPS documents. Repairs to captions are in [brackets]. Every quote links to its timestamp.
- **Speaker** lines say who said it and how sure we are. "**SPEAKER TO CONFIRM**" means the captions don't identify the person; the link goes to the moment so it can be checked.
- Demographic and distance figures come from PPS's per-school family guides (Oct 2026), "No changes" columns unless stated. Averages are simple averages of the schools' own percentages, computed from `analysis/data/school_impact.csv`.
- Labels: **[Said]** something said in a meeting; **[Doc]** a PPS document; **Analysis:** our inference.
- PPS's own counter-arguments are summarized at the end. A reporter will hear them, so they are stated fairly.

## A. "Every focal group gains": what the equity metric actually measures

**A1. The headline equity claim measures school size, not services or outcomes.**
- [Said] "Doing nothing leaves about half of all students and between 54 and 59% of each focal group in a school that is not sustainable. Both scenarios improve results for every group, and every focal group gains more than students overall." Speaker: Margaret Calvert (high, speaker-checked transcript) [YT:xByUlijpcnE@4:08:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xByUlijpcnE&t=14891s).
- [Doc] A "sustainable" school is one above the enrollment threshold (360 for K-5) [BB:14511742 p.10](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14511742?org=915#page=10). The metric therefore counts students in bigger schools.
- [Doc] PPS's own written answer: "there is no data supporting that smaller schools have higher test scores. Test scores are better predicted by socio-economic status than school size" [BB:14493920 p.27](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14493920?org=915#page=27).
- [Doc] "Class size capping is not a part of the rightsizing conversation" [BB:14493920 p.33](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14493920?org=915#page=33).
- [Said] Asked whether consolidated schools would be better resourced: "we're not going to necessarily see more" [YT:9kX3vnfRlvY@4:40:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kX3vnfRlvY&t=16844s). **SPEAKER TO CONFIRM** (staff, Nov 4).
- [Doc] Vice-Chair Chase-Miller's written questions "What specific resources will students gain in the receiving school?" and "Will class sizes get larger, smaller, or remain the same?" were still "In process" on Oct 30 [BB:14618390 p.37](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14618390?org=915#page=37) [BB:14618390 p.38](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14618390?org=915#page=38).
- **Analysis:** the district's main equity number rises by construction when small schools close. PPS has not said what any focal student would gain, and its own data says size does not predict scores.

**A2. The thresholds the metric rests on are called stopgaps by the staff who set them.**
- [Said] "This is a bridge just like with what we ... not what we would say is ideal state." Speaker: Jon Franco (per archive) [YT:zOUH2Jq7Owo@1:38:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zOUH2Jq7Owo&t=5883s).
- [Said] On whether the 360/500 thresholds are right: "I am actually concerned that they're low." Speaker: Margaret Calvert (medium; La Forte asked, Franco referred to "Margaret's point" next) [YT:9kX3vnfRlvY@4:37:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kX3vnfRlvY&t=16663s).
- [Doc] The Board-commissioned SEGC equity audit proposed floors of about 450/600/650 [BB:3997427 p.146](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/3997427?org=915#page=146).

## B. Who is actually affected: PPS's own numbers

**B1. Closures fall hardest on students with disabilities.**
- [Doc] Average share of students receiving special education at the closing schools: **21.6%** (Scenario A, 14 schools) and **22.6%** (Scenario B, 11), against **17.9%** at every other school.
- [Doc] Among the closing schools, Woodmere (30.4%), Creston (31.0%) and Peninsula (30.1%) have the highest special-education shares [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/woodmere.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1VrGxkY8mrjTU6neXgKChfbYmXGah9Pm9&usp=drive_copy) [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/creston.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=17XkDaems0GP2Kkj4Udax81mvsryvOcjC&usp=drive_copy) [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/peninsula.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=15xt7PqKoHCdKNXu2tSzPph_CbsN3yJD2&usp=drive_copy).
- [Doc] Students receiving special education are one of the four focal groups in the KPIs [BB:14430171 p.20](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14430171?org=915#page=20).
- [Doc] Chase-Miller's question on protecting "student belonging, cultural identity, and school-community relationships, especially for racially minoritized students" was "In process" on Oct 30 [BB:14618390 p.38](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14618390?org=915#page=38).

**B2. The closing schools are the district's most walkable, which contradicts the top-ranked guiding principle.**
- [Doc] Share of students living within one mile: **78.6%** at Scenario A's closing schools and **80.4%** at B's, against about **55–56%** at the other schools.
- [Doc] Guiding principle: educational options "maintaining a strong network of neighborhood schools" [BB:14615901 p.5](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14615901?org=915#page=5). In PPS's survey it ranked first: 41% put "Strong Neighborhood Schools" first, versus 17% for the Equity Lens [BB:14615901 p.12](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14615901?org=915#page=12).
- [Doc] Highest-poverty receiving school: **Rosa Parks** (79.4% poverty, 81.9% underserved racial groups). The share of students within a mile falls from **100% to 55.2%** under A and B [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/rosa_parks.pdf p.3](https://drive.google.com/open?id=16Pi-v6Q2LWPMowzGueph9W3W2L-W6lNx&usp=drive_copy) [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/rosa_parks.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=16Pi-v6Q2LWPMowzGueph9W3W2L-W6lNx&usp=drive_copy).
- [Doc] **Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.**: 100% → 64.7% [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/dr_martin_luther_king_jr.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1PvmXVFn4vmiK10t3-grEiwc5irdm_LgB&usp=drive_copy). **Scott** (51.9% poverty) under A: 91.9% → 46.7% [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/scott.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1JXoJ_9D2XYpIvu0JO-OJENWTfISIbQml&usp=drive_copy).
- [Doc] **Harriet Tubman MS** (50.5% underserved racial groups): within a mile falls from 23.7% to 12.5% [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/harriet_tubman.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1xmwGeM5Mp_JxLX-X4mfW_y19bTsVZx6R&usp=drive_copy). Asked by La Forte whether Tubman's relocation was considered: "We have not um incorporated it into this plan." **SPEAKER TO CONFIRM** (staff) [YT:9kX3vnfRlvY@4:30:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kX3vnfRlvY&t=16234s).
- [Doc] No transportation cost or ride-time estimates exist; these "will require further implementation planning" [BB:14509719 p.2](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14509719?org=915#page=2). Chase-Miller's question on "transportation costs, travel-time burdens, and safety" was "In process" on Oct 30 [BB:14618390 p.38](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14618390?org=915#page=38).
- [Doc] The SEGC audit found "the burden of travel falling hardest on families with the least means" [BB:3997427 p.18](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/3997427?org=915#page=18).

**B3. Title I: staff acknowledge the pattern, then repeat it.**
- [Said] "we often disrupt and displace title one schools more than higher [SES] schools. So we're conscious of both pieces." **SPEAKER TO CONFIRM** (staff, Nov 4) [YT:9kX3vnfRlvY@4:33:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kX3vnfRlvY&t=16438s).

In B that is 6 of 11 closures (55%), against 35 of 82 schools districtwide (43%). In A it is 6 of 14 (43%).
- [Doc] Among them: Woodmere, 59.0% poverty, 25.1% multilingual, 30.4% special education [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/woodmere.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1VrGxkY8mrjTU6neXgKChfbYmXGah9Pm9&usp=drive_copy); Marysville, 58.3% poverty [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/marysville.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1ZJXNeZJHjc7sKHR0E_iiA7XJEGXf0NsQ&usp=drive_copy); James John, 54.5% poverty and 63.8% underserved racial groups [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/james_john.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1fEKbTKwDd1AQ5C21l30ntcX0x0BJ6WKq&usp=drive_copy).
- [Doc] Receiving schools lose Title I. Brentwood MS goes from 57.5% poverty and Title I "Yes" to 32.1% (A) or 30.6% (B) and "No" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/brentwood.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1tvGte5mkRfRnQ9oHniBOF3dd1wdQ7H05&usp=drive_copy). Markham under A goes from "Yes" to "No" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/markham.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1Qx13JVd92AZdJotx-gADCdBr0LyczHyT&usp=drive_copy).
- [Said] "They could lose the title one funding. That is true. I don't want to say they wouldn't." **SPEAKER TO CONFIRM** (staff) [YT:9kX3vnfRlvY@4:32:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kX3vnfRlvY&t=16353s).
- [Doc] Engelsman's written question "Why do we have a list that has close to 50% Title I schools closing?" was "In process" on Oct 30 [BB:14618390 p.42](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14618390?org=915#page=42).
- [Doc] The KPI "Number of Title I and CSI/TSI schools impacted" was listed but never reported [BB:14430171 p.20](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14430171?org=915#page=20).

**B4. On poverty and race overall, the closure list mirrors the district.** This is stated for accuracy.
- Average poverty at closing schools is 32.2% (A) / 35.1% (B), against 32.7% / 32.2% elsewhere. Underserved racial groups: 33.3% (A) / 37.4% (B), against 36.2% / 35.5%.
- **Analysis:** the sharpest disparities are disability, walkability and Title I status at receiving schools, not the poverty or race share of the closing list.

## C. "Outcome, not intention": repeat displacement

**C1. SEGC lessons stated, then applied selectively.**
- [Said] "as a alum of the SEGC process I'm very adamant that equity must be the outcome and not just the intention." Speaker: Chair Eddie Wang (high) [YT:5RMfvT-sAPU@2:23:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5RMfvT-sAPU&t=8625s).
- [Said] "Open seats don't make every school ready. In a community that's been displaced before, where students end up is an equity decision, not just a capacity decision." Speaker: Margaret Calvert (high) [YT:uAf7l-ChjFo@2:51:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uAf7l-ChjFo&t=10306s).
- [Doc] Staff answer to Engelsman (Wells area): "the quantitative model optimizes for building utilization rather than non-measurable social networks" [BB:14618390 p.36](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14618390?org=915#page=36).
- [Said] Bridger kept its focus option "because Bridger's gone through substantial amount of changes through the Southeast Guiding Coalition process." **SPEAKER TO CONFIRM** (staff) [YT:zOUH2Jq7Owo@3:36:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zOUH2Jq7Owo&t=12971s).
- [Doc] Other SEGC-affected schools close:
  - Lewis, whose projection SEGC cut from 358 to 281, closes in A [BB:3997428 p.66](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/3997428?org=915#page=66);
  - Marysville, which received displaced SEGC students, closes in both [BB:3997428 p.34](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/3997428?org=915#page=34) [BB:3997428 p.39](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/3997428?org=915#page=39);

## D. Admissions that effects were not modeled

**D1. Families leaving the district.**
- [Said] "What we don't have is a capture rate. The data is not available for a grades one through 12 capture rate ... Is it true that some kids may leave the district if we do right sizing? That is true." **SPEAKER TO CONFIRM** (staff, Nov 4) [YT:9kX3vnfRlvY@5:01:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kX3vnfRlvY&t=18072s).
- [Said] On families who say they might leave: "We're not forcing anybody to stay in our district." Speaker: Michelle DePass (high; chair calls on her at 3:54:20) [YT:uAf7l-ChjFo@4:00:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uAf7l-ChjFo&t=14420s).

The Sept 15 framework promised "An equity analysis review attached to every scenario" [BB:14430171 p.7](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14430171?org=915#page=7).
- [Said] On Oct 20, after engagement had largely run (Oct 6–28): the analysis "attached to each scenario ... will be coming forth as well in the November recommendation." Speaker: Jon Franco (high, speaker-checked) [YT:xByUlijpcnE@3:58:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xByUlijpcnE&t=14291s).

## E. Savings: the message changes by audience

- [Said] Oct 6: "we are not um exclaiming some you know huge dollar amount from closures and savings because ... our educators and staff generally move with students." Speaker: Armstrong (medium) [YT:zOUH2Jq7Owo@3:44:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zOUH2Jq7Owo&t=13463s).
- [Said] Oct 13, OPB, one minute apart:
  - "we are reducing the expense of the district so that we can get to a point where we no longer have to worry about budget cuts anymore" [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:19:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kdPfX6KH05c&t=1147s);
  - "we're not um closing schools to save on the budget" [YT:kdPfX6KH05c@0:20:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kdPfX6KH05c&t=1208s).
  - Speaker: Chair Eddie Wang (high, radio guest).
- [Said] Oct 20: "Scenario A, which models 14 school building closures, estimates approximately $14 million in operational savings." Speaker: Armstrong (high, speaker-checked) [YT:xByUlijpcnE@1:14:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xByUlijpcnE&t=4499s).
- [Doc] Ten days later, Engelsman's "What is the actual net savings to closing Sellwood? What are the net savings to closing any school?" was still "In process" [BB:14618390 p.35](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14618390?org=915#page=35). Staff concede closed buildings keep costing (utilities, security, insurance, repairs), and that "financial estimates will need to account for ongoing and one-time costs when calculating net savings" [BB:14618390 p.39](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14618390?org=915#page=39).

Also: "the trade-offs are actually to our most marginalized communities more" if cuts are spread across all schools [YT:9kX3vnfRlvY@4:40:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kX3vnfRlvY&t=16821s) (**SPEAKER TO CONFIRM**).
