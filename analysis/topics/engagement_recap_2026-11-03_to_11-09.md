## What PPS reports

- **Survey responses:** 3,559 in total [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-11-03_to_11-09_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-11-03_to_11-09_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf), up from 3,408 in the Oct 29-Nov 2 recap [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-10-29_to_11-02_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-10-29_to_11-02_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf).
  - The executive summary dates the count "As of November 3rd" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-11-03_to_11-09_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-11-03_to_11-09_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf) and the body "As of November 9th" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-11-03_to_11-09_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-11-03_to_11-09_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf).
  - The by-school appendix is labeled "as of 11/2/26" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-11-03_to_11-09_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf p.7](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-11-03_to_11-09_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf), although its counts are higher than last week's.
- **This week's focus: receiving schools.**
  - 1,245 families at schools facing boundary changes responded. Their "primary concern ... centers on building capacity, facility strain, and expanding class sizes," and they ask the district to "guarantee physical building upgrades and baseline staffing allocations" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-11-03_to_11-09_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf p.5](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-11-03_to_11-09_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf).
  - 179 receiving-school staff responded [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-11-03_to_11-09_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf p.6](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-11-03_to_11-09_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf):
    - Special-education case managers, SLPs, counselors and behavior specialists "are already operating at capacity."
    - They ask that support staff be added "before enrollment increases occur."
    - Absorbing higher-income neighborhoods could push a school's low-income share "below federal funding thresholds."
- **Responses by school (appendix):** Maplewood 180, up from 177 last week. Hayhurst 34, Rieke 34, Markham 39, Stephenson 244. The total is 6,200, against 5,994 last week; some respondents list more than one school [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/engagement/2026-11-03_to_11-09_engagement_recap_what_we_heard.pdf pp.8-9].

## Analysis

- **The receiving-school concerns match the open Southwest issues.** Rieke was not among the 21 walked sites. PPS's special-education profile for Maplewood, Rieke, Hayhurst and Markham leaves out Maplewood's Learning Center and speech students (`analysis/meetings/2026-11-10_tle_materials.md`).
- **The funding-threshold concern applies to Markham.** Markham's poverty rate falls from 38.3% to 25.7% in Scenario A (`analysis/data/school_impact.csv`).
- **Maplewood's response count stands out.** Its 180 responses are about five times Hayhurst's or Rieke's, and are among the highest of any elementary school.
