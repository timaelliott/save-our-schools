# AD 4.10.049: Student Assignment Review & School Boundary Changes

Source: Administrative Directive 4.10.049-AD, 5 pages, linked from the pps.net rightsize FAQ (sibling question) and archived 2026-11-11
[PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/policies/4.10.049-AD_student_assignment_review_school_boundary_changes.pdf]. The PDF's metadata dates it 2009 (modified 2015). It is not a new rightsizing document. It is the existing directive that the FAQ cites and that Director Engelsman measured Scenarios A and B against on Oct 20, 2026 [YT:xByUlijpcnE@5:40:29].

## What the directive requires

- **Options before boundary changes.** Staff analyze whether enrollment is "significantly greater or less than the building capacity". The options include transfer limits, portables or repurposed space, moving or closing programs, grade reconfiguration (K-8, K-6), opening or closing a school "as provided in 6.10.030-P", and boundary changes [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/policies/4.10.049-AD_student_assignment_review_school_boundary_changes.pdf p.2].
- **Input.** At least one public meeting, plus at least one notice to every family whose students "would be directly impacted within two years of the change" [p.3].
- **Six non-prioritized factors** [p.3–4]:
  - stable feeder patterns (each K-5 school feeding "no more than two middle schools");
  - diverse demographics;
  - compact boundaries (safe routes, keeping neighborhoods together, shorter travel);
  - optimal use of existing facilities;
  - stable enrollment at surrounding schools;
  - limited impact on students ("Affect the smallest number of students possible").
- **What the Superintendent's recommendation must include** [p.4]:
  - the schedule;
  - projected impact at affected schools on "enrollment, school building utilization, student body demographics, transportation and program offerings";
  - any exceptions to assignment rules;
  - "An analysis of school boundary factors."
- **Timing.** Final approval should come "no later than January of the calendar year for implementation the following school year" [p.4].
- **Grandfathering and siblings** [p.5]:
  - students in a changed boundary may stay at their current school "through the highest grade" or attend the new school;
  - younger siblings are "guaranteed a space at the former neighborhood school" if they apply on time and an older sibling will still attend there;
  - transfer students may stay through the highest grade.

## Why it matters for the Southwest package

Analysis:
- Moving Maplewood, Hayhurst, Rieke, Markham, Stephenson or Capitol Hill families changes boundaries. Read literally, the forthcoming recommendation owes the Board, for each affected school:
  - enrollment, utilization, demographic and transportation impacts;
  - a written analysis of the six factors [p.4].
  Check for both when the recommendation posts.
- Section VII sets a default of grandfathering and sibling guarantees [p.5]. The FAQ defers sibling details to "the Superintendent's recommendation" (see `analysis/refresh_2026-10-22.md`). Whether the recommendation departs from the default should also be checked.
- The directive targets January approval for implementation the next school year [p.4]. Against that, the FAQ says implementation will begin "no later than the 2027-28 school year" (see `faq_2026-11-10.html`). Whether a Nov/Dec 2026 vote meets that window depends on what the recommendation phases in for fall 2027.
- Section VIII exempts temporary relocations for renovation or emergencies [p.5]. That is relevant to any Southwest arrangement tied to seismic or bond work.
