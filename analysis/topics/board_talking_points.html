Talking points that fit

  • Her committee’s hard numbers don’t hold for Hayhurst. The Nov 9 profile gives 525 seats, but its own room counts (15 classrooms, 7 special-ed rooms) and the deck’s own rules on p.6 give 456 to 468. It lists the building’s record as 640 students in 2019-20; PPS’s model shows 396 that year, and the most the building has held is 499. Its special-education box says 4 rooms while the left panel says 7 BoardBook 14644366 p.19 BoardBook 14644366 p.6.

  • Hayhurst is not being made bigger. The same profile lists “Planned Future Investments: None.” Its only urgent item is a $30,100 kitchen addition BoardBook 14644366 p.19. She told families PPS would build more room rather than crowd students; for Hayhurst nothing is planned.

  • Scenario A moves students into the building that needs the seismic money. Maplewood’s worst part scores 7.5 and most of its parts already have retrofit work BoardBook 3991109 p.221. Rieke scores 9.6 in an unreinforced-masonry part; a complete retrofit was estimated at $6.04M BoardBook 3991109 p.233. The Nov 9 deck redirects Rose City Park and Beach seismic money to Rieke and says PPS is applying for a state grant for it BoardBook 14644366 p.31. No date is given for that work.

  • Her “north star” families move even though their school stays open. Analysis Scenario A shifts part of Hayhurst’s boundary to Rieke. Hayhurst’s poverty rate falls from 26.2% to 15.5% and Rieke’s rises from 15.2% to 24.0% Hayhurst guide Rieke guide. Our reading of the boundary map puts the Stephens Creek Crossing public housing in the moved area boundary analysis.

  • She asked how many families drive. On Oct 20 she said walkability is a guiding principle but “I don’t know what that number is” Oct 20, 6:12:14. PPS’s own guides show the share living within a mile dropping at both receiving schools (see the numbers above). Staff have promised a sample bus route with the Nov 17 recommendation Nov 4, 5:18:01.

The ask

As FIO chair: have staff reissue the Hayhurst profile with the room-by-room math, add a profile for Rieke, and date Rieke’s retrofit before any student is assigned there. Then: Take Maplewood out of the Dec 15 closure package. Run the Southwest boundary review among the eight Wells-area elementaries instead, after Rieke’s retrofit is scheduled and Hayhurst’s capacity is checked room by room. At the least, move the Southwest changes to phase two (2028-29), the year staff reserve for schools where “the readiness isn’t quite there” Nov 4, 5:19:50.

Lead with something else. Analysis Cost arguments and crowd size have not moved her; she has said savings are not the point and frames resisting organized parents as courage stance file. Keep it to her committee’s numbers and her own tests.

Talking points that fit

  • Maplewood to Rieke is the reverse of “consolidate into safer schools.” Students leave a building whose worst part scores 7.5, with most parts already retrofitted, for one that scores 9.6 in an unreinforced-masonry part BoardBook 3991109 p.221 BoardBook 3991109 p.233. PPS told the Oregonian seismic risk “wasn’t a driver” in choosing closures or receivers Oregonian, Oct 27. Staff on Oct 20 called facilities a possible “tiebreaker” Oct 20, 5:50:08. In the Southwest, where the three schools are close in size, the building is the tiebreaker, and Maplewood’s is the safer one.

  • One school, four directions. Scenario A sends 70% of Maplewood to Hayhurst and 30% to Rieke, moves its K-2 Intensive Skills Classroom to Markham, and switches its middle-grade path from Jackson to Gray Maplewood guide. A slice of Hayhurst also moves to Rieke. That is the “slicing up subgroups” she said she struggles with.

  • The Intensive Skills students are the clearest case of depositing. The special-education class goes to Markham, which the Nov 9 deck rates “Not Yet Ready/Significant Barriers,” noting “Space constraints. Limited street frontage” BoardBook 14644366 p.21. Her written question on protecting belonging and identity in receiving schools was still “In process” on Oct 30 BoardBook 14618390 p.38.

  • Low-income families move twice over. Analysis Hayhurst’s poverty rate drops from 26.2% to 15.5% and Rieke’s rises from 15.2% to 24.0% because part of Hayhurst’s boundary moves Hayhurst guide Rieke guide. Families in that area change schools although their school stays open, and they land in the higher-risk building.

  • PPS named Maplewood a bright spot for Latino students on Nov 4. Staff presenting state test results (Dr. Kelly Charles, per Director Engelsman) said: “We are seeing exciting results for Latin Latino students at schools such as [Whitman], Maplewood, and [Bridlemile].” Nov 4, 1:53:22. Maplewood’s math proficiency rose from 60.1% to 65.3% BoardBook 14615681 p.14. Closing it splits the staff and practices behind those gains.

Numbers she can use

  • Her $250,000 rule covers Maplewood. She proposed closing no school with more than $250,000 of bond-funded work Oregonian, Oct 20. KGW reported about $1.5M of roof-level seismic work at Maplewood KGW, Oct 9; the Holmes assessment lists its 2015 roof seismic upgrades BoardBook 3991109 p.108.

  • The OSAS figures behind her Nov 4 point. Maplewood ELA 62.4% → 64.0%, math 60.1% → 65.3% from 2024-25 to 2025-26; Rieke fell in both (85.3% → 80.5%, 82.7% → 76.5%) BoardBook 14615681 p.14.

  • Maplewood is growing. 304 students on Oct 1, 16 above PSU’s forecast and more than Hayhurst (286) or Rieke (254) Oct 1 enrollment p.2.

  • Hayhurst would be back at its 2016 overcrowding level. Peak 469 against a room-rule capacity of 456–468; in 2016 PPS called 499 students “Over-Crowding” and moved a program out BoardBook 14644366 p.19 2016 KPIs p.3.

Questions for the record

  1. Show the room-by-room math for Hayhurst’s 525 seats using the rules on p.6 of the Nov 9 deck. Why does the profile list 7 special-ed rooms on one side and 4 on the other BoardBook 14644366 p.19?
  2. Why does the profile give Hayhurst’s largest historic enrollment as 640 in 2019-20, when PPS’s own records show 396 that year and a peak of 499 in 2015-16?
  3. Rieke gains about 117 students at peak (our count from the family guides FIO notes) and gets reallocated seismic money, but has no readiness profile BoardBook 14644366 p.31. When will the work on Building Parts A, B and C be finished, and will students move in before it is?
  4. Why are Sitton (111% at peak) and Whitman (112%) rated “Ready” while George (107%) is “Not Yet Ready” BoardBook 14644366 p.27 BoardBook 14644366 p.29?
  5. Maplewood’s Intensive Skills Classroom goes to Markham, rated “Not Yet Ready/Significant Barriers.” What is the plan and date for that room BoardBook 14644366 p.21?
  6. On the seismic slide, Scenario B leaves 33.6% of students in buildings with a part scoring above 9.0, against 33.5% with no changes. How is that a reduction BoardBook 14644366 p.33?
  7. Staff called facilities a possible tiebreaker Oct 20, 5:50:08. In the Southwest, was it used, and why did the 9.6 building win over the 7.5 one?
