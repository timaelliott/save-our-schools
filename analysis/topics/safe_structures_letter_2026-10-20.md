# Safe Structures PPS letter on seismic safety and rightsizing (Oct 20, 2026)

Source: [`sources/community/safe_structures_pps/2026-10-20_safe_structures_pps_letter_rightsizing_seismic.pdf`](../../sources/community/safe_structures_pps/2026-10-20_safe_structures_pps_letter_rightsizing_seismic.pdf) (provenance in that folder's `_manifest.json`).

Safe Structures PPS is the parent group that pushed for seismic money in the 2025 bond. This letter is an **advocacy source**: the student counts are the group's own estimates, not PPS figures.

## What it is
- A four-page letter to Superintendent Armstrong and the Board, dated Oct 20, 2026, titled "Re: Seismic safety implications of the proposed Rightsizing scenarios" [COMM:safe_structures_pps/2026-10-20_safe_structures_pps_letter_rightsizing_seismic.pdf p.1].
- **Press coverage.** Willamette Week reported it the same day and repeated it in Murmurs on Oct 21 [NEWS:NEWS-20261020-wweek-1](https://www.wweek.com/news/schools/2026/10/20/parent-group-asks-pps-for-seismic-bond-spending-plan-amid-rightsizing/).
- **PPS's response, via WW.** If a school slated for seismic work closes, "staff will utilize our already-established seismic project selection strategy to identify and propose projects for a board decision" [NEWS:NEWS-20261020-wweek-1](https://www.wweek.com/news/schools/2026/10/20/parent-group-asks-pps-for-seismic-bond-spending-plan-amid-rightsizing/).

## The group's estimates: students in high-risk schools, 2028
Elementary and middle schools, using PPS's 2028 enrollment estimates per scenario [COMM:safe_structures_pps/2026-10-20_safe_structures_pps_letter_rightsizing_seismic.pdf p.1]:

| | Status quo | Scenario A | Scenario B |
|---|---|---|---|
| Students in URM schools (after planned retrofits) | 3,139 (12%) | 2,725 (10%) | 2,723 (10%) |
| Students at schools with a building part rated 9+ (Holmes) | 9,689 (37%) | 10,089 (39%) | 9,709 (37%) |

- **Rose City Park.** Its Scenario A closure is not counted as a URM reduction, because it is slated for a full retrofit under the status quo (footnote 1, p.1).
- **Students sent to other high-risk schools.** The group estimates "roughly 1,500 to 1,900" students from closing high-risk schools would move into other schools scoring 9.0 or higher [COMM:safe_structures_pps/2026-10-20_safe_structures_pps_letter_rightsizing_seismic.pdf p.2]:
  - The schools named are Rieke (9.6), Roseway Heights (9.3), Rigler (9.2), César Chávez (9.1), Ockley Green (9.1), Boise-Eliot (9.1), Abernethy (9.1), Brentwood (9.0) and Hosford (9.0).
  - The letter says none of them is in the bond's first round of seismic projects.
  - About 400 of those students would go to URM schools with no scheduled retrofit: Rigler, Abernethy and Rieke (same page).
- **No seismic measure in PPS's comparison.** The letter notes that PPS's scenario comparison measures sustainability, student moves and equity, "but includes no seismic measure" (p.2).

## Closures at schools with seismic investment (as the letter states it)
[COMM:safe_structures_pps/2026-10-20_safe_structures_pps_letter_rightsizing_seismic.pdf p.2]
- **Beach:** in the 2025 bond's first round, a targeted retrofit of roughly $10M scheduled for summer 2028. Closes in A and B.
- **Rose City Park:** one of two full-campus retrofits in that round. Closes in A.
- **Marysville:**
  - completed a retrofit of about $7M in September 2026; closes in A and B;
  - $2.5M came from the state Seismic Rehabilitation Grant Program, which, in the group's reading, "would appear to require PPS to continue operating Marysville as a school for 10 years or risk having to repay the grant."
- **Lewis:** a retrofit of about $1.2M. Closes in A.
- **Roof-level seismic work:** Sellwood MS, Maplewood, Stephenson and Sabin together got about $4.8M. All close in A, and all but Stephenson in B.
- **Media totals:** the letter cites media estimates of nearly $18M already spent at schools on the closure lists, and about $40M more planned for Rose City Park and Beach.
  - Its footnote 2 says media reports misstated Rose City Park's retrofit cost and combined roof repairs with bond seismic work, apparently based on PPS information.

## What it asks
Five questions, to be answered publicly before the Board decides [COMM:safe_structures_pps/2026-10-20_safe_structures_pps_letter_rightsizing_seismic.pdf p.3]:
1. How each scenario changes the number of students in 9+ and URM schools, and whether PPS will add those measures to its scenario comparison.
2. Whether the Holmes scores and the 2025 bond seismic plan were considered in choosing which schools close and which receive students.
3. Where Beach's or Rose City Park's committed bond seismic funds would go, by what criteria and on what timeline.
4. What happens to closing schools that already had retrofits paid for by earlier bonds.
5. The plan and schedule for seismic work at the high-risk schools that would receive students.

The letter also argues that Res. 7109 presumes the funds go first to the 8–10 highest-risk schools, and that any deviation needs a written showing and Board approval (p.3).

- **Abernethy as URM.** Holmes lists 1,800 sq ft of URM in Abernethy's 1924 Building A, with a URM-only retrofit estimate of $450,000 [BB:3991109 p.164](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/3991109?org=915#page=164). That supports the letter's URM label, but `data/seismic_facilities.csv` marks Abernethy `has_urm_building_part = no`. That column should be rechecked.
- **Marysville's cost.** The letter's "about $7 million" is consistent with the archive's $6,750,403 for the 2020-bond plus SRGP retrofit (`data/k5_seismic_retrofit_costs_closures.csv`).
- **Marysville's 10-year condition.** PPS's bond status report says the work was built to Category IV "immediate occupancy" "as required as part of the SRGP grant funding", with an SRGP completion date of September 30, 2026 [BB:3996450 p.2](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/3996450?org=915#page=2). WW later quoted the Business Oregon contract itself: the project must be used for its existing purposes for ten years after completion (to 2036), and Business Oregon said an early closure could be treated as a default, though it would work with PPS [NEWS:NEWS-20261016-wweek-1](https://www.wweek.com/news/schools/2026/10/16/by-closing-marysville-elementary-pps-could-be-at-risk-of-defaulting-on-a-state-contract/). The archive still has no copy of the contract.
- **Not checked:** the group's student totals (3,139 / 9,689 and the scenario figures). They would need PPS's per-school 2028 projections for every scenario joined to Holmes scores.
- **The two first-round schools now proposed for closure.** In January and February 2026 Jon Franco told the Board's facilities committee that none of the nine retrofit schools were closure candidates [NEWS:NEWS-20260122-wweek-1](https://www.wweek.com/news/schools/2026/01/22/pps-moves-forward-on-seismic-retrofits-despite-pushback/) [NEWS:NEWS-20260218-wweek-1](https://www.wweek.com/news/schools/2026/02/18/the-pps-seismic-conversation-grows-increasingly-complicated/). Beach (A and B) and Rose City Park (A) are on that list. This is the gap behind the letter's question 3.

## PPS's fuller response (Oregonian, Oct 27, 2026)
- Jon Franco said seismic safety was not a key "driver" of either list; proximity and available space were the primary measures for the "safe and healthy" goal [NEWS:NEWS-20261027-oregonian-1](https://www.oregonlive.com/education/2026/10/how-did-earthquake-risks-factor-into-school-closure-plans-pps-says-it-wasnt-a-driver.html).
- If Rose City Park ($21M full retrofit, planned 2028–29) and Beach ($10M) close, staff will "identify and propose new projects and timelines for Board direction" under the Board's seismic formula. He did not say whether receiving schools would be prioritized. Teams are walking through receiving schools and working with Holmes on their seismic needs (same).
- The paper names Rieke, Jackson MS and Sunrise as very-high-risk schools left out of the first round that could become candidates (same).
- Analysis: this answers the letter's question 3 only in general terms; questions 1, 2, 4 and 5 remain unanswered in the archive.
