# PPS 2026 Rightsizing ("Growing Great Schools" Phase III / "Rightsize. Right Support. Right for Students.")

Synthesis as of 2026-10-10. Scope: the districtwide rightsizing / school-closure process and the linked enrollment-balancing work (Southeast Enrollment & Program Balancing / SEGC, Jefferson dual-assignment sunset, K-8 / middle-grades configuration, focus options, dual language immersion placement).

Companion files:
- `analysis/data/rightsizing_scenarios.csv`: one row per school per scenario (Status Quo, A, B, plus the Dec 2025 Jefferson is Rising high-school scenarios).
- `analysis/data/school_metrics.csv`: per-school metrics (long format) with a source on every row.
- `analysis/topics/rightsizing_criteria_and_principles.md`: guiding principles, criteria, thresholds and KPIs, quoted, with how they changed between drafts.
- `analysis/topics/rightsizing_open_questions.md`: contradictions, data gaps and items to verify against video.

Conventions: "PPS states" = what a PPS document says; "minutes record" = adopted/draft minutes; **Analysis:** = my own inference or arithmetic. Unless noted, enrollment figures are October 1, 2025 counts from the April 20, 2026 map table [BB:3997157 p.1].

## 1. Bottom line (as of October 10, 2026)

- **Two scenarios plus a status quo were released October 5–6, 2026.** Scenario A closes 14 schools and Scenario B closes 11 [BB:14509719 p.1] [BB:14505788 p.1]. The only differences are Stephenson, Rose City Park and Lewis, which close under A and stay open under B [BB:14509719 p.1].
- **Schools closing in both scenarios (11):** Beach, Buckman, Creston, Irvington, James John, Maplewood, Marysville, Peninsula, Sabin, Sellwood MS and Woodmere [BB:14505788 p.2]. **Closing only under A:** Lewis, Rose City Park and Stephenson [BB:14505788 p.2].
- **Both scenarios also include:** eight grade reconfigurations. Seven K-8s become K-5 (Beverly Cleary, Laurelhurst, Sunnyside, Bridger Creative Science, César Chávez, Faubion, Vernon) and MLC becomes K-8 [BB:14509719 p.2] [BB:14509719 p.3]. They also include the discontinuation of Sunnyside's environmental focus option [BB:14509719 p.2] [BB:14509719 p.3], the move of Odyssey to the MLC site (leaving East Sylvan vacant) [BB:14509719 p.2], and the closure of the MLC 9–12 program, which is "not counted as a school closure" [BB:14505788 p.2].
- **District-level outcomes in 2031–32.** Schools above the sustainability threshold: 67% under A, 57% under B, 26% under the status quo. Students attending a school above the threshold: 80%, 73% and 48% [BB:14505788 p.1]. K-8 students who would move: 7,981 (27%) under A and 6,988 (24%) under B [BB:14511742 p.44].
- **Thresholds used:** K-5 360; K-8 and middle school 500; high school 1,100 [BB:14505788 p.1] [BB:14511742 p.10].
- **Decision schedule:** the Superintendent's recommendation is due "mid-November" (Board meeting 11/17) and the Board vote is set for the 12/15 Board meeting [BB:14509719 p.1] [BB:14511742 p.45]. Implementation must begin "no later than the 2027-28 school year" and be "completed within a three-year period" [BB:3988127 p.1]. The scenario maps are labeled "School Year 2027-28" [BB:14505796 p.1].
- **What PPS has not published:** no cost or savings estimate, per closure or in total, appears in any rightsizing document in the archive. Staff told the Board that "The number of potential layoffs can not be determined at this time" [BB:14493920 p.7] [BB:14421589 p.4]. Per-school projected 2031–32 enrollments under each scenario are also not in the released documents (see open questions).

## 2. Origin and rationale

### 2.1 Enrollment decline
- PPS repeatedly shows enrollment change of −12% from 2018–19 to 2025–26 and a forecast −12% from 2025–26 to 2035–36 [BB:3997023 p.6] [BB:3988980 p.6] [BB:3997156 p.11]. Some decks drop the minus sign ("12%") [BB:3997004 p.6] [BB:3997148 p.6].
- The proposed budget says that "Since 2019-20, PPS's student enrollment has declined 12.2%, faster than Oregon's (7.4%)" [BB:3997181 p.14].
- The September 1 deck gives "-6086" as the "Enrollment Decrease Since 2018-19". It also gives "-3446" as the "Enrollment Decrease Projected in 2031-32", says sites have changed by "+2 to 83" since 2018-19, and projects "7 Middle Schools < 400 students by 2031-32" [BB:14357211 p.9].
- From the PSU Population Research Center (PRC), as relayed by staff: "In fall 2025, Portland Public Schools (PPS) enrolled 42,622 students in grades K–12, a decrease of 753 students from fall 2024 and sitting just over 6,000 students below the pre-pandemic baseline". Resident capture rose from 74.4% (2019) to an estimated 76.6% (2025), against a high of 82.2% in 2015 [BB:14493920 p.15]. The PRC forecast used for modeling was "updated on July 24, 2026" [BB:14493920 p.28].
- As of 9/11/2026, kindergarten was "running 115 students below the baseline projections" and total K-12 was "about 700 students below last year's official October counts" [BB:14493920 p.32].
- Median enrollment and range as of October 2025 [BB:3997004 p.7]:
  - K-5 (45 schools): median 269; range 560 − 160 = 400.
  - K-8 (11): median 446; range 610 − 207 = 403.
  - Middle (15): median 447; range 689 − 358 = 331.
  - Alternative (3): median 275; range 324 − 217 = 107.
- The August 4 memo says the decline "will continue throughout the next decade, though the rate of decline will slow over time". It adds: "The steep decline that we saw from the pandemic is working its way through the system and will reach the middle grades in the next five years" [BB:3997426 p.2].

### 2.2 Projected status quo (no action), 2031–32
- August 4 memo: "69% of K8 and 47% of middle schools falling 100 students or more below the enrollment for stable programming by 2031-32" [BB:3997426 p.2]. The memo's charts and the August 4 deck show the following [BB:3997426 p.3] [BB:3997425 p.16] [BB:3997425 p.17]:
  - K-5 schools with 360+ students fall from 27% (2025–26) to 18% (2031–32).
  - K-8 schools with 500+ fall from 23% to 15%.
  - 6-8 schools with 500+ fall from 40% to 13%.
  - 9-12 schools with 1,100+ rise from 78% to 100%, as Jefferson grows.
  - By 2031–32, 7 of 15 middle schools are under 400.
- September 1 framing: "1 in 6" K5 schools with 360+, "1 in 7" K8 with 500+ and "1 in 8" 6-8 with 500+ in five years [BB:14357211 p.10] [BB:14374370 p.5].
- **September 1 "Magnitude" charts** (read from slide images) [BB:14357211 p.11] [BB:14357211 p.12]:
  - K-5, 2031–32: with no closures, 8 of 45 schools are at 360+. That rises to 13 of 40 with 5 closures, 22 of 35 with 10, and 30 of 30 with 15.
  - 6-8: with no closures, 2 of 15 schools are at 500+. That rises to 3 of 14 with 1 closure, 4 of 13 with 2, and 8 of 12 with 3.
- **August 4 projection charts** (read from slide images) [BB:3997425 p.16] [BB:3997425 p.17]:
  - K-5, 2031–32: 15 schools under 260, 9 at 260–309, 13 at 310–359, 4 at 360–449 and 4 at 450+.
  - K-8, 2031–32: 7 under 400, 2 at 400–449, 2 at 450–499, 1 at 500–549 and 1 at 550+.
  - Middle, 2031–32: 7 under 400, 4 at 400–449, 2 at 450–499, 1 at 500–549 and 1 at 550+.
  - High school, 2031–32: 4 at 1,100–1,299 and 5 over 1,300.
  - **Analysis:** the K-8 bands do not match the memo's "69% of K8 ... 100 students or more below" (see open questions).
- KPI baseline, percent of schools above threshold, 2025–26 → 2031–32 [BB:14430171 p.14]:
  - K5 Title I: 18% → 9%. K5 non-Title I: 35% → 26%.
  - K8 Title I: 50% → 50%. K8 non-Title I: 18% → 9%.
  - MS Title I: 29% → 0%. MS non-Title I: 50% → 25%.
  - Counts behind these percentages: K-5 Title I 4/22 → 2/22; K-5 non-Title I 8/23 → 6/23; K-8 Title I 1/2 → 1/2; K-8 non-Title I 2/11 → 1/11; MS Title I 2/7 → 0/7; MS non-Title I 4/8 → 2/8 [BB:14493920 p.33].
- Focal students in sustainable K-8 schools, 2025–26 → 2031–32 [BB:14430171 p.15]:
  - Students experiencing poverty: 35.4% → 23%.
  - Historically underserved by race: 35.5% → 24%.
  - Students receiving special education services: 33% → 22%.
  - Multilingual learners: 43.5% → 29%.
- Enrollment range (largest − smallest), 2025–26 → 2031–32 [BB:14430171 p.16]:
  - K5: 560 − 160 = 400 → 557 − 168 = 389.
  - K8: 610 − 207 = 403 → 602 − 177 = 425.
  - MS: 689 − 358 = 331 → 620 − 350 = 270.
  - HS: 2074 − 391 = 1683 → 1492 − 1117 = 375.

### 2.3 Budget pressure
- In October 2025 PPS projected "a $50 million projected budget shortfall for 2026-27" and listed as an assumption "No consolidations or school closures will occur in 2026-27" [BB:3996700 p.8] [BB:3996700 p.9].
- The $50 million 2026–27 gap carried through every 2026 budget deck [BB:3996811 p.8] [BB:3997106 p.6]. A mid-year Q2 gap of $22.5 million was closed with discretionary-spending controls ($10.5M) and furloughs ($12M) [BB:3997106 p.5].
- The 2026–27 proposed budget lists "Declining Enrollment: We have fewer students and the same number of buildings" among the "Financial Pressures Contributing to Our Shortfall" [BB:3997181 p.14]. It also shows a "($65.2M) Ongoing structural savings" reset to a ~$834.8M baseline [BB:3997181 p.50], and projected 2027–28 reductions of $65.2M on top of $171.8M in service-level reductions from 2023–24 to 2026–27 [BB:3997181 p.51].
- The adopted-budget deck notes that "When enrollment declined 13%, the district deliberately held teacher reductions to only 5% ... We have now reached the structural limit of this approach" [BB:3990695 p.1].
- The CBRC urged the Board "to continue implementing long-term strategies—including program or location reductions if necessary—to rebuild reserves" [BB:3997240 p.15].
- The April engagement decks tie rightsizing to the budget: "As the District faces difficult decisions to close the budget gap, it is critical we hear from communities" [BB:3997148 p.5].
- Staff framing on September 15: "For eight years we have been taking resources from our high schools to subsidize our under enrolled elementary, K8 and middle schools" [BB:14430171 p.23].
- Per-pupil cost evidence on October 6, using General Fund non-equity staffing formulas only [BB:14511742 p.6] [BB:14511742 p.7]:
  - Average per student: K-5 $10,037.04; MS $9,280.79; HS $8,609.99; K-12 $9,484.17.
  - Jefferson HS costs $13,638.41 per student against Grant's $8,010.06 under the same formula.
  - A sample of 10 K-5 schools shows "subsidy received" relative to the K-12 average, from Rosa Parks (+$545,049.34) to Richmond (−$637,503.62).

### 2.4 Under-enrolled schools and program equity
- The September 1 rationale: "Our enrollment is declining and will continue to decline"; "We have maintained the same number of school sites"; "Staff and supports spread both unevenly and thin". The stated goal is to "increase stability and improve student learning experience across the system and in every school, especially for historically underserved students" [BB:14357211 p.8] [BB:14374370 p.3].
- The August 4 memo: "Maintaining more than 80 school sites amid declining enrollment has spread resources increasingly thin. Schools experience this through blended classrooms, an increase in itinerant staff, reduction in school-wide supports such as counselors or social workers, or courses being offered in alternate years" [BB:3997426 p.2].
- Staff reported that 125 of 717 originally allocated K-5 FTE went to blended classrooms [BB:14493920 p.35].
- The September 8 TLE "elementary story" describes three 200–350-student schools within five miles, with "Arts educators split across schools", "Blended grade bands" and a "Principal on duty 2-3 hours a day". The hypothetical future has 360–500-student campuses with single-grade classrooms [BB:14374370 p.9] [BB:14374370 p.10].
- In the middle-grades story, grades 6-8 are served on 28 school sites, ranging from a "~700 LARGEST SITE" to a "~7 SMALLEST SITE" [BB:14374370 p.11]. The paths named are "Program shifts", "Grade-band reconfiguration" and "School consolidation" [BB:14374370 p.12].
- 2025–26 utilization (enrollment / functional capacity) ranges from 25% (Jefferson HS, 391/1590) and 32% (Lent, 256/798) to 107–110% at Franklin, Grant, Odyssey and ACCESS [BB:3997157 p.1]. In total the 82 listed campuses enroll 40,020 students against 58,550 functional capacity [BB:3997157 p.1]. **Analysis:** that is about 68% overall, by my sum of the table.
- PPS defines "Efficient Utilization as a school operating between 70% and 100% of its Functional Capacity". Base K-5 and K-8 rates are reduced for Title I, TSI/CSI, neighborhood K-8 and co-located neighborhood plus immersion programs [BB:14493920 p.26].

- **DLI and programming.** In October 2025 the Superintendent said that "part of the right sizing process is looking at programming", in the context of Dual Language programs [BB:3989403 p.3].
- **Early timeline.** The November 13, 2025 TLE timeline put "Study + Recommend" in the "2026-27 School Year" [BB:3996734 p.20]. This was later pulled forward to a December 2026 vote (§3.1).
- **Jefferson memo.** The December 2025 Jefferson memo: "Review of dual language immersion feeder pathways and other enrollment reviews will be incorporated into subsequent rightsizing processes which will begin in the Spring of 2026" [BB:3988475 p.6].
- **Board discussion, March 10, 2026.** Minutes record that "Some Board members expressed interest in considering the elimination of K-8 models and focus-option schools, as well as a desire to overlay these decisions with facilities improvements and transportation efficiencies" [MTG:2026-03-10/734436] [BB:3990212 p.4].

### 3.2 Resolution 7296 (April 14, 2026): launch
- **Original text.** Recital C: "PPS enrollment has declined steadily for the past five years and is forecast to continue to decline for at least the next five years" [BB:3988127 p.1]. The original:
  - included "All district schools and programs";
  - provided for an "enrollment advisory committee of stakeholders including community members, families, and staff";
  - required implementation "initiated no later than the 2027-28 school year and ... completed within a three-year period";
  - stated in Recital E: "The result of this process will include school and program closures and/or consolidations" [BB:3988127 p.1].
- **Resolved clause.** It directs the Superintendent "to consider school and program consolidations, boundary adjustment, special program changes, and grade reconfigurations" [BB:3988127 p.1].
- **REVISED version.** This version struck the "enrollment" advisory committee and substituted "union representatives, principals, and staff" (shown as tracked text) [BB:3990244 p.1].
- **Director La Forte's proposed amendment.** It added six conditions [BB:3990653 p.1] [BB:3990653 p.2]:
  - (A) Board approval of "clear decision-making criteria prior to developing proposed scenarios";
  - (B) a "standardized equity impact analysis" for each scenario;
  - (C) a published multi-phase engagement plan;
  - (D) a detailed timeline;
  - (E) "Financial Transparency: all proposals shall include clearly stated assumptions, one-time implementation costs, and ongoing financial impacts, and shall be made public prior to Board action";
  - (F) policy alignment.
- **Minutes record these votes** [BB:3997322 p.3] [BB:3997322 p.4]:
  - Engelsman motion to restore recital D(b) (the advisory committee) into the amendment: passed 6-0-1.
  - Adding "boundary changes" after "program closures" in Recital E: passed 6-0-1.
  - Changing "will" to "may" in Recital E: **failed 2-5-1**. Engelsman and Sullivan voted yes.
  - La Forte motion "to adopt the changes to the proposed amendment": recorded as "passed (4-yes, 3-no, 0-abstain)". The printed roll call, however, shows only three yes votes (Engelsman, La Forte, Sullivan) and four no votes (Chase-Miller, DePass, Splitt, Wang). **Analysis:** that roll call would mean the motion failed 3-4, so the minutes contradict themselves.
  - Splitt motion to make Recital E read "The result of this process will include school and program closures, consolidations, or boundary changes": passed 6-0-1.
  - Main motion "as amended": **passed 5-2**. Yes: Chase-Miller, DePass, Splitt, Sullivan, Wang. No: Engelsman, La Forte. Student representative Ritorto: no (unofficial).
- Two other passages suggest the amendment failed:
  - During discussion, Chair Wang "asked if the proposed amendment could be moved to be included in the next resolution they adopt on the guiding principles" [BB:3997322 p.3].
  - The future-agenda list includes "consideration of the proposed amendment that did not pass" [BB:3997322 p.6].
- The October 6 deck quotes the 7296 resolve clause without any "subject to the following" conditions [BB:14511742 p.4].
- The final adopted text of 7296 is not in the archive. **Analysis:** conditions A–F were probably not adopted. Condition B (equity impact analysis) reappears in 7307. See open questions.

### 3.5 Lessons-learned inputs
- **SEGC equity audit** (Green & Hanson, June 10, 2026), presented August 4. Key findings as summarized by staff [BB:3997426 p.1] [BB:3997426 p.2]:
  - "improved enrollment balance across the schools against the backdrop of declining enrollment";
  - outcomes similar to peers, but "this did not match how families experienced the changes, especially in middle school";
  - DLI coherence gained at the cost of neighborhood access and racial composition;
  - "major staffing disruptions especially for staff of color";
  - "The gap between stated intentions and actions eroded trust".
- **Audit recommendations adopted as workstreams:** clearer criteria, transparent data, early engagement, "Receiving School Readiness Plans", "Durable Transition Supports" and "Track Cumulative Burden on Communities" [BB:3997425 p.12] [BB:3997425 p.14].
- **The audit's enrollment numbers:**
  - SEGC enrollment projections "overestimated enrollment at 12 of 20 schools" [BB:3997427 p.6].
  - Elementary SEGC enrollment fell 20% from 2020–21 to 2024–25, against 14% outside SEGC [BB:3997427 p.6].
  - The audit suggested planning floors of about 450 (elementary), 600 (middle) and 650 (K-8), noting these "should not be used as automatic closure thresholds" [BB:3997427 p.145] [BB:3997427 p.146]. PPS adopted 360/500/500 instead (see criteria file).
- **The audit on savings:** closure savings "depend on whether the district can actually reduce ongoing costs". It calls for "a transparent estimate of realistic net savings" [BB:3997427 p.110] [BB:3997427 p.111].
- **Peer districts and reference documents:** San Antonio ISD, Seattle (recommendation "Withdrew") and Denver (Board Executive Limitation 18) [BB:3988980 p.12] [BB:3988980 p.13] [BB:3988980 p.14]. The Denver DSTI annual report [BB:3990455 p.3] and the Oregon PTA School Consolidation Advisory Guidance (approved October 18, 2025) [BB:3991313 p.1] were attached on March 31.
- **PPS closure history** (September 8 TLE) [BB:14374370 p.4]:

## 4. The scenarios (released October 5–6, 2026)

### 4.1 Modeling approach
- **Guiding question:** "How can we increase the number of school sites that operate in a sustainable enrollment range ... while also using our buildings efficiently?" [BB:14511742 p.10].
- **Criteria applied** [BB:14511742 p.11]:
  - Enrollment Trends & Capacity, through 2031-32;
  - Facility & Capital Viability;
  - Programmatic & Grade-Band Optimization (K-8 to K-5/6-8 conversions, DLI pathways, feeder alignment);
  - Student & Operational Guardrails: "Capped receiving schools at no more than two per closure. Ensured students transition to higher-quality facilities."
- **Weighing:** "Criteria were weighed against one another rather than applied in a fixed ranking, with the balance differing by regional conditions" [BB:14509719 p.1].
- **Regional drivers** [BB:14509719 p.2]:
  - Roosevelt/Jefferson: DLI consolidation and middle school enrollment.
  - Grant/McDaniel and Cleveland/Franklin: K-8 reconfiguration, language pathways, facility considerations and split feeders.
  - Lincoln/Wells: geographic isolation, travel distances and elementary enrollment ("immersion programs were already consolidated").
- **Unchanged in both scenarios:** kindergarten DLI seats; "All 5 languages sustained in Dual Language Immersion (DLI) Programs"; programs with entrance requirements (DART, Pioneer, Alliance); ACCESS; and the Benson and da Vinci focus options [BB:14511742 p.13].
- **Starting point:** 80 schools plus 2 programs (45 K5, 13 K8, 15 MS, 9 HS) [BB:14511742 p.19].

### 4.5 Equity impact analysis
- 7307 requires a "standardized equity impact analysis" for each scenario. It must cover focal students, access to specialized programs, impacts on recently impacted and Title I communities, and how community input shaped the result [BB:3997273 p.2].

- The October release reports only the share of focal groups in sustainable schools and the share of K-8 students who move, by group [BB:14505788 p.1] [BB:14511742 p.44]. No per-scenario narrative Equity Impact Statement is among the released documents.

  - **MLC 9–12:** projected savings of $1.1M (February) [BB:3996961 p.13]. This is the only closure-related savings figure in the archive.

The written response, numbered "2.", answers only the layoffs part [BB:14421589 p.4] [BB:14493920 p.7].
