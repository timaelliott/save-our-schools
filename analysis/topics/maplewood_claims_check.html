Fact check · data as of Oct 1, 2026

Four claims about Maplewood, checked against PPS’s own data

The claim as writtenSo across PPS Maplewood has among the highest growth / GPR, biggest forecast error from PSU, closing it will create two of the most overcrowded schools, and closing it has the least impact on equity measures.

What the data supportsSince 2021, Maplewood’s classes have grown grade to grade faster than any other comparable PPS elementary school. PSU under-forecast it this fall and still projects it to shrink 14% by 2031-32. Closing it sends its students to Rieke and Hayhurst, which PPS projects at 93% and 90% full, and Hayhurst’s real figure is closer to 97%. Its students are among the least likely to be low-income of any school on the closure list, so closing it does little for PPS’s equity goals.

“Maplewood has among the highest growth and GPR”

Holds

Maplewood has the highest grade progression ratio of 38 comparable PPS elementary schools since 2021 (1.040), and the 9th-highest K-5 growth (+4.5%, 291 → 304).

“Maplewood has the biggest forecast error from PSU”

Overstated

Not the biggest. PSU under-forecast Maplewood this fall by 16 students (5.3%), the 4th-largest miss of 38. PSU still projects it to shrink 14% by 2031-32, the 3rd-steepest decline of 31 K-5 schools, while it has the highest GPR.

“Closing it will create two of the most overcrowded schools”

Not by PPS’s numbers

Using PPS’s own capacities, Scenario A puts Rieke at 93% (12th of 52) and Hayhurst at 90% (14th). Full, but not the fullest. Corrected for the special-ed rooms Hayhurst actually uses, Hayhurst is about 97%.

“Closing it has the least impact on equity measures”

Holds, with a caveat

Maplewood has the 2nd-lowest poverty rate of the 13 elementary schools PPS would close in Scenario A (19.5%) and the lowest of 10 in Scenario B. It is not Title I. The caveat: the Southwest package moves Hayhurst’s poverty rate from 26.2% to 15.5% and Rieke’s from 15.2% to 24.0%.

1. Growth and grade progression since 2021

Data. PPS “Enrollment by Grade and Program Type,” October counts for 2021-22 through 2026-27 (2021-22 · 2022-23 · 2023-24 · 2024-25 · 2025-26 · 2026-27). All programs at a school are combined (for example, Ainsworth’s Spanish Immersion and neighborhood program). For K-8 schools only grades K-5 are used.

Which schools. 38 neighborhood elementary and K-8 schools. Left out:

  • Boundary or grade changes since 2021: the Southeast Guiding Coalition changes effective 2023-24 and the 2021-22 K-5 reconfigurations (Arleta, Atkinson, Bridger, Bridger Creative Science, Clark, Creston, Glencoe, Lent, Lewis, Marysville, Vestal, Whitman, Woodmere, Woodstock), per the footnotes to PPS’s 10-year enrollment history.
  • Focus, immersion-only, charter and special programs: Odyssey, Sunnyside Environmental, Winterhaven, Richmond, Rigler, MLC, charters, and special services.

Grade progression ratio (GPR). For each school, the number of students in grades 1-5 one October divided by the number in grades K-4 the October before, pooled over the five year-to-year steps from 2021→22 through 2025→26. Above 1.000 means classes grow as they move up; below 1.000 means they shrink. This is the same quantity PSU uses to roll each class forward.

In Scenario B, Rieke is 8th (93%) and Hayhurst 10th (90%) of 54. Maplewood’s K-2 Intensive Skills class goes to Markham, 99% full at peak in Scenario A (6th) and rated “Not Yet Ready / Significant Barriers” on PPS’s Nov 9 readiness profile FIO deck p.21.

Why Hayhurst’s real figure is higher. PPS’s 525 seats assume 2 special-ed rooms and 18 homerooms. Hayhurst’s own Nov 9 profile lists 7 special-education classrooms, and its special-ed box lists 4 rooms FIO deck p.19. Director Splitt said the SCES program “takes up four classrooms” Nov 4, 4:01:52. With those rooms counted, capacity is 486-489, and a peak of 472 is about 97%. In 2015-16, PPS’s own profile flagged this same 22-classroom building as having “little or no room left for growth” at 23 students per classroom; 472 students is 21.5 per classroom 2015-16 profile p.212.

The caveat. The Southwest package does change equity measures at the receiving schools, mostly because part of Hayhurst’s boundary moves to Rieke:

So “least impact” is fair for Maplewood’s own students; it is not true of the package as a whole. A better wording: closing Maplewood does the least for PPS’s equity goals.
