# City BPS memo on the 14 possible closure sites (Oct 30, 2026): notes

**Source.** The City of Portland Bureau of Planning and Sustainability (BPS) wrote a memo, "Potential School Closure Sites Analysis" (preliminary draft, 35 pages, dated Oct 30, 2026). KATU obtained it and published it with its Nov 4 story [NEWS:NEWS-20261104-katu-2](https://katu.com/news/local/portland-study-reviews-future-uses-for-up-to-14-pps-schools-under-closure-talks-housing-development-business-community-vacant-local-students-teachers-parents-neighbors). It is archived at `sources/city/bps/`.

This is a City analysis, not a PPS document. It covers the 14 sites in Scenarios A and B, including Maplewood, Stephenson and Sellwood MS. All three are now off the recommendation list.

## What it says

**Purpose and scope.** For each site the memo describes:
- zoning;
- the area within half a mile;
- transit, bike and walking access;
- the demographics of the catchment area;
- reuse options [CITY:bps/2026-10-30_bps_potential_school_closure_sites_analysis.pdf p.1].

**No sale recommendation.** It makes no recommendation to sell. It says keeping closed sites "is a prudent hedge against future needs" because demographics may shift [CITY:bps/2026-10-30_bps_potential_school_closure_sites_analysis.pdf p.4].

**Households with children.** The share of catchment-area households with children is highest at Stephenson (34%) and Lewis (32%), against 22% citywide. Maplewood is at 24%, and Buckman is lowest at 6% [CITY:bps/2026-10-30_bps_potential_school_closure_sites_analysis.pdf p.3].

**Walking access.** Metro analyzed missing sidewalks within a 1-mile walkshed of each school. Maplewood (88%) and Stephenson (74%) have the highest shares, followed by Woodmere (37%) and Lewis (33%). Irvington (2%) and Sabin (5%) have the lowest [CITY:bps/2026-10-30_bps_potential_school_closure_sites_analysis.pdf p.7].

**Transit.** Access is "more limited" at Maplewood and Stephenson than at the other sites [CITY:bps/2026-10-30_bps_potential_school_closure_sites_analysis.pdf p.7].

**Maplewood site** [CITY:bps/2026-10-30_bps_potential_school_closure_sites_analysis.pdf p.20] [CITY:bps/2026-10-30_bps_potential_school_closure_sites_analysis.pdf p.21]:
- 4.30 acres, built 1949, zoned R7;
- transit is more than a quarter mile away;
- the catchment has 3,214 residents under 18;
- mid-density rezoning is "not recommended" because of limited transit and possible stormwater issues.

**Stephenson site** [CITY:bps/2026-10-30_bps_potential_school_closure_sites_analysis.pdf p.32] [CITY:bps/2026-10-30_bps_potential_school_closure_sites_analysis.pdf p.33]:
- 8.80 acres, the largest of the 14 sites;
- zoned R10;
- a "poor candidate for stand-alone development."

**Analysis.** The memo supports two Southwest arguments made during engagement:
- walking routes there lack sidewalks, so longer trips after a closure would mostly be made by car;
- these catchments have relatively many households with children.

The figures describe the catchment areas, not PPS enrollment. The memo itself says enrollment is "a fraction of this data" [CITY:bps/2026-10-30_bps_potential_school_closure_sites_analysis.pdf p.3].
