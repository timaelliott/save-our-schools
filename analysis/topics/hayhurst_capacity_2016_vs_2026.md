# Hayhurst capacity: what PPS counted in 2015-16 vs. the 2026 model

Compiled 2026-11-07. Sources archived from the Internet Archive in `sources/pps_net/boundaries_2016/wayback_2017/` (pps.net originals now return 404).

## Data pack search
- No D-BRAC "data pack" for Hayhurst exists in the Internet Archive. PPS posted 14 packs in Domain/182 (Abernethy, Alameda, Astor, Beverly Cleary, Bridger, Chapman, CSS, East-West Sylvan, Harrison Park, Kelly, Laurelhurst, Scott, Sitton, Skyline), all captured June 2017. Hayhurst is not among them (CDX listing of `pps.net/cms/lib/OR01913224/Centricity/Domain/182/*`, run 2026-11-07).

## What PPS's own 2015-16 School Profile says
- Hayhurst (K-8, including Odyssey), October 2015 enrollment **499**, **Number of Classrooms 22**, **Density Index 23** [PPS:pps_net/boundaries_2016/wayback_2017/2015-16_School_Profiles.pdf p.212](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/boundaries_2016/wayback_2017/2015-16_School_Profiles.pdf).
- Enrollment indicator: "Building density index below 15 or above 20? Yes" [same page].
- Definitions [PPS:pps_net/boundaries_2016/wayback_2017/2015-16_School_Profiles.pdf p.318](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/boundaries_2016/wayback_2017/2015-16_School_Profiles.pdf):
  - Number of Classrooms: "Actual amount of “potential” classroom space in a school including classrooms housing standard education programs, other District programs, outside programs/partnerships, Special Education classes, English Language Learner classes, and vacant rooms."
  - Building Density Index: enrollment ÷ classrooms; "a building density index greater than 20 means there is little or no room left for growth in that facility".
- 2015-16 grades in the building: K 86, 1 73, 2 76, 3 69, 4 74, 5 50, 6-8 23/24/24 (Hayhurst neighborhood program plus Odyssey) [same profile p.211].

## The 2026 model
- Same 22 classrooms (20 at 27 + 2 science rooms at 30) plus the gym; functional capacity 525 [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/utilization_model/static_v1_public_utilization_model_2026-11-02.xlsx#DISTRICT UTILIZATION v1!R40]. See `analysis/meetings/2026-11-09_fio_materials.md` finding 1 for the formula issues (516 by its own rules).

## Analysis
- The room count did not change between 2015 and 2026 (22 both times), consistent with no additions.
- 525 ÷ 22 = 23.9 students per classroom: the 2026 model calls the building full only above the density PPS itself flagged in 2016 as "little or no room left for growth" (above 20).
- The projected 2027 peak, 469 ÷ 22 = 21.3, is above PPS's own 2016 threshold of 20. At 20 per classroom, 22 rooms ≈ 440 students.
- The 2016 overcrowding percentage (499 = 101.8%) used a separate "preferred enrollment target" analysis. PPS's 2014 planning targets were K-5 = 450, K-8 = 500 students [PPS:pps_net/boundaries_2016/wayback_2017/Neighborhood_population_and_school_targets.v2.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/boundaries_2016/wayback_2017/Neighborhood_population_and_school_targets.v2.pdf) [PPS:pps_net/boundaries_2016/wayback_2017/Neighborhood_population_and_school_targets.v2.pdf p.2](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/boundaries_2016/wayback_2017/Neighborhood_population_and_school_targets.v2.pdf); a 2016-17 KPI sheet describes preferred K-5 ranges as "3 sections per grade in K5 ... 20-30 students per section" [PPS:pps_net/boundaries_2016/wayback_2017/Current_State_KPIs.pdf p.1](https://github.com/timaelliott/save-our-schools/blob/main/sources/pps_net/boundaries_2016/wayback_2017/Current_State_KPIs.pdf).
