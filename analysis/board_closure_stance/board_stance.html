Turning points

  • Mar 10, 2026. The superintendent floats "five to 10 schools". Every director backs a districtwide, all-at-once process.
  • Apr 14. Res 7296 passes 5–2, with La Forte and Engelsman voting no. Their guardrail amendment fails 3–4.
  • May 26. Guiding principles are adopted 7–0. This is the high point of consensus.
  • Sep 1. Staff modeling shows roughly 15–20 closures. Splitt flips to "scalpel", Engelsman hardens, and DePass and Wang push bigger.
  • Oct 6. The scenarios are released (A closes 14, B closes 11). DePass and Sullivan say "do something"; La Forte, Splitt and Engelsman object to specific closures.
  • Oct 20. Splitt offers her own six-building plan; Chase-Miller makes the strongest case for acting now; Engelsman says the scenarios fail board policy.
  • Nov 10. At TLE, La Forte rejects the "too many schools" premise ("definitely not 14 and it's definitely not 11"); Splitt asks what the harm would be in taking a year on DLI.
  • Nov 13. The recommendation is posted: 10 elementary closures over two years; Maplewood, Stephenson, James John and Sellwood MS come off the list. The same day the Signal vote splits 4–3, with Sullivan joining the skeptics.
  • Nov 17. Engelsman: she would rather the plan "unravels" than pass as is. La Forte: amendments "so that I can get to a yes". Wang argues for an "up and down vote". The amendment process becomes the main fight.

Director by director

Shared weak points

  1. The savings figure is modest. The recommendation estimates about $10.2M a year in net recurring savings against a $65M gap, of which the superintendent attributes $44M to PERS. That fits "resources, not the deficit", but undercuts fiscal-emergency framing, and gives the skeptics who demanded the number no clear reason to move.
  2. "Well-resourced" is still not guaranteed. Staff said "we're not going to necessarily see more" resources, only "some sustainability of program". Schools may still face about $42M in reductions.
  3. Equity tests cut in different directions. Three of the first-year closures are led by principals of color (DePass). La Forte says Spanish-speaking families do not want the hubs; the Oregonian finds Lent's Latino students improved after its hub conversion.
  4. Amendments vs "cascading effects". Supporters warn that amendments cascade; skeptics say a package that cannot be amended is not a choice. Both sides treat the Nov 30 to Dec 15 window as too short.
  5. Bond money at closing schools. Woodmere's bond-funded roof work is still on a closing school.
  6. Home-area focus. Splitt (Southwest), La Forte (North and Northeast) and Engelsman (Abernethy, Hosford) draw most examples from their own areas. The recommendation removed all West-side changes.

Watch before Dec 15

  • Nov 23. La Forte said her amendments could be ready.
  • Nov 30. Amendment deadline: DLI timing and focus options (Splitt); Lent, the hubs and transport exceptions (La Forte); Woodmere, Buckman, special-education cohorts, packing dates (Engelsman); North Portland and Forest Park (Sullivan).
  • Dec 1. Board discussion; Splitt states her view. Does Wang still seek a board vote before staff analyze amendments?
  • Dec 2. TLE as an "amendment workshop". Dec 3. Grant HS listening session. Dec 8. Possible extra meeting.
  • Dec 11. Independent Signal report. A finding against leadership would harden La Forte and Engelsman and could move Sullivan.
  • Dec 15. Vote. Amendments first; a deferral motion would most likely come from Engelsman.
