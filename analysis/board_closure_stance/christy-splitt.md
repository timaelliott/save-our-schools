**What matters most to her now**
1. Will it fit: floor plans, receiving-school capacity, class sizes at Title I schools.
2. Focus options and DLI: lottery caps, who DLI serves, and a slower hub transition.
3. Fewer, phased changes (her own six-building plan).
4. State funding and transparency.

**Weak points (Analysis)**
- *The gap between her plan and the recommendation is five schools.* Her "significantly smaller" stance now means naming which of Beach, Sabin, Lewis, Marysville and Rose City Park should stay open. An earlier version of her plan merged Sabin into King [NEWS:NEWS-20261027-opb-1](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/27/oregon-portland-public-schools-salem-keizer/), so Sabin is a hard case for her to oppose.
- *DLI delay conflicts with "changes are necessary now".* A one-year DLI pause would also hold back the César Chávez, Rigler and Lent hubs that the North Portland moves depend on [BB:14695483 p.24](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14695483?org=915#page=24).
- *Home-area exposure is gone.* The recommendation makes no West-side changes [BB:14695483 p.3](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14695483?org=915#page=3). That removes her "surreal" home-school concern ("I'm trying really hard not to bring up the school I know best" [YT:V6r7UqxJ53s@2:10:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V6r7UqxJ53s&t=7803s)), and it also removes a reason she had to vote no.
- *"Over-serving affluent white families" is a hypothesis.* She asked staff whether the 1/3, 1/3, 1/3 model is holding [YT:hbSByH9nzfA@2:44:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hbSByH9nzfA&t=9864s). The archive has no answer yet.

**Contradictions (new since October)**
1. *"Necessary now" vs "taking a year" on DLI.* The Oct 20 memo [NEWS:NEWS-20261020-oregonian-1](https://www.oregonlive.com/education/2026/10/2-portland-public-schools-board-members-pitch-alternatives-to-closing-14-schools.html) and the Nov 10 question [YT:V6r7UqxJ53s@2:51:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V6r7UqxJ53s&t=10286s) are scoped differently (whole plan vs DLI only), but they pull in opposite directions.

**Read for Dec 15 (Analysis):** about even (50%). The recommendation moved toward her plan; her remaining objections are to DLI timing, focus options and a few schools. If the board takes up her focus-options resolution and at least one school change, a yes is plausible.

## Bottom line (October 2026)

**Current (Oct 2026) score: −1 (range −2 to +1).** She says some closures are needed, but she has called the A/B packages "just too much" (14 or 11 closures; 24–27% of K–8 students move). She is most likely to vote yes on a package cut toward Scenario B or below that changes the West-side plan, and most likely to vote no on Scenario A as presented.

## Weak points

1. **The "scalpel" cannot meet the 360 threshold she accepted.**
   - What staff showed on 9/1, for the 45 K-5s:
     - Status quo: eight schools at or above 360 by 2031-32.
     - Five closures: 13.
     - Ten closures: just over half.
     - Fifteen closures: all 30 remaining [YT:5RMfvT-sAPU@1:33:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5RMfvT-sAPU&t=5625s) [YT:5RMfvT-sAPU@1:34:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5RMfvT-sAPU&t=5662s).
   - Analysis: her "5 to 10" baseline would leave roughly half or more of K-5s below a threshold she said the board had "landed". Unless she argues for a lower threshold or a lower share of students in sustainable schools, her scale objection has no stated target.
   - Staff say they modeled 50%, 60% and 70% sustainability targets (meeting summary, `analysis/meetings/2026-10-06_zOUH2Jq7Owo.md`). Analysis: a smaller target is the concrete form a "scalpel" would take, and she has not named one.
   - Her "24 to 27% of our students" is the share of **K–8** students who move under A or B [BB:14511742 p.44](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14511742?org=915#page=44). Analysis: the slide appears to count every K–8 move, including the boundary changes and K-8 reconfigurations she has supported in principle. It is not only students at closing schools.

2. **Keeping kids together pulls against overcrowding and walkability.**
   - The scenarios cap receivers at two per closure [BB:14511742 p.11](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14511742?org=915#page=11), which she credited on 10/6. Keeping cohorts whole means large receiving schools.
   - In the West under Scenario A:
     - Markham is projected at 519–554 students [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/markham.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1Qx13JVd92AZdJotx-gADCdBr0LyczHyT&usp=drive_copy), against a 2025-26 functional capacity of 510 [BB:3997157 p.1](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/3997157?org=915#page=1).
     - Capitol Hill is projected at 370–387 [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/capitol_hill.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1YNJRSQEfCCJRjQIuUCmIP5YTmrdugLzq&usp=drive_copy), against 375 [BB:3997157 p.1](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/3997157?org=915#page=1).
   - Hayhurst would hold 472 in 2027-28 [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/hayhurst.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1BwsMTqcVksEx0je_XtPcti9pgFLBE_TP&usp=drive_copy). The guide says the building has "room for over 500 students" [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/hayhurst.pdf p.3](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1BwsMTqcVksEx0je_XtPcti9pgFLBE_TP&usp=drive_copy). The share of students living within 1 mile falls from 30.5% to 22.6% [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/hayhurst.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1BwsMTqcVksEx0je_XtPcti9pgFLBE_TP&usp=drive_copy).
   - Analysis: her capacity worry has real support in Scenario A. Her own cohort principle helps create it.
   - Analysis on housing: at her 50–70-student estimate, Hayhurst would be near or slightly over capacity in the first years. Because the projection declines to 410 by 2031-32 [PPS:pps_net/rightsizing/school_impact/hayhurst.pdf p.4](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1BwsMTqcVksEx0je_XtPcti9pgFLBE_TP&usp=drive_copy), the problem is more likely tight than structural. (Capacity measures differ between documents.)
