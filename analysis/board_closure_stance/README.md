# Board closure stance: where each member stands heading into the Dec 15, 2026 vote

One file per voting board member, with a dated timeline, contradictions and shifts, fixations, weak points,
what would move them, and watch items. Each member also has a `<slug>_scores.csv`; `all_scores.csv` combines them (333 scored points through Nov 17, 2026).
Method: `_instructions.md`. Every quote was checked against transcripts; medium-confidence attributions are marked in each file.

**Lean score:** if the vote were held that day, how likely is the member to vote to ADOPT a closure package
(Scenario A, B, or an amended version that still closes schools)? The scale runs from +2 (strongly for) to −2 (strongly
against, defer, or no closures). The scores are analytical judgments (Analysis), not predictions of record.

## Shared weak points (details and citations in each member file)

1. **The savings figure is now known, and it is modest.** The recommendation estimates about $10.2M in net recurring savings [BB:14695483 p.3](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14695483?org=915#page=3) against a $65M gap, of which Armstrong attributes $44M to PERS [YT:9kX3vnfRlvY@5:35:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kX3vnfRlvY&t=20144s). That fits the supporters' line that closures are about resources, not the deficit, but it undercuts any fiscal-emergency framing (Wang's "really dire") and gives skeptics who demanded the number (La Forte, Engelsman) no clear reason to move.
2. **"Well-resourced" is still not guaranteed.** Staff said on Nov 4 "we're not going to necessarily see more" resources, only "some sustainability of program" [YT:9kX3vnfRlvY@4:40:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kX3vnfRlvY&t=16821s). On Nov 17 Chase-Miller asked whether a further $42M in school reductions means larger classes or fewer specials [YT:hbSByH9nzfA@1:53:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hbSByH9nzfA&t=6817s).
3. **The equity tests still cut in different directions.** DePass notes that three of the first-year closures are led by principals of color [YT:hbSByH9nzfA@3:40:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hbSByH9nzfA&t=13256s). La Forte says Spanish-speaking families do not want the hubs [YT:V6r7UqxJ53s@3:10:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V6r7UqxJ53s&t=11413s), while the Oregonian finds Lent's Latino students improved after the hub conversion [NEWS:NEWS-20261031-oregonian-1](https://www.oregonlive.com/education/2026/10/plan-to-reshape-spanish-immersion-emerges-as-a-flashpoint-for-portland-school-closures.html).
4. **Amendments vs "cascading effects".** Supporters (Wang, DePass, Chase-Miller) warn that amendments cascade; skeptics (Engelsman, La Forte, Splitt) say a package that cannot be amended is not a choice. Both sides now treat the Nov 30 to Dec 15 window as too short.
5. **Bond and seismic money at closure schools.** Woodmere's bond-funded roof work is still on a closing school (Engelsman, Nov 9 [YT:cyHU9UOQqvc@0:59:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cyHU9UOQqvc&t=3544s)).
6. **Home-area focus.** Splitt (Southwest), La Forte (North and Northeast) and Engelsman (Abernethy, Hosford) still draw most examples from their own areas. The recommendation removed all West-side changes, which takes Splitt's home-area objections off the table.
