# Board closure stance: where each member stands heading into the Dec 15, 2026 vote

One file per voting board member, with a dated timeline, contradictions and shifts, fixations, weak points,
what would move them, and watch items. Each member also has a `<slug>_scores.csv`; `all_scores.csv` combines them (289 scored points).
Method: `_instructions.md`. Every quote was checked against transcripts; medium-confidence attributions are marked in each file.

**Lean score:** if the vote were held that day, how likely is the member to vote to ADOPT a closure package
(Scenario A, B, or an amended version that still closes schools)? The scale runs from +2 (strongly for) to −2 (strongly
against, defer, or no closures). The scores are analytical judgments (Analysis), not predictions of record.

**Analysis, likely tally:** **4 to 5 votes to adopt some closure package** (Wang, DePass, Sullivan, Chase-Miller; La Forte if

contest is **scale and amendments**, not whether to close.

## Shared weak points (details and citations in each member file)

1. **No savings estimate yet.** Every member who frames closures as budget help (Wang, Sullivan's reserve logic) or demands cost
   data first (La Forte, Engelsman) is exposed to the Nov 17 fiscal impact statement. Staff have signaled savings won't be a "huge dollar amount".
2. **The equity tests members cite cut in different directions.** Six Title I schools close in both scenarios. By Chase-Miller's own share test, A is less
   concentrated on Title I schools than B. DePass's "don't re-harm recently disrupted communities" conflicts with closing Marysville and Creston.
   Res 7307 requires a per-scenario equity impact analysis, and none has been released.
3. **"Well-resourced" guarantees aren't on the table.** Wang, Chase-Miller and Sullivan condition support on staffing (librarians, arts, class size).
   Staff have said they "can't commit to the same amount of resources", and class-size caps are "not a part of the rightsizing conversation" [BB:14493920 p.33](https://meetings.boardbook.org/Documents/DownloadPDF/14493920?org=915#page=33).
4. **Thresholds vs. "scalpel".** Splitt and La Forte accept the 360 / 500 / 1,100 floors but resist the closure counts those floors imply.
   Staff modeling shows 5–10 closures leave about half of K-5 schools below 360.
5. **Bond and seismic money at closure schools.** Several members (Wang 9/9/25, DePass, Chase-Miller, La Forte) said seismic money should go only to
   schools safe from consolidation. Yet about $18M of completed seismic work is at closure-list schools (Marysville, Woodmere), and Rose City Park closes under A.
6. **Home-zone exceptions.** Splitt (Southwest: Maplewood and Stephenson) and La Forte (Peninsula, Sabin and Irvington, near where she lives and lived) both warned
   against cherry-picking, then objected to closures in their own areas.
