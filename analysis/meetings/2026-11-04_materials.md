## Rightsizing deck: what it says

The deck is a process update. It contains **no new scenario, no changed school list and no new enrollment numbers**.
Its stated purpose is to "Review the model for developing the recommendation" and share "how it's shaping our
decisions" [BB:14615901 p.2].

**Timeline (confirmed):**
- "We are here: exploring the two scenarios one component at a time, so the full picture is in place before the
  November recommendation" [BB:14615901 p.4].
- Nov 9: Facilities Improvement & Oversight (FIO) committee: "school readiness assessment process" and school-specific
  findings on facilities, meal service, accessibility, security and transportation. Nov 10: TLE committee on special
  education and DLI [BB:14615901 p.19].
- **Nov 17 Board meeting: Superintendent's recommendation.** Dec 1 Board meeting and Dec 2 TLE: review the
  recommendation. **Dec 15 Board meeting: rightsizing vote** [BB:14615901 p.21].
- More engagements: Village Table Nov 5 and Dec 2; Board "Community Connection" sessions Nov 19 and Dec 3
  [BB:14615901 p.20].

**Framework (unchanged):** four guiding principles (well-resourced schools; safe and healthy learning environments;
educational options "maintaining a strong network of neighborhood schools"; the PPS Equity Lens) [BB:14615901 p.5] and
KPIs in three groups: enrollment thresholds, focal students and enrollment balance; utilization, proximity (share of
students within 1.0 and 1.5 miles), split feeders and high school pathways; and scenario impacts on focal students,
Title I schools and student mobility [BB:14615901 p.6].

**Engagement so far:** "over 1,000 community members across 14 Engagement sessions" and "over 3,200 responses" to the
scenario surveys as of Oct 28 [BB:14615901 p.9]. Impacted school sites were the largest engagement group (653). Survey
respondents: 94% "Impacted community members", 74% PPS parents/guardians, 64% White [BB:14615901 p.9].

**Survey: ranking the guiding principles** (share ranking each one 1st, highest priority) [BB:14615901 p.12]:

| Principle | Ranked 1st | Ranked 4th |
|---|---|---|
| Strong Neighborhood Schools | **41%** | 20% |
| Well-Resourced Sustainable Schools | 22% | 21% |
| Safe & Healthy Learning Environments | 22% | 10% |
| PPS Equity Lens | 17% | 46% |

(Read from the slide's chart; figures as printed.)

**What staff say they heard** (all marked "Learning is preliminary"):
- Themes: the magnitude of change; neighborhood schools matter ("Walkability", "Losing community hubs"); wanting
  concrete staffing, transportation and safety plans; special education concerns; Spanish DLI consolidation;
  "Something has to be done"; and **"Skepticism of district data and financial transparency"**, including
  "Distrust in PSU models" and "Wanting more transparency, independent analysis" [BB:14615901 p.10].
- By region, the West (Ida B. Wells and Lincoln): "Fix aging, unsafe buildings"; "Provide safe routes, with
  consideration for unique geography"; "Transparent data and finances" [BB:14615901 p.11].
- Named tensions include neighborhood schools vs. focus options ("Surveys say that the proposed scenarios
  over-emphasize choice programs"), small schools vs. consolidated programming, union seniority vs. retaining teachers
  of color, and "Equity on the East side vs. geographic isolation on the west side": "Westside families concerned
  about topography creating hazards for student safety" [BB:14615901 p.13].

**PSU forecasts slide** [BB:14615901 p.14]. PPS defends the forecasts:
- "Pre-COVID five-year forecasts typically within 1–4% of actuals"; "2020–2021 forecasts missed by up to 10% (COVID)";
  "Forecasts since 2021 within 1%".
- "School-level forecasts tied to the middle series".
- Analysis: these accuracy figures are district-level. School-level forecasts miss by more. Maplewood's Oct 1, 2026
  count (304) was 16 above PSU's 2026-27 forecast of 288, about 5.6% (see
  [`../topics/psu_2026_forecast_vs_actual.md`](../topics/psu_2026_forecast_vs_actual.md)). The slide does not give
  school-level accuracy.

**Student-journey examples** (Beverly Cleary 6th grade to Beaumont; Atkinson DLI to whole-school DLI at Lent),
labeled "Proposed student experience for graphic development. Rightsizing decisions are not final"
[BB:14615901 p.15] [BB:14615901 p.16].

**How staff say they are applying what they heard** [BB:14615901 p.17]:
- Added engagements; published a dashboard, feeder-pattern visuals and "What we heard" reports.
- "Identifying areas where there are unintended impacts".
- "Revising assumptions", naming three: "DLI forecasts", "Prioritization for programs and neighborhood schools", and
  "Transportation needs and planning".
- Analysis: the second item is the closest the deck comes to signaling a change from the October scenarios. It does
  not name schools.

## Board Q&A (Oct 30): rightsizing items

The Oct 30 Q&A is a cumulative document. The rightsizing questions below about the West side also appear in the
Oct 19 version [BB:14580488 p.49] [BB:14580488 p.60]; this is the first time they are in the archive's analysis.

- **Why Maplewood students go to Rieke and Hayhurst, not Capitol Hill or Markham (Engelsman).** Staff answer
  [BB:14618390 p.36]:
  - "In the Wells attendance area there are six elementaries, all are projected to be below the threshold of 360 in
    the fall of 2027. Four of them, Hayhurst (274), Stephenson (271), Rieke (252) and Maplewood (278) are roughly
    80-100 students below the threshold."
  - The threshold is the bottom of a target range whose top is functional capacity; Rieke's range is 360–465.
    "In order to get the remaining schools above the sustainable threshold, we proposed closing two schools in
    Scenario A."
  - "Maplewood students were routed 30% to Rieke and 70% to Hayhurst because Stephenson's students consumed
    Markham's available capacity, while sending them to Capitol Hill would cause overcrowding and split middle school
    feeder alignments with this cohort."
  - "the quantitative model optimizes for building utilization rather than non-measurable social networks".
- **Housing (Splitt).** Staff: PSU "does include these in their forecasts as they monitor housing developments. One in
  particular in the Rieke area was called out in the latest report" [BB:14618390 p.3].
- **Still "In process" (no answer yet):**
  - Chase-Miller: "have we double triple quadruple checked the housing starts? Maplewood seems pretty certain that
    Alpenrose should be a game changer for them" [BB:14618390 p.47].
  - Splitt: "Can I get a full understanding of the PSU projections for Alpenrose, and why fewer units in Rieke yield
    more students?", noting "the estimate of 12 students" for Hayhurst "could lead to issues if that estimate is
    offbase" [BB:14618390 p.58] [BB:14618390 p.59].
  - Splitt: why some schools close in both scenarios and others only in A, and "What makes Stephenson, Lewis, and Rose
    City Park the ones that were selected to stay open in B?" [BB:14618390 p.58].
  - Splitt: a colleague "had the PSU numbers audited and found some inaccuracies. How have we audited or
    double-checked those numbers and can we do that?" [BB:14618390 p.59].
  - Splitt: whether some changes could phase in, letting current students finish in their program
    [BB:14618390 p.59].
  - Chase-Miller: whether James John is in fact ADA-accessible, and if so whether closing it is still right
    [BB:14618390 p.47].
  - Engelsman: the cost of moving schools and programs for 2027-28 and whether it comes from the general fund
    [BB:14618390 p.47].

## Analysis: what this means

- The deck does not change the scenarios. The decision path is now fixed: recommendation **Nov 17**, vote **Dec 15**.
- Staff's own survey puts "Strong Neighborhood Schools" first (41% ranked it top priority, double any other principle)
  and the Equity Lens last. Staff also report distrust of PSU models and requests for independent analysis.
- Several Board members have open questions on exactly the issues that decide Maplewood: housing (Alpenrose / Raleigh
  Crest), PSU accuracy and auditing, and why Scenario B spares some schools.
- The Q&A states the Wells-area 2027-28 projections the closures rest on: Maplewood 278, Hayhurst 274,
  Stephenson 271, Rieke 252. Maplewood's 278 is PSU's 2026-27 forecast for fall 2027 [PPS:pps_net/data-and-reporting/2026-27/psu_prc_enrollment_forecast_2026-27_to_2035-36.pdf p.39],
  which was built before the Oct 1, 2026 count of 304 [PPS:pps_net/data-and-reporting/2026-27/enrollment_by_grade_and_program_type.pdf p.2].
