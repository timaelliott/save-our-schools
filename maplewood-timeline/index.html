Maplewood Elementary · Southwest Portland

History of Maplewood School

From a one-room schoolhouse with 69 students in 1912 to the school on the hill today: buildings, closures, campaigns to save it, and the community that keeps showing up.

  1. 1910s
  2. 1912

    Maplewood School opens

    In the fall of 1912, Maplewood School opens with 26 boys and 43 girls and one teacher. The one-room portable schoolhouse features a gable roof, weatherboard siding painted white, five windows, a front and back door, and a pot-bellied stove. Maplewood School joins the Multnomah County School District.

    Source: Northwest Friends, September 1953. George Fox University Archives

  3. 1913-1914

    Second school building constructed

    Maplewood School quickly outgrows the one-room schoolhouse. A larger building, with two large classrooms, two outhouses, and a rooftop bell, is constructed during Winter 1913-1914.

  4. 1915

    Significant building renovations

    The new larger schoolhouse is raised off the ground for the addition of a new basement, restrooms to replace the outhouses, water fountains, and a furnace.

  5. 1920s
  6. 7 October 1920

    First prize in rural exhibits at Multnomah County Fair

    Maplewood School wins first prize in rural exhibits at the Multnomah County Fair in Gresham. “The work done by the school children through the clubs includes sewing, cooking, canning, garden and field crops, and the raising of sheep, calves, hogs and goats. The children themselves are greatly interested in the work and some keen competition has developed.”

    Source: “Races and Old Sol Draw 5000 to Fair,” Morning Oregonian (Portland, Or.), 8 October 1920

  7. Summer 1921

    Book wagon visits during summer break

    Maplewood students are able to check out books from Multnomah County's Central Library via a book wagon that visits the community on Fridays over summer break.

    Source: "Library Wagon Enlarges Scope on Daily Round," The Oregon Sunday Journal, 10 July 1921

  8. 1926

    Maplewood District No. 52 annexed into Portland School District

    Maplewood District No. 52 is annexed into Portland School District No. 1.

    Source: “Maplewood Plans Up,” The Oregonian, 28 August 1926

  9. 1928

    First schoolhouse sold and moved across the street

    Maplewood's first one-room schoolhouse is sold to the Maplewood Sunday school for $10 and subsequently moved across the street for $52. The building is used for community and religious gatherings until it is demolished in 2001.

    Source: George Fox University Archives

  10. 1930s
  11. 1935

    School board decides to close Maplewood School

    The school board decides to close Maplewood School. The Maplewood community launches a campaign to save the school.

  12. 1936

    Maplewood and Multnomah Schools join Multnomah School District No. 11

    In order to prevent closure by the Portland school district, Maplewood's sub-district is granted permission to withdraw from District No. 1 and joins District No. 11.

    Source: “District Releases Maplewood Area,” Oregonian, 18 August 1936

  13. 1940s
  14. 1942

    Maplewood students raise a pig named Victory in place of victory garden

    In place of a victory garden, Maplewood School’s children raise a pig named “Victory,” feeding her with scraps from the school cafeteria. Victory and the school children are featured in a photo spread published in The Oregonian, with the caption: “But behind all the fun lies serious purpose. Victory is a war pig. When fat enough, the children will eat her.”

    Source: “Victory Pig Has High Time,” The Oregonian, 15 April 1942

  15. 1947

    New gymnasium constructed

    A new gymnasium is constructed for $84,000 and is still in use today.

    Source: Image courtesy of Lorene & Dick Lehrer

  16. 1950s
  17. January 1951

    Students stay home due to power outage and scarlet fever outbreak

    12 cases of scarlet fever reported among 150 students enrolled at Maplewood School. Students stay home that Monday due to a power outage caused by hurricane-force winds in the area and for the rest of the week due to the scarlet fever outbreak.

    Source: “Scarlet Fever Closes School; 12 Cases Reported at Maplewood,” Oregonian, 16 January 1951

  18. 1951-1954

    Third school building constructed

    Between 1951-1954, the second schoolhouse building is demolished and the school's third building is constructed.

  19. 7 September 1954

    New school building opens to 322 students

    Construction is completed in the summer of 1954, and Maplewood's third school building opens to 322 students for the 1954-1955 school year.

  20. July 1956

    Maplewood joins School District No. 1

    Maplewood School is annexed into School District No. 1. 375 students attend Maplewood.

  21. 1960s
  22. 1961

    Portables add 2,448 square feet of space

    Maplewood's blacktop playcourt is converted to classroom space.

  23. 1962-1963

    Maplewood residents vote in favor of City of Portland annexation

    After many previous failed votes, residents of the Maplewood Water District vote in favor of annexation to the City of Portland.

  24. 1970s
  25. 1976

    Community forum on school closures

    The Maplewood Citizens Advisory Committee holds a community forum seeking feedback to a PPS proposal that would consolidate and close schools in the then Wilson HS cluster. Attendees are asked "is it important to maintain a school in Maplewood? Why? Why not?" Answers include: "the building is in good shape," "the area is developing," "lack of statistics to back this," "boundary changes should be explored," and "locking it up would be like striking a death blow to the community."

    Source: Maplewood PTA Historical Archives

  26. 1979

    Board votes to close Multnomah School

    Portland School Board votes to close Multnomah School at the end of the 1978-1979 school year and assigns Multnomah School's K-5 students to Maplewood and 6-8th grader to Hayhurst beginning in September 1979.

    Source: Maplewood PTA Historical Archives

  27. 1980s
  28. December 1980

    Maplewood School "inadvertently" put on school closure list

    Maplewood School is “inadvertently” put on a district closure list due to “an arithmetic error.” This error is especially challenging for Maplewood families who recently transferred from the closed Multnomah School.

    Source: Maplewood PTA Historical Archives

  29. 1987

    Maplewood students win the Great Portland Glass, Tin and Paper Chase

    Maplewood students win first place in the Great Portland Glass, Tin and Paper Chase in Pioneer Courthouse Square, beating a City of Portland team that included Mayor Bud Clark and City Commissioner Mike Lindberg. Maplewood students were selected "because they recently recycled 18 tons of paper in one week during a school project."

    Source: The Oregonian

  30. 1988

    Maplewood expected to exceed capacity

    Maplewood is among 12 elementary schools that PPS expects to exceed capacity by 1992, including Bridlemile, Hayhurst, and Stephenson. The report suggests moving Maplewood kindergarteners to another school for the next year, reopening Reike, and redrawing boundaries in the area.

  31. 1990s
  32. 1990

    Maplewood commissions sculpture for library

    Maplewood commissions Georgia Gerber to sculpt two bronze bears for the school library. The art project was among 22 others made possible by $250,000 in funding to purchase art for schools.

    Source: Photo credit: Melissa Laney

  33. 1998

    Accessibility improvements

    Accessibility improvements to the school building include an elevator, ramps, gender-neutral bathroom, and drinking fountain. The gym roof is also replaced and the fire safety system upgraded.

  34. 1999

    Arts Alive grant

    Thanks to an Arts Alive grant of $1,700, Maplewood kindergarteners get to "tap dance, paint and act out stories they read, drawing on rhythm, color and dramatic storytelling to make them better readers."

  35. 2000s
  36. 2001

    Maplewood's original one-room school house torn down

    89 years after its construction, Maplewood's original one-room schoolhouse is torn down. In two locations, it served many purposes, including dance hall, community gathering space, preschool, primary school, and place of worship.

    Source: George Fox University Archives

  37. 2002

    2 Maplewood teachers honored by Portland Schools Foundation

    Maplewood librarian Pepye Ladd and second-grade teacher Marianne McClenagh are among 16 educators honored by the Portland Schools Foundation for “boosting achievement.” The school receives $1,000 for books or materials for each award.

  38. 2004

    Bookbaggers 20th anniversary

    Maplewood celebrates the 20th anniversary of its Bookbagger event. Students participate in reading activities and camp out in the school gymnasium. Other popular long-running PTA events over the years include: B.Y.O.B. (Bring your own bear or other stuffed animal) P.J. Storytime, Carnival, Halloween Parade, Back to School Picnic, and many more.

  39. 2007

    Oregon Battle of the Books (OBOB)

    4th and 5th grade teams participate in the inaugural Oregon Battle of the Books (OBOB) under the leadership of Maplewood’s librarian, Elin Kordohl. The next year, 3rd graders join.

  40. 2010s
  41. 2010

    Elin Kordahl named Oregon Elementary School Librarian of the Year

    Elin Kordahl, Maplewood’s librarian, is named the Oregon 2010 Elementary School Librarian of the Year by the Oregon Association of School Libraries.

  42. 2012

    Maplewood School celebrates its centennial

    Maplewood School celebrates 100 years since its founding in 1912.

  43. 2016

    Maplewood boundaries redrawn due to overcrowding

    Due to a PSU report that forecasts overcrowding at Chapman, Capitol Hill, and Maplewood, boundaries are redrawn to balance enrollment.

  44. 2017

    "It's a school on a hill in a friendly neighborhood"

    At the request of school counselor Kendall Cunningham, Maplewood parent Kris Kirkman writes a new school song, highlighting Maplewood's values of safe, responsible, and kind. Ms. Cunningham leads students in the song at school assemblies.

    Watch the school song
  45. 2020s
  46. 16 March 2020

    Governor Brown orders statewide school closures

    Governor Kate Brown orders statewide school closures in response to COVID-19 pandemic. The initial 6-week closure extends to the cancellation of in-person instruction for the remainder of the 2019-2020 school year.

  47. 3 April 2020

    Teacher parade

    Families and community members line up to wave to a caravan of Maplewood teachers and staff as they drive by.

  48. 2021

    Maplewood's bike bus launches

    Maplewood's bike bus launches in 2021 and is still going strong. On 16 October 2026, 400 students, parents, teachers, and community members ride and line the route to rally for Maplewood!

  49. 2021

    Maplewood Mural Project

    Maplewood collaborates with muralist William Hernandez, through an art in residence program, to create a mural that reflects the school community values of inclusion, collaboration, diversity, and education inspired by Portland and neighborhood history. Students paint the colorful leaves on lower walls of the main building.

    Source: William Hernandez Art, williamhernandezart.com

  50. 2023

    "HELLO!" mural

    Maplewood's "HELLO!" mural is installed. The mural says "hello" in 25+ languages and features art created by and representing Maplewood students. "This is a small effort by us, the PTA, to acknowledge the diversity that exists within the community (the school and Portland)."

  51. January 2024

    Maplewood welcomes students and teachers from Markham Elementary

    Markham Elementary school is forced to close for hazardous material cleanup from extensive ice storm damage. Maplewood welcomes four Markham classrooms - kindergarten, first grade, and ISC.

  52. 2026

    William Chou, Regional Teacher of the Year

    William Chou, Maplewood's beloved Teacher Chou, is recognized by the Oregon Department of Education as a statewide finalist and Regional Teacher of the Year 2026-27.
