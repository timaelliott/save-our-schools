The status quo also hurts focal students

Without changes, staff project that the share of K-5 Title I schools meeting the size threshold falls “from 18% to 9%” and Title I middle schools fall to 0% by 2031-32 {{SRC_SMITH}}. The $65 million budget gap means cuts somewhere.

  • On poverty and race overall, the closure list roughly mirrors the district: average poverty is 32.2% (A) and 35.1% (B) at closing schools against about 32–33% elsewhere. The sharper gaps are in special education, walkability and Title I status at receiving schools.

About this page

All sources are public PPS records: Board meeting videos, BoardBook agenda documents and Board Q&A, and the school family guides PPS published in October 2026. Because PPS edits and removes posted documents, every source is also archived with its retrieval date in the save-our-schools research archive, which has the full transcripts, data tables and method notes.

Averages across schools are simple averages of the percentages printed in each school’s family guide (“No changes” column). Speakers are named from the video; meeting captions do not label speakers.
