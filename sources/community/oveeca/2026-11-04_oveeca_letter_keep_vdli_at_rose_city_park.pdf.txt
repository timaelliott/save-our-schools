     November 4, 2026



     Re:    Please Keep the Vietnamese Dual Language Immersion (VDLI) Program at
            Rose City Park Elementary



     To Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong and all members of the PPS School Board:

     We write to respectfully and strongly oppose the recommendation to move the Vietnamese Dual
     Language Immersion (VDLI) program from Rose City Park Elementary School to Vestal
     Elementary School.

     This is not simply a move from one building to another. For the Vietnamese American community - the
     second-largest group of foreign-born residents in Oregon - PLACE matters. HISTORY matters. And
     above all, the IMPACT on students and families must matter.

     In 1975, the rst Vietnamese refugees to arrive in Portland were housed at Halsey Square, near NE
     67th Avenue and Halsey Street. With the help of churches and community sponsors, and from among
     those few families, a community grew. We opened businesses and restaurants along Sandy
     Boulevard, bought homes, founded churches and organizations, and built what many of us called
     Portland's Little Saigon.

     Rose City Park and Northeast Portland are where our roots in this city were planted.

     Families in the VDLI bene t greatly from the current walkable cluster of Rose City Park to Roseway
     Heights to McDaniel High School. Middle school and high school students in the neighborhood often
     walk back to Rose City Park to help with after-school activities and to celebrate important cultural
     events for our community such as Autumn Moon Festival and Tet Lunar New Year - all of these are
     centered around our community’s roots in Rose City Park.

     There are also other practical concerns with the proposed move. Vestal’s neighborhood kids feed into
     Harrison Park Middle School while students in the VDLI would feed into Roseway Heights.
     Furthermore, Vestal is farther for many current VDLI families. Parents have already described serious
     transportation and access challenges, and some have said they may leave the program if it moves.
     That should concern all of us. Dual language immersion depends on long-term family commitment. If
     this move drives families away, it weakens the very enrollment and community that have made VDLI
     successful. Decisions about this program should strengthen access, not create new barriers.

     Over the decades, the community has spread across Portland and beyond. But our community’s
     history does not disappear. Keeping VDLI at Rose City Park honors it and maintains a visible
     cultural and educational presence where our community's journey began.

The Vietnamese American community is often seen as a “model minority,” but that perception is far
from the truth. Many of our families still face language barriers, economic hardship, and limited access
to resources. For them, VDLI is not a luxury. It is a lifeline that helps children succeed academically
while staying connected to their language and heritage.

This decision should also be measured against PPS’s own Racial Educational Equity Policy (Board
Policy 2.10.010-P). In that policy, the Board commits to welcoming and empowering families of color,
including those whose rst language is not English, and to providing additional and differentiated
resources so every student can succeed. Moving VDLI farther from the families it serves, at the
risk of driving them out, runs counter to those commitments. A decision true to this policy would
reduce barriers for Vietnamese American families, not add to them.

Before making a decision with such signi cant consequences, I urge PPS to fully consider:

  •    The historical connection between Rose City Park and Portland's Vietnamese refugee
       community;
  •    The disruption of the cohesive walkable cluster and creation of transportation burden on VDLI
       families; and
  •    The potential loss of families and the long-term sustainability of the program.

The Vietnamese community has already been displaced once as Portland changed around us. Please
do not make another decision that further disconnects us from the place that represents our
history and roots.

The Oregon-Vietnam Economic, Education and Cultural Association (OVEECA) is a non-pro t
organization of community leaders focused on fostering and sustaining cultural, education and
economic relationships between the Vietnamese people and the United States. We actively forge new
educational opportunities for our next generation of leaders.

VDLI is more than an academic program. It represents language, culture, identity, belonging, and the
hopes of refugee families who worked hard to build a future for their children. I respectfully urge you to
keep it at Rose City Park.

Thank you for carefully considering the voices of the students, families, educators, and community
members who care deeply about this program.
