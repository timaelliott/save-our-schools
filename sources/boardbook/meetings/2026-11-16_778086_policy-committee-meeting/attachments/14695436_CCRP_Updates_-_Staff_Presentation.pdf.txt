Policy Subcommittee Meeting




                              11.16.26
Recommendation on Proposed
Indoor Air Quality (IAQ) Policy Additions

●   Core Recommendation: Pause adoption of IAQ additions to
    the Climate Crisis Response Policy (CCRP).


●   Policy Separation: If desired, establish Indoor Air Quality (IAQ)
    as a standalone policy with dedicated leadership (IAQ
    Coordinator).


●   Primary Objective: Protect the current scope of the CCRP
    goals while defining a separate and rigorous indoor
    environmental health policy, if needed.
Core Policy Misalignment:
Why IAQ & CCRP Should Remain Separate
●   Scope & Focus Shift:
     ○ CCRP Focus: Global emissions mitigation, decarbonization, and
         long-term climate action (net-zero by 2040).
     ○ IAQ Focus: Micro-environmental public health and occupational
         safety protocols, which historically exist as internal Standard
         Operating Procedures (SOPs).
●   Risk of Policy Dilution:
     ○ Combining daily occupational health mandates and global
         decarbonization in one policy dilutes core CCRP objectives.
     ○ Pivots Climate Justice, Energy & Sustainability (CJES) team priority
         from structural carbon reduction to routine maintenance.
●   Governance Alignment:
     ○ Operational metrics evolve rapidly; standard operating procedures
         (SOPs) and Environmental Health & Safety (EH&S) frameworks
         provide proper flexibility compared to rigid policy language.
Energy & Emissions Impact
Conﬂict Between IAQ & CCRP

●   Ventilation vs. Carbon Goals:
     ○ Targeting 6 to 12 Air Changes per Hour (ACH) requires
         continuous HVAC operation and high intake of unconditioned
         outdoor air.
●   Increased Energy Consumption:
     ○ HVAC energy usage would increase by 10% to 40% per site.
     ○ Directly conflicts with CCRP mandate to prioritize investments
         achieving the greatest emissions reduction.
●   Impact on CCRP Targets:
     ○ Higher energy demand creates significant headwinds against
         reaching district-wide net-zero targets.
Financial & Resource Constraints
●   Estimated Annual Operating Cost Increase (per site):
     ○ Elementary School (e.g., Atkinson): +$15,000 to $30,000 / year
●   Capital Construction Costs:
     ○ Estimated 1% to 5% increase in total project costs.
●   Capital Diversion:
     ○ Mandating continuous in-room sensors, 5-year airflow rebalancing,
        and rapid filter turnover diverts limited capital from primary climate
        initiatives:
          ■ Solar installs
          ■ Building envelope sealing
          ■ Fleet electrification
     ○   Increasing ventilation rates above codes and standards could
         risk the longevity of our aging systems and increased failure
         rates of our building systems.
3rd-Party Technical Evaluation
Industry Expert Findings & Standards Baseline

 ●   About Apex Companies: 38 years of experience, ~2,000 expert
     consultants in ESG, environment, compliance, and health & safety.
 ●   Regulatory Code vs. Voluntary Guidance:
      ○ Oregon Mechanical Specialty Code (OMSC) / ASHRAE
         62.1-2022: Legally adopted, peer-reviewed engineering standards
         balancing air quality, energy efficiency, and cost.
      ○ Harvard Healthy Buildings Program: Voluntary pandemic
         mitigation research framework; not a regulatory design standard
         (targets are 50%–200% higher than code minimums).
 ●   Key Finding: Air Change Rates (ACH) alone are not a universally
     accepted benchmark for ventilation safety or system efficacy.
Climate Crisis Response Committee
Involvement
 ●    Climate Crisis Response Committee (CCRC):
       ○ Charter: 9 members (2 students, 7 adults) advising the Board on
           CCRP implementation.
       ○ Current Status: Not consulted on the proposed policy expansion.
 ●    Governance Concerns:
       ○ Alters committee oversight scope without committee member input.
       ○ Risks setting a precedent for rushed policy modifications that could
           compromise long-term climate targets.
 ●    Best Practice: Have the IAQ policy exist independent of the CCRC, or,
      at minimum, engage the CCRC to ensure policy stability and alignment
      with original charter goals.

     “The mission of the Climate Crisis Response Committee is to monitor effective
     implementation, provide transparency, and to track and report progress or
     challenges of the Portland Public Schools’s Climate Crisis Response, Climate
     Justice and Sustainable Practices Policy and the goals in it.”
Staff Recommendation & Next Steps

 ●   Pause Policy Adoption: Pause embedding IAQ additions into the CCRP
     to allow thorough Board review of 3rd-party data.
 ●   Maintain Policy Boundaries: Keep CCRP focused on emissions
     reduction, net-zero goals, and structural climate action.
 ●   If Establishing a Standalone Policy: Draft a separate, dedicated IAQ
     policy supported by an IAQ Coordinator and tailored operational
     procedures and non-conflicting goals of CCRP.
