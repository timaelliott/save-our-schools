              DIGITAL DEVICE AND SCREEN USE POLICY​
                          SCHOOLS BEYOND SCREENS PORTLAND DRAFT



I. PURPOSE
 This Policy establishes rules for the evaluation, deployment, and use of digital technologies and
 screen-based devices within Portland Public Schools. It supports PPS’s Graduate Portrait and
 Portland Public Schools’ mission to provide an equitable, high-quality education by
 safeguarding students' physical, social-emotional, and cognitive development, and by ensuring
 that adopted technologies enhance, rather than diminish learning outcomes and instructional
 time.

 Through clear, consistent, age-appropriate expectations, this Policy reflects a shared
 responsibility among schools, families, and students to promote academic focus, critical
 thinking, and healthy, lifelong learning habits, preparing students to be responsible digital
 citizens throughout their school years and beyond.

II. DEFINITIONS
 A. Personal data: Any information that identifies or could reasonably identify a student. This
 includes the categories listed in Section IV.L and data generated through a student’s use of a
 digital tool, such as usage, location, and behavioral data.

 B. Device: As used in this policy, a device means any PPS-owned, PPS-issued, or PPS-managed
 laptop, desktop computer, tablet, mobile phone, wearable device, virtual or augmented reality
 equipment, or other internet-connected hardware used to access PPS systems or instructional
 content.

 C. Digital tool: Any software, application, website, online platform, browser extension, or
 online service accessed on a digital device, including learning management systems and
 assessment platforms.

 D. Educational Technology: Educational Technology means software, digital applications,
 online services, websites, platforms, and other digital tools used for instructional, assessment,
 accessibility, safety, communication, or administrative purposes.

 E. Technology Education” or “TechEd: Instruction in which the technology itself is the
 learning objective, including computer literacy, keyboarding, research skills, and computer
 science.

 F. Approved Educational Technology: Approved Educational Technology means Educational
 Technology that PPS has reviewed and authorized under its educational technology approval
 process.

 G. PPS Digital Toolkit means the District’s published list of approved digital tools, including
 the approved grade levels and uses for each tool and any features the District has disabled.

 H. Core Digital Curriculum Product: A District-adopted or District-licensed instructional
 program that students use on a device for instruction, practice, or assessment as part of a
 structured curriculum, such as i-Ready.

 I. Screen Time: Screen Time refers to the amount of time a student is expected to view or
 interact with device displays, including structured educational technology used for instruction
 and unstructured or non-instructional media. Screen Time does not include use of assistive
 technology required to provide a student with access to instruction or PPS services.

 J. Independent Evidence: means at least one study that:

   1.​ Was conducted or validated by a party with no financial interest in the product
   2.​ Meets at least Tier 3 (“promising”) of the ESSA evidence standards
   3.​ Measures retention or application of the targeted skill outside the tool, ideally at least one
       week after use
   4.​ Included students comparable in grade level and learning needs to those who will use the
       tool

K. Generative artificial intelligence” or “generative AI: Software that produces text, images,
audio, video, or code in response to user prompts. This includes large language model (LLM)
chatbots and generative AI features embedded within other digital tools.

III. STANDARDS FOR SCREEN USE IN INSTRUCTION
 A. Proven Benefit Standard. Digital tools will be used when they offer a clear, evidence-based
 advantage over non-digital methods in meeting curricular objectives.

 B. Multi-Sensory Learning Priority. Screens will be used only for specific, approved purposes
 and will not replace hands-on, experiential learning.

 C. Paper-Based Materials. Paper-based materials will be the primary medium for instruction
 and assignments in reading, writing, and mathematics. Students will complete written drafts and
 revisions by hand, particularly in the early grades. As students advance and demonstrate mastery
 of these foundational skills, digital tools may be introduced gradually to supplement, rather than
 replace, paper-based learning.

IV. GENERAL RULES AND RESTRICTIONS

 The following rules will apply to all digital device and screen use, except as dictated by
 Individualized education plans (IEPs), 504 plans, or Personalized education plans (PEPs):

 A. Centralized Device Use for K-8. Devices will be used to support specific in-class
 curriculum and learning activities and will not serve as the primary instructional medium, unless
 the student is participating in an online school or class approved or delivered by PPS. PPS will

     B. K-8 Screen-Free Periods. Recess, lunch, and passing periods will be screen- and device-free
     for grades K-8 by default.

     C. Screen-Free Lunch for Grade 9-12. The use of computers during high school lunch periods
     will be restricted to specific classrooms or library areas specifically designated as study halls.

     D. Instructional Flexibility. PPS recognizes that some courses and learning experiences may
     require greater use of technology to achieve their instructional objectives. Screen time
     guidance should be applied with consideration for the unique requirements of courses such as
     computer science, graphic design, digital media arts, yearbook, engineering, career technical
     education pathways, film studies, and other technology-intensive programs. In these courses
     and programs, technology use may exceed general screen time recommendations when it is
     directly connected to curriculum, student creation, design, programming, production,
     research, or other active learning experiences. Such use should remain purposeful,
     instructionally aligned, and consistent with the goals of this policy.

     E. Offline Homework. Homework assignments will be offered in a paper format whenever
     possible. Internet connectivity will not be required for the completion of homework
     assignments. This does not prohibit the use of a learning management system in grades 6-12.

     F. Use only PPS approved digital tools and apps. PPS-approved digital tools/apps will be
     vetted as detailed in this policy and approved to ensure they are safe, effective, and legal.. When
     students are using devices, all teachers, including substitutes, will utilize PPS-approved
     monitoring tools (ie Securly, etc.) to monitor student screen use for appropriateness.

     Staff may not require or direct students to create accounts, disclose personal information, upload
     student work, or use an educational technology service unless the service has been approved
     through the PPS educational technology review process. Staff may not accept click-though
     terms on behalf of PPS or use free applications, browser extensions, pilot programs, or artificial
     intelligence tools unless authorized under that process.

     G. Prohibition on Advertisement and Sales. No advertisements will appear on any computer
     device or projected screen at school. Any device used at school, including those used by
     teachers or administrators, must be installed with premium ad blockers. Furthermore, students
     and families will not be required to use any app or website that pushes “paid upgrades,”
     “premium services,” “in-app purchases,” or similar features.1

VI. PROHIBITION OF ONE-TO-ONE DEVICES
    A. Prohibition on One-to-One Devices. PPS will not issue individual devices to K-8 students.
    All student access to digital devices will be limited to shared devices in dedicated computer labs
    or supervised computer carts as set forth in Section IV of this Policy.

    B. Exceptions. Students may take PPS devices home to address an identified instructional,
    accessibility, equity, or academic-support need, as approved by the principal or designee, or as
    required by an individualized education program (“IEP”), Section 504 plan, or applicable law.
    PPS will provide the student’s parent or guardian with notice of the take-home arrangement and
    obtain an acknowledgment of applicable device-use requirements. Failure to return an
    acknowledgment will not prevent PPS from providing a device when the device is required to
    provide the student with equal access, appropriate services, or legally required
    accommodations.

VII. Digital Literacy
PPS will integrate developmentally appropriate Digital Literacy instruction into the core
curriculum for grades K–12.

    A. Digital Literacy instruction will focus on:

     1.​ Evaluating source credibility and detecting misinformation;
     2.​ Understanding data privacy and the Responsible Use Policy;
     3.​ The environmental and social impacts of digital technologies;
     4.​ Safe and respectful use of digital tools;
     5.​ Skills to critically evaluate digital content;
     6.​ Developing critical thinking, creativity, communication, collaboration, empathy,
         judgment, and independent problem-solving skills in a rapidly evolving technological
         world; and
     7.​ Maintaining healthy technology habits, including attention management, period breaks,
         ergonomics, and awareness of persuasive or engagement-focused design.

IX. DIGITAL TOOL VETTING AND APPROVAL PROCESS
    A. Rubric and Vetting. PPS, in collaboration with the Technology Advisory Committee, will
    review and update the PPS Digital Technology and App Approval Process with a formal,
    transparent rubric and vetting process to evaluate ed tech and digital tools. The rubric will consider
    targeted learning objectives, independent evidence*, cognitive processes, student data privacy and
    safety, attention-capturing design and game mechanics, and time bound requirements.

    New student-facing digital products should only be adopted when there are proven benefits over
    analog alternatives. A process for teachers and staff to request approval for a new tool or app
    should also be in place.8

    *Independent evidence as defined above.

XI. FACULTY AND STAFF SUPPORT
 A. Ongoing Professional Support. PPS will provide educators and staff with ongoing training,
 guidance, and resources to implement this policy, including:

  1.​ Instructional practice: effective, curriculum-aligned use of digital tools, and
      high-quality non-digital alternatives.
  2.​ Platform and tool training: hands-on training on each approved platform and app before
      staff are expected to use it with students, including privacy and data settings, how to
      disable restricted features, and how to confirm parental consent status where it is required.
  3.​ Consistent, equitable enforcement: clear districtwide standards and procedures so that
      the same behavior results in the same response for every student, with an emphasis on
      non-exclusionary responses rather than confiscation or discipline.
  4.​ Digital literacy instruction: strategies for teaching students to regulate their own
      technology use, evaluate information, and protect their privacy.
  5.​ Policy procedures: using the allow list request process, and meeting accommodation
      obligations for assistive technology and students with IEPs or 504 plans.

                            APPENDIX A: GUIDING PRINCIPLES
These guiding principles were conceived with the whole child in mind, centering around the
question of what is truly best for all children's learning and development.

    A. Research-backed Pedagogy. Neuroscientific research shows that effective learning requires
    sustained focus, deliberate effort, and emotional, and relational connection.9 Students require a
    balance of direct instruction, social interaction and collaboration, physical activity, and
    hands-on learning. Technology use should not replace these essential experiences. Reading
    comprehension is often lower when on a screen rather than paper.10 Multitasking and
    gamification can diminish learning.11 Whenever possible, independent research should be used
    to evaluate the efficacy of digital tools.

    B. Teacher-led Instruction First: Learning is the most effective when directed and guided by
    skilled educators and meaningful human interaction.12 Technology should support and enhance
    teacher-led instruction, not replace the essential role of educators in fostering engagement,
    relationships, and learning.

    C. Adaptability. Cognitive skills and habits of mind enable students to adapt to evolving
    circumstances and future technological trends. Teaching students to think deeply enables them
    to use any tool; teaching students to use a specific tool limits them to that tool.

    D. Real-life and Multi-sensory Practices. Analog learning methods–including whole book
    reading and handwriting as well as paper based math and use of manipulatives–reliably produce
    stronger comprehension and retention than digital methods.13

    E. Student Health and Well-Being. Excessive device use has been shown to be correlated with
    poor attention, distraction, anxiety, depression, myopia, and sleep disruption among children and
    adolescents.14 Students deserve learning environments that minimize distractions and maximize

     F. Equity and Inclusion. Excessive reliance on edtech can reinforce inequity and widen the

     H. Technology Education vs. Technology-Delivered Education. Technology Education
     ("TechEd") is distinct from Education Technology ("EdTech"). TechEd refers to a curriculum
     of computer literacy and may include, among other things, typing and research instruction.
     EdTech refers to digital tools, software, platforms, and devices used to deliver core subject
     content.

     I. Transparency. Educators need clear guidelines on acceptable digital platforms, devices, and
     screen use. Families need clear communication at the start of each school year about what to
     expect regarding screen time, educational app and tool use, and the data privacy policies
     governing these tools.17

     J. Data privacy. Some data can help show when and why a learner is struggling, but the same
     information can be used for marketing, product development, or other purposes that do not
