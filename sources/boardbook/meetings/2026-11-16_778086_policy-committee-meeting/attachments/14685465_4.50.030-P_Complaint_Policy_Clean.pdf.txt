                   Complaint Policy
I.​   Definitions and Introduction

      Complainant: The person filing a complaint against PPS.

      Division 22: The educational standards, found in Oregon Administrative Rules Chapter 581,
      Division 22, that the State of Oregon has determined must be met in order to be a standard
      school district.

      Final Decision: The written decision from PPS that settles a matter at the district level. Some
      Final Decisions have additional appeal rights to other agencies.

      Affirmed: The decision made after a review of the evidence that there is reason to believe
      that a violation of state or federal law or District policy has occurred.

      Not Affirmed: The decision made after a review of the evidence that a violation of state or
      federal law or District policy has not occurred.

      We know that when families are engaged with their children’s schools, children are more
      likely to have academic success, graduate from high school, develop self-confidence, and
      demonstrate strong social behaviors. When we put children at the center of our decisions
      and actions, we can deliver better experiences for all students and families. We also know
      that our school district can be complex and that families and students will have questions
      and sometimes concerns about our curriculum, classrooms, programs, and other
      experiences.

      Portland Public Schools welcomes expressions of concern as opportunities to learn, clarify
      our intentions, and engage in continuous improvement to benefit all students. We welcome
      those conversations and expect that all adults, including PPS educators and other staff, will
      model our core values--including respect, relationships, honesty and integrity, collaboration,
      and a strong commitment to racial equity and social justice--when we have those
      conversations.

      When a student or family has a concern, we encourage a direct conversation with the
      educator or department directly involved in the issue. There are many other PPS staff who
      may be able to help, too, including administrators who oversee specific schools or
      departments. PPS also has a District and Family Liaison/Complaint Coordinator, who can
      help families access information, connect with PPS staff, and help resolve concerns.

      We also have several complaint processes for PPS students, families, and all who reside
      within the PPS community. All parties to a PPS complaint will be treated and treat others
      with respect and dignity, and PPS will provide support to those making complaints so that
      the process is welcoming, accessible, transparent, and reflects our strong commitment to
      racial equity and social justice.

      PPS will resolve complaints as quickly as possible and in compliance with state law.

II.​    Complaints: Accessibility; Training; and Annual Reports

           A.​A full explanation of the complaint procedure, including all forms, shall be available at
              PPS’s administrative office and on PPS’s Conflict Resolution website found here.

           B.​District Complaints

              PPS has a common complaint form (PPS Unified Complaint Form) for concerns that
              will be addressed by specific departments within PPS, such as concerns or
              complaints regarding enrollment and transfer, transportation, educational records,
              restraint and seclusion, discrimination, bias incidents, Title IX, teen dating violence,
              and 504 accommodations. Complainants will receive a final written decision from the
              Superintendent’s designee within 60 days of receipt of the complaint.

              Complaints or reports about child abuse should be directed immediately to the Child
              Abuse Hotline at 1-855-503-SAFE, as well as the PPS supervisor.

           C.​Division 22 Complaints

                 i.​    Complaints under the Oregon Department of Education’s Division 22
                        regulations [Chapter 581, Division 22 (Standards for Public Elementary and
                        Secondary Schools] include those about instructional standards and
                        district-wide practices (class size, curriculum, etc.), special education and
                        Talented and Gifted programs (but not individual concerns), health and safety,
                        equitable education opportunities, sports safety, annual reports on restraint
                        and seclusion, and mandatory District policies and plans.

                        If a Complainant is not satisfied after exhausting PPS’s complaint procedures,
                        PPS fails to provide a written decision within 30 days of submission of the
                        complaint at any step, or PPS fails to resolve the complaint within 60 days of
                        the initial filing of the complaint, Complainants may appeal PPS’s final
                        decision to the Oregon Department of Education.

                ii.​    Because complaints can be an important indicator of the health of an
                        organization, the Superintendent will provide to the Board at least annually
                        data on (1) Division 22 trends, emerging issues, and District responses, as
                        well as an assessment of the Division 22 complaint process; and (2) a
                        summary of complaints that are not governed by a process that is required by
                        statute or regulation (e.g., Title IX) that have been filed using PPS’s complaint
                        process concerning matters that affect more than an individual student or
                        school.

                iii.​   PPS will provide training for designated PPS staff on how to handle
                        complaints under Division 22.

III.​   Timelines for All Complaints

           A.​In order to investigate a complaint while memories are recent and witnesses and
              documents are likely more available, a complaint must be filed within the following
              time limits:

                 i.​    Within two years after the alleged violation or unlawful incident occurred or the
                        Complainant discovered the alleged violation or unlawful incident. For

                      incidents that are continuing in nature, the time limit runs from the date of the
                      most recent incident; or

               ii.​   Within one year after the affected student has graduated from, moved away
                      from, or otherwise left PPS, whichever is later

          B. The time limits for bringing complaints may be extended by PPS for complaints about
             significant student safety issues, including those arising out of employee misconduct,
             sexual abuse or conduct, or other allegations of harm to students.

          C. PPS accepts confidential anonymous complaints concerning Division 22 matters
             through the PPS Unified Complaint Form. Those making complaints anonymously
             should provide as much information as possible when making the complaint. PPS will
             investigate any complaint, including anonymous complaints, as fully as it can.

IV.​   Filing a Division 22 Complaint – Step 1

          A.​The written complaint must be filed with PPS’s Complaint Coordinator by letter, email,
             or the PPS Unified Complaint Form. The written complaint should include the name
             and contact information for the Complainant, a description of the concern, and the
             student’s name, if applicable. It is helpful if the written complaint also includes the
             names of any other parties involved, including witnesses; a description of efforts to
             resolve the concern; and suggestions for resolution. The Complainant will receive a
             written acknowledgement of receipt of the complaint within five days of submitting the
             complaint.

          B.​All Division 22 complaints processed under this policy will receive a decision in writing
             that addresses each concern raised and contains reasons for PPS’s decision within
             30 days of receipt of the complaint, unless the parties agree to extend the deadline,
             and the decision will include information about next steps in the complaint process.

V.​    Filing an Appeal of a Division 22 Complaint Decision – Step 2 Appeal to the School
       Board

          A. If a complaint about a Division 22 matter is not fully substantiated, the Complainant
             may request a review of the unsubstantiated allegations by the School Board. The
             request for review will be submitted in writing within 10 days of the Complainant
             receiving the decision in Step 1.

          B. The Board will vote on the appeal within 30 days of the written request to appeal
             PPS’s Step 1 decision unless the parties agree to a longer time period. The Board will
             have the full written record of the decisions at Step 1. The Complainant will also have
             the full written record and any materials or information provided to Board members
             before the hearing about the complaint unless otherwise protected from disclosure by
             law.

          C.​PPS staff and the Complainant will exchange any other written background
             information they intend to present to the Board at the appeal hearing at least 24 hours
             before the hearing. If new information is offered less than 24 hours before the Board
             hearing, the Complainant may ask for an extension of time.

          D.​Complainants may bring an advocate or support person to any meeting or
             proceeding. For hearings before the Board, PPS will inform the Complainant which
             PPS staff will be attending the hearing.

          E.​Because the Board makes the final decision at the Step 2 appeal, Board members
             should not knowingly initiate or continue contact with Complainants about the topic of

             a complaint or independently investigate allegations in the complaint once they have
             notice that a Division 22 complaint has been filed. PPS staff will regularly notify Board
             members about new Division 22 complaints that are filed.

          F.​ To make the process as fair and transparent as possible, PPS will provide a written
              overview of the structure and format of the hearing to the complainant, the ability to
              submit additional material 24 hours before the hearing, as well as whether the
              session is open or closed to the public and/or media. The Complainant may provide
              testimony when the complaint is heard.

          G.​The Board will issue a final decision after the hearing that PPS’s Step 1 decision is:

                i.​    Affirmed and no further action will be taken; or

                ii.​   Not affirmed in whole or in part and may direct the Superintendent to take
                       alternative steps or other courses of action.

          H.​With agreement from the Complainant, the Board may hold the complaint in
             abeyance until a certain date or event.

          I.​ PPS will make all reasonable attempts to schedule a special meeting to consider the
              appeal of the Division 22 complaint at a time that is convenient for the Complainant.
              If, after multiple attempts and at least 90 days have passed since the appeal was
              made, it has not been possible to coordinate a time for the meeting with the
              Complainant, the Board may meet to consider the appeal on the written materials
              submitted and will give notice to the Complainant.

          J.​ The Board’s final decision will be communicated to the Complainant in writing. If the
              complainant is not satisfied with the decision of the Board, the Complainant may file
              an appeal with the Oregon Department of Education (ODE) as permitted by OAR
              581-022-2370(4)(b).

VI.​   Other Provisions

          A.​PPS will provide translation and interpretation services and other reasonable
             accommodations to assist Complainants. Legal advice is not a resource provided by
             PPS.

          B.​The PPS representative assigning staff to investigate and respond to a complaint will
             avoid any conflicts of interest or the appearance of conflicts of interest.

          C.​Retaliation against any person who files or participates in a PPS complaint process is
             strictly prohibited. Retaliation is any intentional action that would deter a reasonable
             person from participating in the process. Anyone who believes they have suffered
             retaliation should immediately report it to the Superintendent or School Board.

          D.​PPS will share with Complainants as much information as possible about the findings
             of the investigation and will, in all cases, share the outcome of its investigation of
             complaints. However, PPS is often prohibited from disclosing specific information
             about disciplinary action taken against an employee or student involved in the
             complaint or other legally protected information.

          E.​The Board will hear appeals of complaints that involve confidential student records in
             a session closed to the public and media unless the student or family requests the
             appeal be heard in a session open to the public.

            F.​ As used in this policy, “days” will be counted as “calendar days.” Any period for
                response under this policy that falls on a weekend or legal holiday will be extended to
                the next business day.

            G.​PPS may not be able to assure confidentiality of the names of persons who file
               complaints under this policy.

            H.​If a complaint alleges employee misconduct that is outside the scope of this policy,
               the complaint coordinator will notify the Superintendent in writing of that filing, and
               PPS will endeavor to respond in a timely manner.

            I.​ Current and former employees may not bring a complaint under this policy regarding
                the terms, conditions, or status of their employment.

            J.​ Complaints that have been previously filed by a Complainant, investigated, and
                responded to and for which appeals have been exhausted or the time for appeal has
                expired, cannot be refiled when the complaint is regarding a particular incident or
                issue that has already been addressed.

VII.​    Complaints Against the Superintendent or Members of the Board of Education

         Any complaint about alleged individual misconduct by the Superintendent (but not PPS
         action for which the Superintendent may have ultimate responsibility as the chief executive
         officer of PPS) will be overseen by the Board of Education.

         Board members are volunteers serving in an elected capacity and are accountable to the
         citizens in the Portland Public Schools district for their actions and policy positions.
         Complaints alleging ethics violations or violations of the law against an individual Board
         member should be made to the Board Chair, who will refer these issues to appropriate
         governmental jurisdictions or a third party if a majority of the Board approves of either
         referral. Complaints related to ethics violations or violations of the law against the Board
         Chair should be made to the Board Vice-Chair(s) who will refer these issues to appropriate
         governmental jurisdictions or a third party if a majority of the Board approves of either
         referral. If a third party investigates a complaint, after receiving the results of the
         investigation, the Board will decide, within 30 days, in open session, what action, if any, is
         warranted.

VIII.​   Complaints Submitted to Members of the Board of Education

         School Board members who receive Division 22 complaints from constituents or staff will
         forward complaints to PPS’s Complaint Coordinator. Board members will forward all other
         complaints to the appropriate school or department in order to address the concern.

 IX.​    Further Appeal Rights

         If any complaint alleges a violation of Oregon Administrative Rules (OAR) Chapter 581,
         Division 22 (Standards), Oregon Revised Statutes (ORS) 339.285 to 339.303 or OAR
         581-021-0550 to 581-021-0570 (Restraint and Seclusion), or ORS 659.852 (Retaliation),
         and the complaint is not resolved through a PPS complaint process, the Complainant, who
         is a student, a parent or guardian of a student attending a school in PPS, or a person who
         resides in PPS district, may appeal a final decision by PPS to the Oregon Department of
         Education.

         If the complaint alleges discrimination pursuant to ORS 659.850 or OAR 581-021-0045 to
         581-021-0047 (Discrimination in Education), a Complainant may appeal a final decision by
         PPS to the Oregon Department of Education or may file a complaint directly with the U.S.
         Department of Education without having to exhaust PPS procedures under this policy.
