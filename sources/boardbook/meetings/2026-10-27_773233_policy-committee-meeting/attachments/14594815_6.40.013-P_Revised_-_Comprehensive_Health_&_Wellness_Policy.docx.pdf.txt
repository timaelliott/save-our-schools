                 Comprehensive Health & Wellness Policy
I.​ Background

        A.​ Portland Public Schools (PPS) is committed to creating safe and healthy learning
            environments to support the growth and development of every student. The
            development of health literacy- the ability to access, understand, appraise, apply,
            and advocate for health information and services to maintain or enhance one's health
            and the health of others- is necessary for students to be healthy throughout their
            lives.

        B.​ The Whole School, Whole Community, Whole Child (WSCC) model responds to the
            call for greater alignment, integration, and collaboration between education and
            health to improve each child’s cognitive, physical, social, and emotional
            development. It incorporates the components of a coordinated school health
            program, grounded in the tenets of a whole-child approach to education, and
            provides a framework for addressing the symbiotic relationship between learning and
            health.

        C.​ PPS will take a comprehensive approach to reviewing and incorporating changes in
            policy, curriculum, and operating procedures to promote lifelong healthy behaviors
            and sustainable wellness practices for all students and staff. Implementing this policy
            relies on the collaboration of instructional, operational, clinical, and administrative
            staff at schools and central office departments. The PPS Wellness Policy will
            encompass the following:

                    i.​ School Food and Nutrition Program

                   ii.​ Physical Education & Physical Activity

                   iii.​ Comprehensive Health Education

                   iv.​ Healthy School Environment

                   v.​ Safe and Supportive Environment

                   vi.​ Health Services

                  vii.​ Employee Wellness

        D.​ This Comprehensive Health and Wellness Policy, coupled with the associated
            Administrative Directives, will serve as overarching guidelines for each element of
            PPS’s Whole School, Whole Community, Whole Child model.

II.​ Policy

        A.​ PPS is committed to eliminating disparities in health and educational outcomes by
            ensuring equitable access to wellness supports, health education, nutritious food,
            physical activity, safe environments, and health services for all students. To that end,
            the Superintendent will appoint a District Wellness Council, sometimes known as a

B.​ Family Engagement & Community Involvement

      i.​     PPS will actively communicate ways in which the community can participate
              in the development, implementation, periodic review, and update of the
              District Wellness Policy and Action Plan through a variety of means
              appropriate for PPS. One of these methods will include community
              participation in the School Health Advisory Council. PPS will also ensure that
              communications are culturally and linguistically appropriate for the community
              and delivered through the same communication channels used by PPS
              central office and schools.

C.​ School Food & Nutrition Program

      i.​     Nutrition promotion and education positively influence lifelong eating
              behaviors by using evidence-informed techniques and nutrition messages,
              creating food environments that encourage healthy nutrition choices, and
              encouraging participation in school meal programs. Students and staff will
              receive consistent nutrition messages throughout the school environment.

     ii.​     Integral aspects of School Food & Nutrition Programs include, but are not
              limited to: school cafeteria and meal expectations, staff qualifications and
              professional development, competitive foods and beverages, celebrations
              and rewards, fundraising, and food and beverage marketing in schools.

D.​ Physical Education & Physical Activity

      i.​    Physical activity opportunities will be incorporated throughout the school
             experience for students in grades pre-K–12 and may include
             standards-based physical education, recess, classroom movement activities,
             and co-curricular opportunities. PPS will not withhold physical activity as
             punishment for any reason. OAR 581-022-2263 sets physical education
             requirements, and PPS will fulfill them accordingly.

E.​ Comprehensive Health Education

      i.​    PPS requires all students in grades K–12 to receive comprehensive health
             education that is medically accurate, age- and developmentally appropriate,
             culturally and linguistically sustaining, inclusive of LGBTQ+ individuals and
             people of all abilities, and delivered in safe and supportive learning
             environments where all students feel valued. All PPS schools will take a
             skills-based approach to teach comprehensive health education that
             addresses a variety of topics, such as tobacco, alcohol, and other drug
             prevention and intervention, healthy eating/nutrition, mental and emotional
             health, personal health and wellness, physical activity, safety and injury
             prevention, violence prevention, and comprehensive sexual health education
             that is LGBTQ+ inclusive.

     ii.​    Per state law, all schools must provide all students with standards-based
             comprehensive health education (HE).

              a.​ Schools serving grades K-8 must provide at least 9 weeks of
                  standards-based comprehensive health education annually. All lessons
                  must be aligned with all relevant state laws, mandates, and standards.

              b.​ All high school students must complete two semesters of
                  standards-based comprehensive health education to graduate.

              c.​ In grades 6-12, a licensed and endorsed health education teacher will
                  teach comprehensive health education.

F.​ Healthy School Environment

      i.​    PPS is committed to maintaining school facilities that are safe, clean,
             sanitary, accessible, and conducive to learning. PPS will maintain buildings
             and grounds in accordance with applicable health, safety, environmental, and
             accessibility standards.

     ii.​    PPS recognizes that healthy physical environments are critical to preventing
             asthma and other chronic and infectious diseases that affect learning. PPS is
             committed to providing school buildings and grounds that are clean and in
             good repair, with healthy indoor air and water quality, sanitary and accessible
             bathrooms, and efficient resource use. PPS departments and all schools will

G.​ Safe and Supportive Environment

      i.​    PPS recognizes that suicide is a leading cause of death among youth and
             that many more youth consider and attempt suicide. The possibility of suicide
             and suicidal ideation requires vigilant attention from all PPS staff. As a result,
             PPS is committed to implementing best practices in suicide prevention,
             intervention, and postvention strategies to reduce risk, increase help-seeking
             behavior, identify students in need of support, and prevent suicide attempts
             and deaths. PPS also prioritizes creating safe, inclusive, and nurturing school
             environments grounded in belonging, emotional safety, and school
             connectedness, where all students feel valued and supported.

     ii.​    In addition to suicide prevention efforts, PPS is committed to fostering school
             climates that are trauma-informed, restorative, and free from bias,
             discrimination, and harassment. These approaches support positive
             relationships, strengthen student resilience, and promote equitable access to
             learning. PPS recognizes that students’ emotional well-being is directly
             connected to attendance, engagement, and academic success, and therefore
             emphasizes practices that build strong relationships, reinforce protective
             factors, and promote students’ sense of belonging and self-worth.

H.​ Health Services

      i.​    PPS aims to provide comprehensive, prevention-oriented health services to
             support all students' well-being and academic success. This policy guides
             schools’ health service delivery and communicable disease management to
             remove barriers to learning, improve school attendance, increase health
             literacy, reduce risky behaviors, and promote healthy lifestyle choices. Health
             Services includes school nursing, medication administration and
             management, chronic disease management, preventive screenings,
             emergency preparedness and response, and care coordination to support
             students’ physical, mental, and emotional health needs.

     ii.​    The goal of Health Services is to ensure the health and safety of all students
             at each PPS-owned or leased facility where students are present for regular
             programming, to promote healthy school environments, and to provide
             education and access to effective health services. PPS also recognizes the
             importance of partnerships with families, community providers, and
             behavioral health organizations to support student wellness, early
             intervention, and access to care
