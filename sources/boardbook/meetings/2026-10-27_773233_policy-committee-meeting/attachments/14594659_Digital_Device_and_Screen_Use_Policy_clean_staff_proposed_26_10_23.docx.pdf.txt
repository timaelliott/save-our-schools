                Digital Device and Screen Use Policy
I. PURPOSE

 This Policy establishes rules for the evaluation, deployment, and use of digital technologies
 and screen-based devices within Portland Public Schools. It supports PPS’s Graduate Portrait
 and Portland Public Schools’ mission to provide an equitable, high-quality education by
 safeguarding students' physical, social-emotional, and cognitive development, and by
 ensuring that adopted technologies enhance, rather than diminish, learning outcomes and
 instructional time.

 Through clear, consistent, age-appropriate expectations, this Policy reflects a shared
 responsibility among schools, families, and students to promote academic focus, critical
 thinking, and healthy, lifelong learning habits, preparing students to be responsible digital
 citizens throughout their school years and beyond.

II. DEFINITIONS

      A. Screen Time: Screen Time refers to the amount of time a student is expected to view
         or interact with Device displays, including structured educational technology used for
         instruction and unstructured or non-instructional media. Screen Time does not include
         use of assistive technology required to provide a student with access to instruction or
         PPS services.

      B. Device: As used in this policy, a Device means any PPS-owned, PPS-issued, or
         PPS-managed laptop, desktop computer, tablet, mobile phone, wearable device,
         virtual or augmented reality equipment, or other internet-connected hardware used to
         access PPS systems or instructional content.

      C. Educational Technology: Educational Technology means software, digital
         applications, online services, websites, platforms, and other digital tools used for
         instructional, assessment, accessibility, safety, communication, or administrative
         purposes.

      D. Approved Educational Technology: Approved Educational Technology means
         Educational Technology that PPS has reviewed and authorized under its educational
         technology approval process.

III. PRINCIPLES FOR SCREEN USE IN INSTRUCTION

      A. Screen Value: Digital tools are implemented to support collaboration, creativity and
         curiosity with age-appropriate technology using learning science.

      B. Multi-Sensory Learning Priority. Screens shall be used only for specific, approved
         purposes and shall not replace hands-on, experiential learning.

      C. Paper-Based Materials. Paper materials shall be provided for reading, writing, and
         math instruction and assignments.

   The following rules shall apply to all digital device and screen use, except as dictated by

   A.​Screen-free PK. Individual screen use shall be prohibited for students in
      Pre-Kindergarten, except as required by state law.

   B.​Centralized Device Use for 3-8. Devices will be used to support specific in-class
      curriculum and learning activities and will not serve as the primary instructional
      medium, unless the student is participating in an online school or class approved or
      delivered by PPS. PPS will not operate a standard one-to-one take-home Device
      program for students in Pre-K through Grade 8.

      Students may take PPS Devices home only to address an identified instructional,
      accessibility, equity, or academic-support need, as approved by the principal or
      designee, or as required by an individualized education program (“IEP”), Section 504
      plan, or applicable law. PPS will provide the student’s parent or guardian with notice
      of the take-home arrangement and obtain an acknowledgment of applicable
      Device-use requirements. Failure to return an acknowledgment will not prevent PPS
      from providing a Device when the Device is required to provide the student with equal
      access, appropriate services, or legally required accommodations.

   C.​K-8 Screen-Free Periods. Recess, lunch, and passing periods will be screen- and
      device-free for grades K-8. Digital device and media use by students during these
      times, including movies, videos, gaming, or streaming content, may be allowed
      with administrative approval.

   D.​Screen-Free Lunch for Grade 9-12. The use of computers during high school lunch
      periods will be restricted to specific classrooms or library areas specifically
      designated as study halls.

   E.​Instructional Flexibility. PPS recognizes that some courses and learning
      experiences may require greater use of technology to achieve their instructional
      objectives. Screen time guidance should be applied with consideration for the unique
      requirements of courses such as computer science, graphic design, digital media
      arts, yearbook, engineering, career technical education pathways, film studies, and
      other technology-intensive programs. In these courses and programs, technology use
      may exceed general screen time recommendations when it is directly connected to
      curriculum, student creation, design, programming, production, research, or other
      active learning experiences. Such use should remain purposeful, instructionally
      aligned, and consistent with the goals of this policy.

   F.​ Offline Homework. Homework assignments will be offered in a paper format
       whenever possible. Internet connectivity will not be required for the completion of
       homework assignments. This does not prohibit the use of a learning management
       system in grades 6-12 where “creation tools” (word processing, digital tools for
       production of learning) are in alignment with the focus of the graduate portrait.

   G.​Use Only PPS-approved Digital Tools and Apps. PPS-approved digital tools/apps
      will be vetted and approved to ensure they are safe, effective, and legal. Use of PPS
      teacher/student accounts to sign up for online accounts, digital tools, or apps outside
      of this process is prohibited. When students are using devices, all teachers, including
      substitutes, will utilize PPS-approved monitoring tools to monitor student screen use
      for appropriateness.

   H.​Prohibition on Advertisement and Sales. No advertisements will appear on any
      computer device or projected screen at school. Any device used at school, including
      those used by teachers or administrators, must be installed with premium ad

        website that pushes “paid upgrades,” “premium services,” “in-app purchases,” or

     I.​ Educational Use of Video, Visual Media, and Other Content. PPS devices will only
         be used for curriculum-aligned educational purposes that promote active student
         engagement during the school day. Video and device use should not be used as a
         substitute for teacher-led instruction, classroom engagement, or active learning
         experiences, and should be followed by opportunities for discussion, reflection,
         application, or other forms of student interaction. Non-educational use, including
         passive consumption for entertainment or video streaming, will be prohibited and
         blocked without administrator approval. Background video or visual media not aligned
         to active instruction should not be used. When not in active use, devices and
         projection screens should be off and away. Exceptions for specific disability related
         needs as outlined in an IEP or 504 plan are allowed.

     J.​ Gamified Learning Applications. Gamified learning applications for academic
         subjects will be prohibited. For purposes of this Policy, "gamified learning
         applications" means educational apps/software that rely on extrinsic
         game mechanics—including but not limited to points, badges, levels, leaderboards, or
         virtual rewards—for student engagement with academic content.

     K.​Prohibition on Screen-Time as Reward. Individual digital device use or screen time
        will not be used as a reward, incentive, or behavioral reinforcement for students.

     L.​ Platform and Content Restrictions. PPS will filter Internet content in alignment with
         PPS policy. Staff will be provided classroom management tools that have the
         capability to block Internet websites. This should effectively restrict access to
         platforms and websites primarily designed for entertainment or passive consumption.

        PPS will allow teacher-led use of YouTube for instructional purposes. PPS will block
        all other non-approved streaming platforms and non-approved gaming platforms.
        Platforms will only be permitted for educator-directed instructional use when they
        directly support curriculum-aligned learning objectives and follows an administrator
        approval process.

        Staff may not direct students to create accounts, disclose personal information,
        upload student work, or use an educational technology service unless the service has
        been approved through the PPS educational technology review process. Staff may
        not accept click-though terms on behalf of PPS or use free applications, browser
        extensions, pilot programs, or artificial intelligence tools unless authorized under that
        process.

  and federal requirements, Individualized Education Plans (IEPs), 504 plans, or Personalized
  Education Plans (PEPs). The minutes below should be considered as a balance over the
  course of a unit of instruction to allow for flexibility.

     A. Screen-free PK. Individual screen use will be prohibited for students in
        Pre-Kindergarten, except as required by state law.

     B. Grades K-8:

            i.​   Kindergarten: up to 45 minutes/week

     C.​Exceptions: Exceptions to the time limitations set forth in this Section may be
        granted under the following circumstances: (i) IEPs, 504 plans, or PEPs*; (ii)
        emergency situations requiring immediate communication or safety measures; (iii)
        temporary, unexpected teacher substitutions where prepared lesson plans
        necessitate screen-based instruction; (iv) special assemblies featuring guest
        speakers or presentations that require audiovisual components; (v) other special
        circumstances approved in advance by the principal.

        *Online curriculum should not be used for specialized designed instruction (SDI) or
        modified curriculum unless there is a specific IEP goal that is being addressed.

        other technology-intensive programs. In these courses and programs, technology use
        may exceed general screen time recommendations when it is directly connected to
        curriculum, student creation, design, programming, production, research, or other
        active learning experiences. Such use should remain purposeful, instructionally
        aligned, and consistent with the goals of this policy.

VI. PROHIBITION OF ONE-TO-ONE DEVICES

     A. Prohibition on One-to-One Devices. PPS will not issue individual devices to K-8
        students. All student access to digital devices will be limited to shared devices in
        dedicated computer labs or supervised computer carts as set forth in Section IV of
        this Policy, except when indicated by a student’s IEP or 504.

     B. Availability of “Home only” Devices for Optional Check-Out. PPS will establish an
        “Opt-In” system where families may choose to receive a PPS-issued device for home
        use only, rather than requiring devices to travel between school and home daily. This
        will be on an as-needed basis, with written permission from a parent or guardian, for
        school-related work or access to online services. This will ensure equity in at-home
        access to computers and online services for all students, regardless of
        socioeconomic status.

     C. Exceptions. Exceptions to this prohibition may be granted when the student has a
        documented need requiring individual device access, such as students under 504,
        PEPs, or Individualized Education Programs (IEPs), where use of assistive devices is
        explicitly required.

VII. DIGITAL LITERACY

   PPS will integrate developmentally appropriate Digital Literacy instruction into the core
   curriculum for grades K–12. This instruction will focus on:

      A.​ Evaluating source credibility and detecting misinformation;

      B.​ Understanding data privacy and the Responsible Use Policy;

      C.​ The environmental and social impacts of digital technologies;

      D.​ Safe and respectful use of digital tools;

      E.​ Skills to critically evaluate digital content;

      F.​ Developing critical thinking, creativity, communication, collaboration, empathy,
          judgment, and independent problem-solving skills in a rapidly evolving technological
          world; and

      G.​ Maintaining healthy technology habits, including attention management, period
          breaks, ergonomics, and awareness of persuasive or engagement-focused design.

VIII. PARENT AND GUARDIAN RIGHTS

   Parents and legal guardians receive annual information about for their children’s use of
   EdTech at school, including for the use of a school issued device and apps listed in the PPS
   digital toolkit.

   The information will include: information regarding their child's use of digital tools, including
   estimated/actual total weekly time their child spends on a device; a complete list of apps,
   programs, Google Workspace Services; and information as to how those apps, programs,
   and websites were vetted to ensure they meet the standards and guidelines to have been
   deemed appropriate for educational and instruction use.

IX. DIGITAL TOOL VETTING AND APPROVAL PROCESS

   PPS, in collaboration with the Technology Advisory Committee, will review and update the
   PPS Digital Technology and App Approval Process with a formal, transparent rubric to
   evaluate ed tech and digital tools. The rubric will consider targeted learning objectives,
   cognitive processes, student data privacy and safety, independent efficacy research, and
   time-bound requirements, adopting new student-facing digital products only when there are
   proven benefits over analog alternatives, and will include a process for teachers and staff to
   request approval for a new tool or app.

X. TECHNOLOGY AUDIT PROCESS

      A. Regular Technology Audit. PPS will conduct annual audits of all digital tools and
         technologies currently in use across all grade levels and programs. These audits will
         evaluate: (i) continued compliance with approval criteria; (ii) actual learning outcomes
         achieved; (iii) any reported adverse effects on student privacy, learning, physical and
         mental health; (iv) budget impacts. The Technology Advisory Committee (discussed
         below) will review audit findings and make recommendations.

      B. Removal of Tools. Any digital tool found to not meet approval criteria will be removed
         from use within ninety (90) days of such determination.

XI. ONGOING PROFESSIONAL SUPPORT

  Educators will be provided training on the use of digital devices and platforms.

XII. STAKEHOLDER COLLABORATION

     A. Technology Advisory Committee. PPS will maintain a Technology Advisory
        Committee that ideally includes technology personnel, educators, administrators,
        psychologists, students, and parents. The Committee will advise how technology is
        implemented throughout PPS, evaluating impacts on the learning and well-being of
        students and the community.

     B. Committee Responsibilities. The Technology Advisory Committee will: (i) work with
        PPS to develop a digital tool rubric (ii) review and recommend updates to this Policy
        annually; (iii) evaluate proposed new technologies prior to adoption; (iv) monitor
        research and developments in educational technology and child health; (iv) collect
        information from parents, faculty, and staff regarding technology use; (v) evaluate
        outcomes of digital literacy curriculum and recommend changes as needed and (vi)
        report findings and recommendations to the school board as part of the District
        Annual Report.

XIII. MONITORING, ACCOUNTABILITY, AND CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT

     A. Policy Review. The Technology Advisory Committee will review this Policy annually
        and make suggestions to the board to update as necessary based on current
        research, stakeholder feedback, and operational experience. The policy will be
        updated as needed to reflect new research, implementation feedback, equity
        analyses, and emerging tools and information. The first annual review will include
        feedback from students, educators, administrators, staff, parents, and families
        regarding policy implementation and data on screen time usage.

     B. District Annual Report to the Board. PPS will provide an annual report to the
        Board, presented no later than August, detailing implementation of this Policy. The
        report will include, at minimum:

            i.​    Screen Time Data. A summary of average student screen time across PPS,
                   compared against the goals and benchmarks established under this Policy.

           ii.​    Digital Tools and Platforms in Use. A current inventory of approved
                   applications, digital tools, and commonly used websites utilized for
                   instructional purposes and associated costs. Data on learning outcomes for
                   each tool should be presented as well.

           iii.​   Digital and AI Literacy Curriculum. An update on the implementation and
                   status of digital and artificial intelligence literacy curricula, including progress
                   made, instructional outcomes, and consistency of implementation across
                   schools.

           iv.​    Data Privacy Incidents. A summary of any student data privacy issues or
                   incidents that arose during the school year, including the nature of each issue
                   and the steps taken to address or mitigate it.

           v.​     Monitoring and Filtering Systems. An update on the effectiveness of device
                   monitoring and content-filtering systems, including efforts to ensure device use
                   remains educational, minimizes access to distracting or non-instructional
                   content, and maintains a safe digital environment for students. Include filtering
                   mishaps and mitigation strategies.
