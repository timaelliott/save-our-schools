        Methodology
        How Portland Public Schools developed the proposed changes:
        the data behind the model, the criteria the district weighed, and
        how those criteria were applied region by region.




 Overview

 The proposed changes were developed by district leaders and district planning staff, guided
 by a set of criteria that reflect what the district is trying to achieve: schools large enough to
 offer stable, adequately resourced programming, buildings used well, and school
 communities kept together through change as much as possible.


 These criteria often pull in different directions. Keeping a school community together may
 require a larger boundary change. Consolidating a small immersion program may lengthen
 some students' trips. For that reason, the criteria were weighed against one another rather
 than applied as a fixed ranking, and the balance struck differs from one part of the city to
 another, because conditions differ.


 Two scenarios were developed, which differ mainly in how many schools they close. A
 scenario that closes fewer schools affects fewer families and changes fewer boundaries, but
 leaves more schools below the enrollment at which a school can offer stable, fully resourced
 programming. A scenario that closes more schools does the reverse.

  Applying Criteria

  The data and modeling team included all schools and programs across the district. As
  changes were modeled they were considered by region. Each region was formed based on
  two adjacent high school attendance areas. The four regions include Ida B. Wells and
  Lincoln on the west side, Jefferson and Roosevelt in the north, Grant and MacDaniel in the
  northeast, and Cleveland and Franklin in the southeast. The primary drivers of scenario
  development were to stabilize elementaries and middle schools, establish clear dual
  language immersion pathways, and reduce the number of split feeder patterns. Each of
  those schools was then compared with other schools in the same region using the criteria
  above. The result is a set of recommendations shaped not only by how a school's current
  enrollment compares to its sustainable threshold, but by the full set of priorities the district
  committed to weighing.



      HELD CONSTANT IN THE MODEL
      Factors that remained the same throughout the modeling: number of
      kindergarten seats available for dual language immersion, languages offered in dual
      language immersion programs, seats available at ACCESS, Benson, da Vinci, and
      Winterhaven, and schools or programs with specific entrance requirements are not
      included such as DART.




  Scenario Enrollment Assumptions

  In each scenario the implementation begins with the 2027-2028 school year and ends with
  all changes being completed by the beginning of the 2028-2029 school year. The scenarios
  assume the PSU projection of students will be enrolled in PPS during all years.
