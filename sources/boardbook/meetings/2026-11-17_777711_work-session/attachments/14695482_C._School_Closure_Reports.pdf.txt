1a. School Description
Beach Elementary School is a K–5 school offering English neighborhood classes and Spanish
Dual Language Immersion in the Overlook neighborhood. It emphasizes culturally aﬃrming,
rigorous instruction in a nurturing and inclusive environment. Beach was built in 1928 and the
campus consists of a two-story school building with a ground ﬂoor basement, a detached
classroom building, and an L-shaped portable. Approximately 280 students are enrolled at
Beach for the 2026-2027 school year.

1b. Financial Impact
The primary ﬁnancial beneﬁt of consolidating elementary schools and programs is to align
school capacity and operating costs with enrollment, allowing PPS to concentrate limited
resources in fewer, more eﬃciently resourced schools. Combining school communities can
reduce duplicative school-level administrative and operating expenses, improve building
utilization, and support more eﬃcient staﬃng and program delivery. These consolidations are
intended to generate ongoing savings while supporting strong core instructional programs and
student services.

If the school remains open, the projected cost per student for the 2027-2028 school year is:
$17,196.

If the school is closed in 2027, the projected overall savings is $957,658. This includes savings
from administrative, custodial, and oﬃce staﬃng, 80% of utilities, and includes the one-time
cost of teacher moves. We anticipate ongoing expenses, including 20% of current utility costs
and an average annual security cost, if the school were closed and unoccupied.

Under the proposed change, 40% of students would live more than one mile from Chief Joseph
and would be offered school bus transportation. To accommodate all eligible students, PPS
anticipates maintaining service to Chief Joseph with three buses. The Transportation
Department estimates an additional savings of approximately $105,000. The proposed change
would reduce the total number of buses serving the closing and receiving schools, resulting in
transportation cost savings.

Transportation and Safe Routes to School planning will identify needed supports for safe arrival
and dismissal, including walking and biking improvements, crossing supports, or traﬃc
circulation changes.

3. Community/Cluster and District Impact Analysis

3a. Cluster Analysis

How will the proposed closure maintain or improve equity of access to schools offering high
quality educational choices?
In the 2026-27 school year Portland Public Schools staffs and supports 56 elementary
programs across the district. Eleven of these programs are situated within K-8 campuses. The
enrollment of these programs ranges from 160 students to over 550 students. Using the QEM
and our system definition of “well-resourced schools” as a guide, we have established an
elementary enrollment of 360 as the minimum threshold to be able to staff and support a
comprehensive and stable elementary program. Maximizing the number of sites at or above this
minimum threshold allows the district to maximize the number of students who experience
grade-level classrooms, comprehensive core enrichments, and targeted academic supports. It
also maximizes the number of educators who work full-time on one campus and who benefit
from regular collaboration with grade- and content-alike colleagues.

By operating fewer elementary schools and spreading current enrollment more evenly across
remaining sites, the district can stabilize and offer a more comprehensive and sustainable
program at each elementary site.

In recent years, we have used resources from other parts of our system to subsidize elementary
programs with low enrollment. Given our current and projected budget shortfalls, we can no
longer sustain subsidies for underenrolled sites. As a result, elementary schools have already
faced several programmatic and operational constraints:
    ●​ Over half of our elementary programs have been forced to place students in multi-grade
        classrooms;
    ●​ Staffing for instructional coaching, academic coaching, and school climate support
        positions have been reduced;
    ●​ Core enrichment teachers that support Arts, PE, and Library/Media instruction have been
        split across multiple sites, reducing the capacity to have full-school enrichment
        programs;

Beach Elementary School​ ​   ​      ​      ​      ​       ​      ​            ​     4
    ●​ Support staff that help monitor lunch and recess have been reduced, forcing principals
       to spend much of their time monitoring duty;
    ●​ Opportunities for strong teacher collaboration and professional learning have been
       compromised by multi-grade classrooms and single-strand programs.

If we continue with no rightsizing action, all of these constraints will be compounded in the
2027-2028 school years.

3b. Effects on Other Schools, Enrollment, and Community Access
The proposed merger would affect Chief Joseph Elementary School within the Roosevelt
cluster. Enrollment at Chief Joseph is projected to change from 242 to 440 in the 2027–2028
school year. Based on current enrollment projections, there will be two to four classrooms at
each grade-level, with blends not required. These changes would support a combined
neighborhood elementary school community, with opportunities to stabilize staffing and sustain
educational programs and student supports. Implementation would require coordinated
staffing, classroom assignments, and use of shared spaces to accommodate incoming
students while supporting students already enrolled. Chief Joseph is ready to receive new
students with minimal kitchen upgrades needed, sufficient instructional and student support
spaces, and coordination of school-based partnerships and services.

Beach is located approximately 1.3 miles from Chief Joseph. Nearby community resources
include Arbor Lodge Park and its accessible Harper’s Playground. The proposed change would
shift neighborhood students’ daily school activities toward Chief Joseph and Arbor Lodge Park.
Transportation options include walking, biking, family transportation, and school bus service for
eligible students. PPS would support school bus service for students who live more than one
mile from the school and work with the school community and the district Safe Routes to
School coordinator to identify appropriate crossing, pedestrian, and bicycle safety measures.

3c. Community Partnerships and Potential Community Impact
Beach does not have Racial Equity & Social Justice (RESJ) partner programming. Closure is not
expected to affect RESJ partner services. PPS will continue working with building leaders and
school communities at receiving schools so that partnerships reflect what students and
families need. Future community use of the facility would be considered through the Building
Repurposing process

Beach partners with the YMCA to provide child care for approximately 42 students. Relocating
students to Chief Joseph and César Chávez will require a clear plan to maintain access to care.
Chief Joseph currently partners with Neighborhood House, serving approximately 45 students,
while César Chávez transports approximately 10–15 students to Astor for child care. We ask
that the YMCA, Neighborhood House, Chief Joseph, César Chávez, and Astor be included in
early transition planning to determine how many students will need care at each location,
assess available space and program capacity, and explore opportunities to continue existing
provider relationships. This should include evaluating whether Neighborhood House can
accommodate additional students, whether César Chávez can offer on-site child care, and
whether transportation to Astor can support increased demand. Families need reliable child
care options, clear communication, and minimal disruption to established caregiver
relationships and routines. A successful transition requires ensuring that all Beach students


Beach Elementary School​ ​    ​      ​       ​       ​      ​       ​            ​      5
who need care have access to safe, dependable, and appropriately supported child care
arrangements.

Beach partners with Self Enhancement, Inc. (SEI) to operate its SUN Community School,
providing community-based services and wraparound family supports through collaboration
among PPS, Multnomah County, and the City of Portland. SUN provides free extended-day
enrichment opportunities, quality instruction and extracurricular activities, access to food
pantries and other basic-needs resources, and culturally relevant programming supported by
staff who reflect and understand the student population. Through SUN, students and families
also have connections to food, housing, utility assistance, health and mental health services,
family activities, and other community resources. SUN serves as an important point of
connection for families seeking individualized support, enrichment opportunities, and
assistance navigating services beyond the school day. Closure could disrupt students' access to
established relationships with SEI staff, after-school programming, enrichment providers, and
basic-needs resources, while requiring families to navigate new service locations and referral
processes. These changes may be particularly significant for families who rely on SUN as an
accessible connection to food assistance, housing and utility resources, and other wraparound
supports. Opportunities for partnerships to continue include transitioning students to Chief
Joseph and César Chávez. César Chávez operates a SUN Community School through Latino
Network, creating an opportunity for students to continue accessing SUN services, although the
transition would involve a change in lead agency from SEI to Latino Network. This change may
require families to establish new relationships with SUN staff and navigate different community
partnerships, programming, and family-support coordination. Chief Joseph does not operate a
SUN Community School, meaning students transitioning there would not have equivalent access
to the established SUN service system at their receiving school.

Services requiring further coordination include assessing César Chávez's capacity to
accommodate additional students and service needs, identifying which existing SEI
partnerships and enrichment opportunities could continue, coordinating the transfer of family
referrals and individualized support needs, and identifying alternative providers or referral
pathways for students transitioning to Chief Joseph. Particular attention should be given to
maintaining access to food assistance, housing and utility resources, health and mental health
referrals, and other supports that families may currently access through SUN. Additional
community impacts include the potential loss of a trusted neighborhood connection point for
families, reduced access to free after-school enrichment and wraparound services for students
attending Chief Joseph, and disruption to established relationships with SEI staff and
community partners. Proposed responses include coordinating with SEI, Latino Network, and
receiving-school leadership to develop a transition plan, establish warm handoffs for students
and families moving to César Chávez, identify alternative community-based resources for
families transitioning to Chief Joseph, assess staffing and service capacity, and communicate
changes in programming and resource access.

Any future community use of the facility would be considered through the Building Repurposing
process.

3c. Community Partnerships and Potential Community Impact
Buckman currently partners with Friends of the Children to provide long-term mentoring for
students. Closure is not expected to interrupt this service. Friends of the Children has a
long-term commitment to each child regardless of school placement, and mentors (referred to
as Friends) continue supporting students when they transition to another school. These
services are provided at no cost to the District. No Racial Equity & Social Justice (RESJ) partner

licensing, staffing, and operational capacity. We ask that Champions, the YMCA, Art 4 Life, and
both receiving school administrations be included in early transition planning to determine how
students will be divided, ensure sufficient appropriately licensed space, and minimize
disruptions to existing programs and families. Clear communication about enrollment, program
locations, transportation, and timelines will also be essential. A successful transition requires
collaboration and adequate resources to accommodate Buckman families while preserving the
services and partnerships already established at Sunnyside and Abernethy.

community-based services and wraparound family supports through collaboration among PPS,
Multnomah County, and the City of Portland. SUN provides free extended-day enrichment

Woodmere is located approximately 0.7 mile from Whitman Elementary School. Nearby
community resources include Flavel Park and Brentwood-Darlington Community Center, with
Holgate Library serving the broader area. The proposed change would shift students’ daily
school activities toward Whitman and Flavel Park. Transportation options include walking,
biking, family transportation, and school bus service for eligible students. Potential barriers
include longer walking distances and crossings of SE Flavel Street and other busy streets,
depending on students’ home addresses. PPS would support school bus service for students
who live more than one mile from the school and work with the school community and the
district Safe Routes to School coordinator to identify appropriate crossing, pedestrian, and
bicycle safety measures.

3c. Community Partnerships and Potential Community Impact
Woodmere does not have Racial Equity & Social Justice (RESJ) partner programming. Closure is
not expected to affect RESJ partner services. PPS will continue working with building leaders
and school communities at receiving schools so that partnerships reflect what students and
families need. Future community use of the facility would be considered through the Building
Repurposing process.

Woodmere does not partner with a child care provider, as there has not been an identified need
for on-site child care within its school community. Therefore, closure would not directly affect
an existing child care partnership or require the relocation of a program. However, community
needs may change over time, and we remain open to reassessing family demand and exploring
partnerships with qualified child care providers if a need emerges. Any future program would
require consideration of available space, licensing, staffing, and operational resources. We
encourage the district to remain open to future opportunities to establish on-site child care if
demand increases, ensuring that students and families have access to this important resource
when needed.

Woodmere partners with Impact NW to operate its SUN Community School, providing
community-based services and wraparound family supports through collaboration among PPS,
Multnomah County, and the City of Portland. SUN provides free extended-day enrichment
opportunities, access to food resources, educational support, family engagement opportunities,

and connections to social, health, and mental health resources. Additional services specific to
Woodmere include a weekly family food backpack program providing groceries for entire

access educational enrichment, food assistance, family support, and community resources.
Closure could affect student and family access to these services, disrupt established
relationships with SUN staff and community partners, and require adjustments to programming
locations and service delivery.

Opportunities for partnerships to continue include transitioning students and services to
Whitman, which also operates an Impact NW-led SUN Community School. Having the same
community-based organization operating SUN at both schools creates an opportunity to
maintain service continuity, preserve established relationships, coordinate family transitions,
and potentially integrate existing programming and partnerships into Whitman's service delivery
model.

Services requiring further coordination include assessing Whitman's capacity to accommodate
additional students and families, maintaining access to food assistance and the family
backpack program, coordinating family engagement and resource navigation supports, and
identifying opportunities to continue existing enrichment programming and community
partnerships.

Additional community impacts include potential disruptions to food access, family engagement,
and trusted relationships with school-based support staff, as well as increased demand on
Whitman's existing SUN resources. Proposed responses include coordinating with Impact NW
and Whitman school leadership to develop a transition plan, assess staffing and program
capacity, identify opportunities to maintain Woodmere's existing services and partnerships,
transfer family referrals and support needs, and communicate alternative access points for
families relying on SUN resources. Particular consideration should be given to maintaining
access to food assistance and ensuring families experience a seamless transition into
Whitman's existing community-based service system. Future community use of the facility
would be considered through the Building Repurposing process.
