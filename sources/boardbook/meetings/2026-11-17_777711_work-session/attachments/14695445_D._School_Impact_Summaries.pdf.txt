Abernethy E.S.                                                                   Changes beginning in
                                                                                      2027-28




    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Abernethy would remain open as a neighborhood K-5 school.
     ●   Abernethy would welcome the Buckman student population who live south of
         Burnside Street.
     ●   Buckman neighborhood students who live south of Burnside St. would move to Abernethy
         ES.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Enrollment is dropping: Abernethy can hold 441 students, but enrollment is expected to drop to
    241 without changes.
●   Filling open space: Bringing Buckman and Abernethy student communities together supports a
    stable size and keeps student enrollment numbers steady.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Abernethy was chosen to stay open because the Buckman building needs urgent, major structural
    repairs.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   More support and programs: Steady enrollment numbers support stable staffing for classrooms
    and programs.
●   On-site childcare: Families would continue to have access to reliable before- and after-school
    childcare

REINVESTMENTS FOR RECEIVING SCHOOLS
Schools that receive additional students through consolidation will be allocated a proportion of the total programmatic
savings that result from Rightsizing actions. This allocation will be distributed as discretionary FTE that can be used for
instructional coaching, academic intervention, school climate support, social work, or duty support. School principals, with
consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.


  School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
  includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
  districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
  others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
  December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.

Arleta E.S.                                                                       Changes beginning in
                                                                                       2028-29




    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Arleta would remain open as a neighborhood K-5 school.
     ●   Arleta would welcome the entire neighborhood school area and student body from
         Marysville.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Arleta chosen for building size: While building conditions at Arleta and Marysville were similar,
    Marysville was too small to fit both school communities together.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Large school space: Arleta has 28 regular classrooms and can comfortably hold up to 653 students.
    This proposal keeps the enrollment well above current enrollment but well below the most
    students the building can hold.
●   Major building upgrades: Arleta is getting a brand-new heating and cooling (HVAC) system in
    Summer 2027 and has a full kitchen setup.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Upgraded learning spaces: Students would gain modern air quality and heating, and large spaces
    for learning and play.
●   Stronger school programs: Combining communities allows the school to offer programs that can
    continue over time and support student services.

Atkinson E.S.                                                                    Changes beginning in
                                                                                      2027-28




    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Atkinson would remain open as a neighborhood K-5 school.
     ●   Atkinson would welcome neighborhood K-5 students from Creston.
     ●   The Spanish Dual Language Immersion (DLI) program would move to Lent K-5.
     ●   Middle school pathway: All students in the new Atkinson boundary would go together to
         Kellogg for middle school and continue to Franklin for high school.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Creating space for neighborhood students: Moving Spanish DLI to Lent creates the physical space
    needed at Atkinson for the Atkinson and Creston student communities.
●   DLI Schools serving each area: Spanish DLI is moved into schools serving each area of the district.
    For southeast Portland, this is Lent.
●   Closer to Kellogg: Creston students north of Powell live closer to Atkinson and Kellogg Middle
    School.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Atkinson's central location: Atkinson was chosen to stay open over Creston because it is larger and
    more central to where families live in the new boundary.
●   Lent DLI school serving the area: Lent was chosen as the DLI school serving the area in the
    Southeast because it already functions as a full immersion school and families accessing Spanish
    DLI in this part of town live closer to Lent than Atkinson.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Safer, modern classrooms: Atkinson provides upgraded learning spaces.
●   Concentrated DLI program: Moving Spanish DLI to Lent builds a focused language center with full
    teacher collaboration.
●   Smooth middle and high school moves: Clear pathways keep friend groups together from
    elementary all the way through Franklin High School.

Beaumont M.S.                                                                    Changes beginning in
                                                                                      2027-28




    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Beaumont would remain open as a neighborhood middle school (grades 6-8).
     ●   Beaumont would welcome 6th-8th grade students from Beverly Cleary, including students
         from the former Irvington boundary.
     ●   The Spanish Dual Language Immersion (DLI) program at Beaumont would move to
         Roseway Heights M.S.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Beverly Cleary is changing to K-5: Beverly Cleary is changing from a K-8 to a K-5 model, so middle
    grade students need a comprehensive middle school location.
●   Avoids temporary building moves: Welcoming Beverly Cleary middle schoolers to Beaumont
    keeps students close to home and prevents them from having to move to temporary buildings
    during Beverly Cleary's planned summer 2027 seismic construction.
●   Logical school pathways: Adding the former Irvington boundary creates clear, connected middle
    school pathways for all students in the area.
●   Bringing DLI programs together: Moving the Spanish DLI from Beaumont to Roseway Heights
    builds a middle school serving the area for the Spanish DLI program that then moves to McDaniel
    for High School.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Full middle school options: Middle school students gain access to broader electives, athletics,
    middle school sports, and learning options.
●   Concentrated DLI program: Moving Spanish DLI to Roseway Heights builds a strong, focused
    language program location where teachers can plan and work together.

  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and

  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in

  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.

consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.

Sabin E.S.                                                                       Changes beginning in
                                                                                      2027-28




    WHAT’S CHANGING?

     ●   Sabin School would close as a school building.
     ●   Its full neighborhood K-5 school boundary would move to Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.
         Elementary.
     ●   Special Education students receiving Learning Center support would continue to receive
         support at Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Elementary in their learning center programming.
         Students in Sabin’s Social Emotional Skills program would also receive services in K–5 SES
         classrooms at Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., with individual placements determined by the
         Special Education Department in collaboration with school teams and families.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Low enrollment and building risks: Sabin's enrollment is expected to remain low (235 students),
    and the building faces major plumbing and heating system replacement needs, alongside
    dependencies on leased spaces for early learning.
●   Upgraded school features: Bringing together Sabin and Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Elementary
    moves students to a building with a newly upgraded roof, larger grounds (4.9 acres), and other
    strengthened school features.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Building capacity at Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.: Space capacity was evaluated to ensure the
    Mandarin Dual Language Immersion program could remain and also welcome Sabin families.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Better school grounds: Sabin students gain access to modern turfed fields, large outdoor spaces,
    and an updated roof.
●   Unified services: Elementary programs and early childhood services exist together under one roof.

Woodmere E.S.                                                                 Changes beginning in
                                                                                   2028-29




    WHAT’S CHANGING?

     ●   Woodmere School would close as a school building.
     ●   100% of its neighborhood K-5 school boundary would shift to Whitman K-5.
     ●   Special Education students receiving Learning Center support would move to Whitman
         K-5 and join their Inclusive School Community. Special Education CB students would
         either move to a CB class at Kelly K-5 or move with their general education peers to
         Whitman K-5 and join their Inclusive School Community - this will be determined on a
         case-by-case basis.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Low neighborhood enrollment: Both Whitman and Woodmere are significantly under district
    enrollment goals, with Woodmere's enrollment remaining in the low 220s.
●   Central school space: Whitman has room for 416 students, and receiving students from Woodmere
    makes better use of school space in a centrally located school with planned schoolyard
    improvements.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Whitman chosen over Woodmere: Whitman was chosen over Woodmere as it is more central to
    where students live and having similar amounts of space and building conditions.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Upgraded school grounds: Students benefit from planned schoolyard improvements and a central
    location.
●   Combined school strength: Combining school communities helps keep student numbers steady,
    allowing for stronger learning options, focused staff, and upgraded learning environments.
