RE: 2025–26 OSAS Achievement Results

Executive Summary & Narrative Overview
This memorandum outlines student performance on the 2025–26 Oregon Statewide Assessment System
(OSAS) in English Language Arts (ELA) and Mathematics. Over the past three years, Portland Public
Schools (PPS) has established a clear trajectory: systematic implementation of core instruction and
High-Quality Instructional Materials (HQIM) is driving steady student achievement that continues to
outpace statewide trends.

Key narrative takeaways include:

    ●​ Sustained Multi-Year Achievement Growth: PPS has demonstrated steady, multi-year gains in
       overall proficiency across both subjects. Over the last five years, ELA proficiency increased from
       55.2% to 58.6%, while Mathematics proficiency rose substantially from 44.9% to 52.1%.
    ●​ Expanding Lead Over Oregon Averages: PPS students outperformed statewide proficiency levels
       in both ELA (by 15.1%) and Mathematics (by 17.9%), widening the gap in PPS’s favor.
    ●​ Core Instructional Strategy Working: Strategic investments in adopted HQIM, grade-level
       alignment, and teacher observation/feedback loops underpin gains across most grade levels and
       student groups.
    ●​ Persistent Equity Imperative: Significant outcome gaps remain, specifically for Black students
       and Multi-Lingual Learners. Black student achievement in PPS continues to lag behind statewide
       Black student averages, reinforcing the urgent need for targeted classroom-level interventions.
    ●​ Actionable Next Steps: In 2026–27, PPS is transitioning to Ideal Student Learning Action 2,
       ensuring students directly engage with and persist through rigorous, grade-level tasks using
       newly adopted HQIM.

District Performance: Outpacing Statewide Trends
Districtwide ELA achievement showed steady, incremental growth, reaching 58.6% proficient in 2025–26
compared to 43.4% statewide (where trends have remained largely flat). In Mathematics, PPS proficiency
reached 52.1% compared to 34.2% statewide, demonstrating consistent multi-year gains. In addition to
grade level year-over-year gains, PPS longitudinal cohort tracking reveals that PPS students consistently
outpace statewide cohort growth trajectories as they move through middle school, particularly in
7th-grade math and ELA.

Disaggregated Achievement: Progress and Strategic Equity Focus
While multi-year gains are evident across Latinx, Multi-racial, White, and Special Education student
groups, a disaggregated look highlights critical areas where PPS is not yet meeting its equity goals.

    ●​ Black/African American Students: Performance showed slight gains (19.7% ELA, 13.2% Math),
       but achievement remains well below district averages and below statewide averages for Black
       peers (26.3% ELA, 17.8% Math). Addressing this gap must remain one of the district's highest
       priorities.
    ●​ Multi-Lingual Learners: Achievement remains low and flat across both ELA (6.2%) and Math
       (8.5%), pointing to a critical need for language-embedded Tier-1 instruction within core content
       classrooms.
    ●​ Students Eligible for Special Education Services: PPS students with disabilities continue to
       outperform statewide peers nearly two-to-one, reaching 34.0% in ELA and 31.6% in Math.

Board Goal Progress Summary (2025–26):

    ●​ 3rd Grade ELA: Board goals were Not Met for Asian, Black, Latinx, Native American, and Pacific
       Islander groups. Only Latinx students demonstrated 1-year growth (+2.8%).
    ●​ 5th Grade Math: Goals were Met by Asian (+3.0%) and Native American (+15.4%) students.
       Goals were Not Met for Black, Latinx, and Pacific Islander students.
    ●​ 8th Grade Readiness: Goals were Met by Pacific Islander students (+3.5%). Latinx students
       showed growth (+1.5%), while Black and Native American readiness dropped year-over-year.
Action Plan: District Continuous Improvement Plan Year Two (2026–27)

The 2025–26 gains validate the foundational strategies set forth in the District Continuous Improvement
Plan (DCIP). Year One established Ideal Student Learning Action 1 by ensuring students understood
learning targets, success criteria, and why the learning mattered.

To close persistent equity gaps and accelerate growth for Black students and Multi-Lingual Learners, PPS
is moving into Ideal Student Learning Action 2 for the 2026–27 school year.




Key Instructional Commitments for 2026–27:

    1.​ Grade-Level Rigor & HQIM: Ensuring all students, especially historically underserved groups, are
        consistently given grade-level, standards-aligned tasks using newly adopted High-Quality
        Instructional Materials rather than remedial work.
    2.​ Student Persistence & Engagement: Coaching and classroom feedback loops will directly
        measure student persistence through complex tasks and student questioning to clarify
        understanding.
    3.​ Culture in the Classroom: Embedding strategies that cultivate a strong sense of belonging and
        cultural connections (IF 2.6–2.9, 3.9) to build the psychological safety required for students to
        risk mistake-making and persist through challenging academic content.
