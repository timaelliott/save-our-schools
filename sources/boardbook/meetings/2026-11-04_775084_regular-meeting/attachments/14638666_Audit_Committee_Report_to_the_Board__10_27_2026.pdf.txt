Office of Internal Performance Audit (OIPA) Updates

Overview of OIPA:

The Board’s Sr. Internal Performance Auditor (Internal Auditor) Janise Hansen, CIA provided
the Overview of OIPA presentation. This presentation discussed the purpose and mission
of OIPA with a focus on the independence of OIPA and professional skepticism.

Our Internal Auditor provided Audit Standard’s criteria to maintain independence and how
each criteria is met. One criteria I would like to draw to the attention of the full Board is the
criteria to ensure OIPA is sufficiently removed from pressures to conduct engagements and
report findings, opinions, and conclusions objectively without fear of reprisal. Safeguards
are necessary to ensure the OIPA can meet this criteria. Such safeguards include:

   •   OIPA reports functionally to the Board via the Audit Committee
   •   Annual performance evaluation for the Internal Auditor is conducted by the Board
       and this process is lead by the Chair of the Audit Committee
   •   OIPA’s annual budget approval process

Any changes to these safeguards should to be carefully considered and discussed in the
Audit Committee as additional or alternative safeguards may be necessary to maintain
OIPA’s independence.

Our Internal Auditor also provided an article from Internal Audit 360, titled Why
Professional Skepticism Is Too Important to Leave to Internal Audit Alone,” which helped
provide some additional perspective on professional skepticism. I hope the full Board will
take a few minutes to read the article, and I would like to draw you attention to the below
two paragraphs that resonated with me:

       “The strongest governance environments are not necessarily those with the most
       policies, controls, or procedures. They are the environments where challenge is
       normalized. Where assumptions can be questioned. Where difficult conversations
       are not avoided. Where internal auditors can ask uncomfortable questions without
       fearing personal or professional consequences.

       Governance structures alone cannot achieve that. Culture must support it.
       Leadership must model it. Governance must protect it.”

Communication Protocols Memo:

Audit Standards recognize the importance of direct and open communication between
individual Board members, Committee members, and our Internal Auditor; this
communication does not violate Oregon Public Meetings Law. It was helpful for our
Internal Auditor to clearly communicate that there are some boundaries to this
communication, and where those boundaries are.

The Internal Auditor, Board, and Committee members should not use individual
communications, whether directly or through an intermediary, that create deliberation or
decision-making by a quorum of the Committee outside of a meeting conducted in
accordance with Oregon Public Meeting Law.

For example:

   •   The Internal Auditor should not communicate one Board and/or Committee
       member’s substantive position on a matter to other Board and/or Committee on
       topics that the Board and/or Committee will be deliberating.
   •   Board and/or Committee members should not ask the Internal Auditor to poll other
       Board and/or Committee members regarding their positions on a matter that is or
       may come before the Board and/or Committee member positions.

Nothing in the communication protocol is intended to restrict an individual Board and/or
Committee member’s access to the Internal Auditor or the Internal Auditor’s ability to
communicate directly with individual Board and/or Committee members as appropriate to
the Internal Auditor’s responsibilities.

I hope the full Board will take a few minutes to review the Communication Protocol Memo
to ensure we all due our part to protect our direct communications with our Internal
Auditor.

Monthly and Quarterly Financial Statement Reviews

Administrative directive 8.10.030-AD states in part:

       Budget to Actual Financial Reports and Revenue Reports will be provided to and
       discussed with the Board Finance, Audit, and Operations Committee on a monthly
       basis.

The Board does not have a Finance, Audit, and Operations Committee and these reports
are not provided and discussed with any of the Board’s Committees. Given the unexpected
mid-year budget shortfall we experienced as a District last year, it is important these
reports are provided and discussed with one of the Board’s committees consistently.

I have requested the monthly Budget to Actual Financial Reports and Revenue Reports be
provided and discussed with the Audit Committee. The Audit Committee does not meet
every single monthly. As a result, more than one monthly Budget to Actual Financial Report
and Revenue Reports may be presented and discussed in each of the Committee’s
meetings.

I have also requested the Chief Financial Officer provide the Committee with some training
on how to review monthly Budget to Actual Financial Reports and Revenue Reports. This
type of training may be something the full Board is interested in, and I am working with the
Chief Financial Officer to identify a training format that will be made available to the full
Board.

The Audit Committee anticipates beginning a standing Committee agenda item to discuss
the Budget to Actual Financial Reports and Revenue Reports in the December Committee
meeting and I will provide the full Board with information on how they can participate in the
applicable training once an appropriate training format is identified.
