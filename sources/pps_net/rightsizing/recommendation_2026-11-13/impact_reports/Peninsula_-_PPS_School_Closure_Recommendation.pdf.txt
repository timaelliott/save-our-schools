    WHAT’S CHANGING?

     ●   Peninsula School would close as a school building. Its full neighborhood K-5 school
         boundary would move to Rosa Parks K-5.
     ●   Peninsula’s Inclusive School Community with four Learning Center Classes would merge
         with Rosa Parks K-5’s Inclusive School Community.
     ●   Peninsula area students will go to George M.S. beginning with 6th graders in 2028-29.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Low enrollment numbers: Peninsula’s current enrollment is 239 students and is expected to remain
    low at 236 students by 2031-32 (over 100 students below target levels).
●   Updated building space: Bringing Peninsula and Rosa Parks students together helps keep steady
    student numbers at elementary enrollment across the Roosevelt cluster at an updated school site
    (Rosa Parks) with more room for students.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Shared support models: Both Peninsula and Rosa Parks are Title I schools with SUN school and
    community support programs, food pantries, and support for students with and without
    disabilities.
●   Building quality: Rosa Parks offers an updated building compared to Peninsula.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Updated school: Students move into Rosa Parks’ updated classrooms, learning spaces, and
    buildings.
●   Complete community support: Rosa Parks offers early learning classrooms, SUN school and
    community support programs, and an on-site food pantry.

ENROLLMENT AND TRANSFER

 ●   Neighborhood students: K-4th grade students and incoming K students residing in the Peninsula
     attendance area will enroll at Rosa Parks E.S. Rising 6th graders who reside in the former Peninsula
     attendance area will enroll at George M.S. for 6th grade beginning in 2028-29.
 ●   PPS transfer students: K-4th grade students attending Peninsula but not residing in the attendance
     area will enroll at Rosa Parks E.S. or may transfer to their neighborhood school by home address.
     Rising 6th grade students who reside in the Peninsula attendance area and have a sibling
     attending Ockley Green M.S. have a guaranteed transfer to Ockley Green.

School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
