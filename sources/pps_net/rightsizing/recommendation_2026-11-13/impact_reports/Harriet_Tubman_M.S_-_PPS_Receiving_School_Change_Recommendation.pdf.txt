    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Harriet Tubman would remain open as a neighborhood middle school. Harriet Tubman
         would welcome 6th-8th grade students from Vernon as Vernon moves to a K-5 model.
     ●   Middle grade students residing in the current Irvington school area would move to
         Beaumont Middle School.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Changing Vernon: Reconfiguring Vernon from a K-8 to a K-5 school requires a middle school
    location for 6th-8th grade students.
●   Building stable student enrollment: The Harriet Tubman site provides adequate classroom space,
    helping keep student enrollment numbers steady while ensuring students access a full range of
    middle school programs.
●   Fewer split middle schools: The Irvington community would move to Beverly Cleary and to
    Beaumont to reduce split pathways to high schools in the region.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Option to stay: Policies would allow middle grade students currently living in the Irvington school
    area the option to remain at Harriet Tubman through 8th grade.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Full middle school experience: Provides access to broader electives, athletics, and learning options
    at a comprehensive middle school.
●   Stable school programs: Higher student enrollment help support full-time middle school teachers
    and support staff.

ENROLLMENT AND TRANSFER

●   Neighborhood students: 5th-7th grade students who live in the Vernon attendance area will enroll
    at Harriet Tubman M.S. 5th grade students residing in the Irvington attendance area will enroll at
    Beaumont M.S. for 6th grade. Students residing in the Irvington attendance area attending Harriet
    Tubman M.S. may remain until the highest grade or transfer to Beaumont M.S. beginning in
    2027-28.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●   Academic Programs: Based on current enrollment projections, there will be six to eight sections at
    each grade level. Tubman will continue to house a small Mandarin Dual Language Immersion
    program. The school will continue to offer three-year pathways in Art, Band, and Dance, as well as
    a menu of other electives. Based on enrollment, the school will be supported by a full-time
    counselor.
●   Special Education: Currently Tubman has two Learning Center classes and one ISC class. With
    rightsizing, the current ISC classroom at Tubman will absorb a small number of students from the
    Beverly Cleary middle school ISC classroom that are in the Jefferson pathway. Tubman will
    continue to have two Learning Center classes and one ISC class.
●   Student and Family Supports: Happy Valley Counseling and Multnomah County School-Based
    Mental Health are identified as Harriet Tubman's school-based mental health partners. Student
    Services is reviewing continuity of services for students transitioning from Vernon and those who
    may move from Tubman to Beaumont.

REINVESTMENTS FOR RECEIVING SCHOOLS
Schools that receive additional students through consolidation will be allocated a proportion of the total programmatic
savings that result from Rightsizing actions. This allocation will be distributed as discretionary FTE that can be used for
instructional coaching, academic intervention, school climate support, social work, or duty support. School principals, with
consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.


  School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
  includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
  districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
  others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
  December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
