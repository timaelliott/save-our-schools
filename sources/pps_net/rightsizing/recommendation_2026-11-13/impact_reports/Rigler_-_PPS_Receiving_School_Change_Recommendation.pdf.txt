Rigler E.S.                                                                          Changes beginning in

     WHAT’S HAPPENING?

      ●​   Rigler would remain open as a focused Spanish Dual Language Immersion (DLI) K-5 school
           serving the Grant/McDaniel region. Rigler would receive Spanish DLI students from Scott
           K-5.
      ●​   Middle and high school pathway: All K-5 Spanish DLI students would move to Roseway
           Heights Middle School and then to McDaniel High School.
      ●​   Rigler neighborhood students who opt-out of Spanish DLI will continue to attend ​
           Scott K-5.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●​   DLI program serving the area: Moving Scott Spanish DLI to Rigler follows the district model of
     creating focused Spanish DLI schools serving each area.
●​   Reaching sustainable enrollment: Bringing together Scott Spanish DLI and Rigler Spanish DLI
     students brings enrollment above sustainable target levels.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●​   Regional DLI model: Follows the districtwide strategy for concentrated language program
     locations.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●​   School-wide language culture: Unifies dual language programs into a single school, creating a
     vibrant language learning culture where DLI teachers plan and work together.
●​   Connected middle school path: Ensures a clear, connected DLI pathway through middle and high
     school.

ENROLLMENT AND TRANSFER

●​   PPS transfer students: K-4th grade students attending Scott Spanish Immersion will enroll at Rigler
     E.S. Spanish Immersion or may exit DLI and transfer to their neighborhood school by home
     address. Native Spanish speakers residing in the Grant/McDaniel attendance areas may directly
     enroll at Rigler Spanish Immersion. Incoming K students residing in the historical Rigler attendance
     area may directly enroll at Rigler Spanish Immersion. Incoming 1st-4th grade students residing in
     the historical Rigler attendance area may directly enroll at Rigler Spanish Immersion if they meet
     language requirements. All other students must apply to Rigler Spanish Immersion through the
     lottery, with preference given to students residing in the Grant/McDaniel attendance areas.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●​   Academic Programs: Rigler will maintain and expand its whole school Spanish Dual Language
     Immersion model. SDLI instruction will continue to follow the 90-10 model. Based on current
     enrollment projections, there will be two to four classrooms at each grade-level, with blends not
     required. Students will receive PE, Arts, and Library/Media instruction each week. The majority of
     staff in the building will be Spanish-bilingual. Based on enrollment, the school will be supported by
     a full-time counselor.
●​   Special Education: Currently, Rigler has one Learning Center class. With rightsizing, the special
     education service delivery model at Rigler will remain unchanged.
●​   Student and Family Supports: Morrison Child and Family Services is identified as Rigler's
     school-based mental health partner. Services is working with the agency to review capacity and
     continuity of services as students transition with the Spanish immersion program. As district and
     county budgets allow, the Latino Network SUN program and other culturally-specific partnerships
     will continue to support the school community. Our childcare team is also exploring expanded
     capacity for after school programming at Rigler.

REINVESTMENTS FOR RECEIVING SCHOOLS
Schools that receive additional students through consolidation will be allocated a proportion of the total programmatic
savings that result from Rightsizing actions. This allocation will be distributed as discretionary FTE that can be used for
instructional coaching, academic intervention, school climate support, social work, or duty support. School principals, with
consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.



  School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
  includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
  districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
  others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
  December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
