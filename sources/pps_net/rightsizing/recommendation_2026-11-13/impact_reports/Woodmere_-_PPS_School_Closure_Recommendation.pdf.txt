Woodmere E.S.                                                                 Changes beginning in
                                                                                   2028-29




    WHAT’S CHANGING?

     ●   Woodmere School would close as a school building.
     ●   100% of its neighborhood K-5 school boundary would shift to Whitman K-5.
     ●   Special Education students receiving Learning Center support would move to Whitman
         K-5 and join their Inclusive School Community. Special Education CB students would
         either move to a CB class at Kelly K-5 or move with their general education peers to
         Whitman K-5 and join their Inclusive School Community - this will be determined on a
         case-by-case basis.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Low neighborhood enrollment: Both Whitman and Woodmere are significantly under district
    enrollment goals, with Woodmere's enrollment remaining in the low 220s.
●   Central school space: Whitman has room for 416 students, and receiving students from Woodmere
    makes better use of school space in a centrally located school with planned schoolyard
    improvements.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Whitman chosen over Woodmere: Whitman was chosen over Woodmere as it is more central to
    where students live and having similar amounts of space and building conditions.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Upgraded school grounds: Students benefit from planned schoolyard improvements and a central
    location.
●   Combined school strength: Combining school communities helps keep student numbers steady,
    allowing for stronger learning options, focused staff, and upgraded learning environments.

ENROLLMENT AND TRANSFER

●   Neighborhood students: K-4th grade students and incoming K students residing in the Woodmere
    attendance area will enroll at Whitman E.S.
●   PPS transfer students: K-4th grade students attending Woodmere but not residing in the
    attendance area will enroll at Whitman E.S. or may transfer to their neighborhood school by home
    address.

School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
