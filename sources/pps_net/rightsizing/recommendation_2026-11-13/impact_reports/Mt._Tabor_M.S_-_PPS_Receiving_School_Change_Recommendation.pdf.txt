    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Mt. Tabor Middle School would expand its school boundary to welcome 6th-8th grade
         students from Laurelhurst as Laurelhurst becomes a K-5 school.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Changing Laurelhurst: Reconfiguring Laurelhurst to a K-5 model requires a middle school location
    for 6th-8th grade students.
●   Space and direct pathway: Mt. Tabor provides nearby building space and connects elementary
    students with a direct middle school pathway into Grant High School.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Unified high school path: Laurelhurst middle grade students join Mt. Tabor Japanese DLI students,
    leading directly to Grant High School.
●   Restored student enrollment: Restores student enrollment numbers at Mt. Tabor following past
    feeder pattern reductions.

ENROLLMENT AND TRANSFER

●   Neighborhood students: 5th-7th grade students residing in the Laurelhurst attendance area will
    enroll at Mt. Tabor M.S.
●   PPS transfer students: 5th-7th grade students attending Laurelhurst but not residing in the
    attendance area will enroll at Mt. Tabor M.S.or may transfer to their neighborhood school by home
    address.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●   Academic Programs: Based on current enrollment projections, there will be six to seven sections at
    each grade level. Mt. Tabor will continue to house the Japanese Dual Language Immersion
    program. Mt. Tabor will also continue to offer multi-year pathways in Art, Band, Theater, and STEM,
    as well as a menu of other electives. Based on enrollment, the school will be supported by a
    full-time counselor.
●   Special Education: There will be no change to the Special Education service delivery model at Mt.
    Tabor.
●   Student and Family Supports: Trillium is identified as Mt. Tabor's school-based mental health
    partner. Student Services will review any changes in service needs as school enrollment and
    program plans are finalized.

REINVESTMENTS FOR RECEIVING SCHOOLS
Schools that receive additional students through consolidation will be allocated a proportion of the total programmatic
savings that result from Rightsizing actions. This allocation will be distributed as discretionary FTE that can be used for
instructional coaching, academic intervention, school climate support, social work, or duty support. School principals, with
consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.

School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
