     WHAT’S HAPPENING?

      ●​   Metropolitan Learning Center (MLC) would change from a K-12 to a K-8 alternative
           (choice) school.
      ●​   High school (9th-12th) students would transition to another PPS alternative school, attend
           a focus option, or return to their designated neighborhood high schools.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●​   Enrollment decline: MLC high school enrollment has fallen to a level that risks providing the level
     of programming students deserve.
●​   Changing MLC to K-8 matches district grade structures.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●​   Increasing enrollment, timing of student moves, and where students may transition to were all
     considerations.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●​   High schoolers will have a range of other options, from small and focused alternative learning
     communities to attending their neighborhood comprehensive high school with full high school
     athletics, advanced placement (AP) courses, and electives.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●​   Academic Programs: MLC will serve grades K-8 only. Based on current enrollment projections,
     there will be blends required at both the elementary and middle school levels.
●​   Special Education: Currently, MLC has two Learning Center classes. With rightsizing, MLC will have
     1.5 Learning Center classes serving K-8th grade.
●​   Student and Family Supports: William Temple House is identified as Metropolitan Learning
     Center's school-based mental health partner. Student Services is reviewing continuity of services
     for students who may transition to other schools, including high school students whose receiving
     schools have not yet been confirmed.


  School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
  includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
  districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
  others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
  December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
