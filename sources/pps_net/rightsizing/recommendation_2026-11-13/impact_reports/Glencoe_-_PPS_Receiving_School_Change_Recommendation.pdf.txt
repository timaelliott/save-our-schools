     WHAT’S HAPPENING?

      ●​   Glencoe would remain open as a neighborhood K-5 school.
      ●​   Glencoe will partner with Columbia Regional Inclusive Services (CRIS) to welcome students
           in the Deaf and Hard of Hearing (DHH) Program.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●​   Clear middle school path: Moving the Deaf and Hard of Hearing Program, currently located at
     Creston, to Glencoe connects elementary students directly with the Mt. Tabor Middle School
     feeder pathway.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●​   Route to middle and high school cohesion: Surrounding schools were evaluated, but space,
     numbers, and route to high schools made Glencoe the best fit for the DHH program



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●​   Connected special education pathway: DHH students gain a direct, logical move from elementary
     to Mt. Tabor Middle School.
●​   Efficient building use: Bringing DHH to Glencoe uses classroom space more efficiently while
     keeping enrollment stable.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●​   Academic Programs: Glencoe will welcome students from the Deaf & Hard of Hearing CRIS
     program. The general education elementary program will remain unchanged. Based on enrollment
     projections, there will be 2-3 classrooms at each grade level. Blends may be required. Students will
     receive PE, Arts, and Library/Media instruction each week. Based on enrollment, the school will be
     supported by a part-time counselor.
●​   Special Education: Currently, Glencoe has one Learning Center class. With rightsizing, the DHH
     CRIS program at Creston is planned to move to the Glencoe school site. This would constitute
     three classrooms, one instructional space, and one Preschool Program. The DHH program serves
     32 students from 22 school districts in the region and currently serves seven PPS students. PPS
     DHH students come from all over PPS. Glencoe will continue to have one Learning Center Space.
●​   Student and Family Supports: Glencoe does not have a current mental health partnership.

REINVESTMENTS FOR RECEIVING SCHOOLS
Schools that receive additional students through consolidation will be allocated a proportion of the total programmatic
savings that result from Rightsizing actions. This allocation will be distributed as discretionary FTE that can be used for
instructional coaching, academic intervention, school climate support, social work, or duty support. School principals, with
consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.


  School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
  includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
  districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
  others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
  December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
