Clark E.S.                                                                           Changes beginning in
                                                                                          2028-29




     WHAT’S HAPPENING?

      ●​   Clark would remain open as a neighborhood K-5 school.
      ●​   The Mandarin Dual Language Immersion (DLI) program would move and bring students
           together at Woodstock K-5.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●​   Low program enrollment: Enrollment in Clark's Mandarin DLI program dropped below 8
     kindergarteners, making it difficult to keep as a stand-alone program.
●​   Focusing on neighborhood core: Moving DLI reduces operational complexity and lets Clark focus
     entirely on its neighborhood elementary program.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●​   Program Strength: Concentrating Mandarin DLI programs at Woodstock creates a language
     program with enough students to continue on the east side.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●​   Focused neighborhood programming: Clark can dedicate all of its classroom space and staff to
     neighborhood elementary learning.
●​   Concentrated DLI program: Mandarin DLI students get a larger, fully supported language learning
     program at Woodstock.
●​   Teacher teamwork: Concentrating dual language programs allows DLI teachers to work together,
     plan together, and grow alongside fellow language educators.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●​   Academic Programs: School year 2028-29: Based on current enrollment projections, there will be
     one to two classrooms at each grade level, with blends possibly required. All classrooms will be
     neighborhood English Scholar classrooms. Students will receive PE, Arts, and Library/Media
     instruction each week. Based on enrollment, the school will be supported by a part-time counselor.
●​   Special Education: Currently, Clark has one Learning Center and one CB classroom. With
     rightsizing, there will be no change to the Special Education service delivery model at Clark for the
     2027-2028 school year.
●​   Student and Family Supports: Clark has Sankofa & Happy Valley Counseling as their School-Based
     Mental Health Partner. Depending on funding, current mental health and childcare programming
     will remain intact.


  School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
  includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
  districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
  others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
  December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
