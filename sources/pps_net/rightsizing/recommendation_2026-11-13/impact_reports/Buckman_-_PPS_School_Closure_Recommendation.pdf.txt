Buckman E.S.                                                                    Changes beginning in

    WHAT’S CHANGING?

     ●   Buckman School would close as a school building.
     ●   The Arts special-focus choice program would close.
     ●   Neighborhood boundary split: Buckman neighborhood students living south of E.
         Burnside St. would move to Abernethy E.S. Buckman neighborhood students living north
         of E. Burnside St. would move to Sunnyside K-5.
     ●   Special Education students in the SES classrooms would move to Sunnyside K-5. Special
         Education students receiving Learning Center support would continue to receive support
         at either Abernethy E.S. or Sunnyside K-5.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   The Buckman building needs major repairs. The school requires urgent structural fixes and heavy
    maintenance to make it safe and modern.
●   Enrollment is dropping. Buckman is expected to drop to just 239 students over the next few years,
    making it difficult to fund full programs and staffing.
●   Nearby schools are in better shape. Both Abernethy and Sunnyside have better buildings and are
    in good locations to welcome Buckman families.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Building condition over size: Even though Buckman is a larger building than Abernethy, Buckman’s
    building repair needs are severe.
●   Title I Funding Support: District teams considered Buckman’s Title I status carefully to ensure
    students moving to receiving schools continue to get strong learning and family support services.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Better, safer classrooms: Moving to Abernethy and Sunnyside means learning in updated buildings
    with better resources and learning spaces.
●   More support and activities: Combining school communities gives students access to more
    classmates, full-time staff, and steady classroom support.

    School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
    includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
    districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
    guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
    others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
    onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
    and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
    December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
    working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
