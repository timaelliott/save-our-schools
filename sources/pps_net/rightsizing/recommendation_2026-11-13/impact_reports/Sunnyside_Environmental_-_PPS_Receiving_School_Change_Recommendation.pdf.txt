Environmental E.S.

    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Sunnyside would change to a K-5 neighborhood school and would welcome 50% of the
         neighborhood school boundary from Buckman (the area north of Burnside).
     ●   Middle grade (6th-8th) students would move to Hosford Middle School.
     ●   Sunnyside K-5 Environmental School would continue in the focus option lottery.
     ●   Middle and high school path: High school students would move to Cleveland High School
         instead of Franklin High School.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Richer middle school options: Changing Sunnyside from a K-8 special-focus choice school to a K-5
    neighborhood school allows 6th-8th grade students to access a focused middle school with
    sustainable programming. This means students will have more access to educational activities.
●   Better building conditions: Sunnyside provides a central location and better building conditions
    compared to the closing Buckman school.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Sunnyside Environmental School has been a high demand lottery program. Retaining the choice
    portion for the Sunnyside Environmental School mitigates the impact of closing the Buckman Arts
    program and provides families with access to at least one of the two choice options.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Updated building environment: Incoming Buckman students move into a facility that has received
    substantial updates.
●   Comprehensive middle school access: Neighborhood 6th-8th graders join Hosford for broader
    electives, athletics, and middle school staff.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●   Academic Programs: Based on current enrollment projections, there will be two to three
    classrooms at each grade-level, with blends not required. Currently, Buckman has blended
    elementary classrooms and Sunnyside does not. The Academics team will work with educators to
    plan curriculum for students who are in blends during the 2026-27 school year and may not be
    during the 2027-28 school year. Sunnyside will serve only K-5 students and will retain its
    Environmental Focus Option program. The Academics team will work with staff to merge the
    instructional program currently in place at Buckman with that at Sunnyside, including consideration
    of Buckman's current Arts focus. Students will receive PE, Arts, and Library/Media instruction each
    week. Based on enrollment, the school will be supported by a full-time counselor.
●   Special Education: Currently, Buckman has one Learning Center and two SES classrooms.
    Sunnyside has three Learning Center classrooms. Hosford has two Learning Centers and one ISC
    classroom. With rightsizing, the Buckman SES classrooms will move with much of the Buckman
    student population to Sunnyside. Sunnyside will have a projected 3.5 Learning Center FTE and two
    SES classrooms. For the Sunnyside 6th-8th graders attending Hosford, Hosford will add a CB
    classroom to address neurodivergent Special Education needs. Hosford will have three Learning
    Center FTE, one ISC classroom, and one CB classroom.
●   Student and Family Supports: Currently, both Sunnyside and Buckman offer before and after
    school programming. Our RESJ team is exploring an expansion of childcare capacity at Sunnyside.
    Currently, there is SUN programming at Buckman, but not at Sunnyside. Continued or expanded
    SUN programming will depend on County, PPS and City budget processes. Currently, there are no
    RESJ partners at Buckman or Sunnyside. RESJ partnerships continuing or expanding will be
    dependent on the PPS budget process. Currently, Buckman has a school-based mental health
    partnership with Morrison Child and Family Services. Student Services is reviewing partnership
    arrangements to support students transitioning to Sunnyside, as well as continuity of support for
    middle-grade students who may move from Sunnyside to Hosford.

REINVESTMENTS FOR RECEIVING SCHOOLS
Schools that receive additional students through consolidation will be allocated a proportion of the total programmatic
savings that result from Rightsizing actions. This allocation will be distributed as discretionary FTE that can be used for
instructional coaching, academic intervention, school climate support, social work, or duty support. School principals, with
consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.


  School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
  includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
  districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
  others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
  December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
