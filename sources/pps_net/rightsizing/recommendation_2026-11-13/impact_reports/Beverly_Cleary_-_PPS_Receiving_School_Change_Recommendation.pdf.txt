    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Beverly Cleary would change from a K-8 school to a neighborhood K-5 school.
     ●   Beverly Cleary's boundary would expand to include K-5 students from the Irvington
         neighborhood.
     ●   Middle grade (6th-8th) students would move to Beaumont Middle School.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Central location and space: Beverly Cleary has plenty of space and a central location to serve
    elementary students.
●   Major seismic construction: Planned seismic upgrades make changing to K-5 ideal to reduce the
    need for middle grade students to move to temporary buildings during construction.
●   School designed for younger students: Elementary students benefit from a school focused entirely
    on K-5 learning and development.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Avoiding temporary moves: Moving middle grade students to Beaumont in Fall 2027 significantly
    reduces how many students need to move to temporary sites during construction.
●   Chosen over Irvington: Beverly Cleary was selected over Irvington because it is more central and
    its planned building upgrades will create an improved space for Irvington students.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Enough students to support programs: By combining Irvington and Beverly Cleary K-5, students
    and families will benefit from long-term steady student numbers.
●   Safe and improved building: Once seismic upgrades are completed in the summer of 2028, both
    communities will benefit from a seismically safe school with upgraded spaces.
●   Full middle school options: 6th-8th graders gain access to comprehensive middle school electives,
    sports programs, and activities at Beaumont.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●   Academic Programs: Beverly Cleary will house only grades K-5. Based on current enrollment
    projections, there will be 3 to 4 classrooms at each grade-level, with blends not required.
    Currently, Irvington has blended classrooms and Beverly Cleary does not. The Academics team will
    work with educators to plan curriculum for students who are in blends during the 2026-27 school
    year and may not be during the 2027-28 school year. Students will receive PE, Arts, and
    Library/Media instruction each week. Based on enrollment, the school will be supported by a
    full-time counselor.
●   Special Education: Currently, Beverly Cleary has 1.5 Learning Center Classes and one ISC
    classroom. With rightsizing, Beverly Cleary, K-5, will have two Learning Centers after receiving
    Irvington K-5. Students currently in the Beverly Cleary 6-8 ISC will either attend the Tubman ISC or
    Beaumont ISC, depending on whether they live in the Jefferson boundary or the Grant boundary.
    Special education will work directly with families during the transition. Both Beaumont and Tubman
    already have ISC classrooms.
●   Student and Family Supports: Currently, both Beverly Cleary and Irvington offer before and after
    school programming. Our RESJ team is exploring an expansion of childcare capacity at Beverly
    Cleary. Currently, there is no SUN programming at Beverly Cleary or Irvington. Continued SUN
    programming will depend on County, PPS and City budget processes. Currently, there are no RESJ
    partners at Beverly Cleary and one at Irvington. Continuation or expansion of existing partnerships
    will be dependent on the PPS budget process. Currently, the Student Services Department is
    reviewing school-based mental health partnership arrangements at Beverly Cleary as students
    transition from Irvington and middle-grade students move to Beaumont.

REINVESTMENTS FOR RECEIVING SCHOOLS
Schools that receive additional students through consolidation will be allocated a proportion of the total programmatic
savings that result from Rightsizing actions. This allocation will be distributed as discretionary FTE that can be used for
instructional coaching, academic intervention, school climate support, social work, or duty support. School principals, with
consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.


  School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
  includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
  districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
  others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
  December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
