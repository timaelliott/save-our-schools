    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Duniway would remain open as a neighborhood K-5 school.
     ●   Duniway would welcome 100% of the neighborhood students from Lewis K-5 school.
     ●   Duniway's boundary area west of McLoughlin Blvd would move to Llewellyn.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Central location and space: Duniway offers a central location, plenty of school space, and strong
    building conditions for Duinway and Llewellyn students.
●   Closer neighborhood access: Shifting the far west boundary to Llewellyn assigns students to the
    school physically closest to them.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Duniway chosen over Lewis: When reviewing both schools together, Duniway was selected to
    remain open because the building can support more students.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Enough students to support programs: Bringing Lewis and Duniway student communities together
    supports a strong, sustainable school with full academic and student support.
●   Logical boundary lines: Neighborhood areas are assigned to schools with shorter, safer walks.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●   Academic Programs: School year 2028-29: Based on current enrollment projects, there will be
    three to four classrooms at each grade level, with blends not required. Students will receive PE,
    Arts, and Library/Media instruction each week. Based on enrollment, the school will be supported
    by a full-time counselor.
●   Special Education: Currently, Duniway has one Learning Center classroom. With rightsizing, the CB
    classroom currently located at Lewis Elementary, will move to Duniway with their general education
    peers. Duniway will continue to support a Learning Center program.
●   Student and Family Supports: Student Services is reviewing school-based mental health support
    arrangements at Duniway as district school-change plans are developed. Our childcare team is
    exploring an expansion of after school capacity at Duniway.

REINVESTMENTS FOR RECEIVING SCHOOLS
Schools that receive additional students through consolidation will be allocated a proportion of the total programmatic
savings that result from Rightsizing actions. This allocation will be distributed as discretionary FTE that can be used for
instructional coaching, academic intervention, school climate support, social work, or duty support. School principals, with
consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.


  School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
  includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
  districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
  others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
  December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
