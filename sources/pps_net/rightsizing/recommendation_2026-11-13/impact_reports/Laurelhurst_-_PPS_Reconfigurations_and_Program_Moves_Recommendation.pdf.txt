    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Laurelhurst would change from a K-8 school to a neighborhood K-5 school.
     ●   Middle grade (6th-8th) students would move to Mt. Tabor Middle School and continue to
         Grant High School.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Richer middle school options: Moving 6th-8th grade students to a focused middle school will
    create sustainable programming. This means students will have more access to educational
    activities.
●   Balances enrollment: Strengthens enrollment at Mt. Tabor Middle School after significant impacts
    from other recent changes.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Direct Grant High pathway: Laurelhurst middle grade students join Mt. Tabor DLI student groups,
    leading directly to Grant High School.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Elementary-focused school: Allows Laurelhurst to dedicate all school spaces and playgrounds
    specifically to K-5 learning.
●   Grant High pathway: Laurelhurst middle grade students move with Mt. Tabor Japanese DLI
    students to Grant High School.

ENROLLMENT AND TRANSFER

●   Neighborhood students: 5th-7th grade students residing in the Laurelhurst attendance area will
    enroll at Mt. Tabor M.S.
●   PPS transfer students: 5th-7th grade students attending Laurelhurst but not residing in the
    attendance area will enroll at Mt. Tabor M.S.or may transfer to their neighborhood school by home
    address.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●     Academic Programs: Laurelhurst will house only students in grades K-5. Based on current
      enrollment projections, there will be two to three classrooms at each grade level. Students will
      receive PE, Arts, and Library/Media instruction each week. Based on enrollment, the school will be
      supported by a full-time counselor.
●     Special Education: Currently, Laurelhurst has two Learning Center classes. With rightsizing,
      Laurelhurst will maintain the current programming.
●     Student and Family Supports: Happy Valley Counseling is the identified School-Based Mental
      Health Partner.


    School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
    includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
    districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
    guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
    others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
    onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
    and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
    December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
    working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
