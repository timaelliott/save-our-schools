    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Vernon would change from a K-8 school to a neighborhood K-5 school.
     ●   Middle grade (6th-8th) students would move to Harriet Tubman Middle School.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Comprehensive middle school access: Changing Vernon from a K-8 to a K-5 model provides
    middle grade students access to Harriet Tubman Middle School, helping keep student numbers
    steady at Tubman.
●   Seismically upgraded building: Vernon received a full seismic retrofit in Summer 2026, offering
    strong building conditions for elementary learning.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Middle grade enrollment support: Moving middle grades to Harriet Tubman supports balanced
    middle grades enrollment across the cluster.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Elementary-focused school: Allows elementary students to benefit from a school designed entirely
    for early childhood development.
●   Full middle school experience: Middle school students gain access to the broader electives,
    athletics, and learning options of a comprehensive middle school.

    attendance area will enroll at Harriet Tubman M.S. or may transfer to their neighborhood school by

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●     Academic Programs: Vernon will only serve students in grades K-5. Based on current enrollment
      projections, there will be two classrooms at each grade-level, with blends not required. Students
      will receive PE, Arts, and Library/Media instruction each week. Based on enrollment, the school will
      be supported by a part-time counselor.
●     Special Education: Currently, Vernon has two Learning Center classes. With rightsizing, Vernon will
      receive one SES classroom that currently serves students at Sabin. Vernon will have two Learning
      Centers and one SES classroom.
●     Student and Family Supports: Happy Valley Counseling is identified as Vernon's school-based
      mental health partner. Student Services is reviewing continuity of services for middle-grade
      students who may transition to Harriet Tubman. Depending on country and district budgets, the
      existing SUN program and other culturally-specific partnerships will remain intact.


    School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
    includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
    districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
    guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
    others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
    onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
    and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
    December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
    working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
