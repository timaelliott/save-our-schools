    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Abernethy would remain open as a neighborhood K-5 school.
     ●   Abernethy would welcome the Buckman student population who live south of
         Burnside Street.
     ●   Buckman neighborhood students who live south of Burnside St. would move to Abernethy
         ES.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Enrollment is dropping: Abernethy can hold 441 students, but enrollment is expected to drop to
    241 without changes.
●   Filling open space: Bringing Buckman and Abernethy student communities together supports a
    stable size and keeps student enrollment numbers steady.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Abernethy was chosen to stay open because the Buckman building needs urgent, major structural
    repairs.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   More support and programs: Steady enrollment numbers support stable staffing for classrooms
    and programs.
●   On-site childcare: Families would continue to have access to reliable before- and after-school
    childcare

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●   Academic Programs: Based on current enrollment projections, there will be two to three
    classrooms at each grade-level, with blends not required. Currently, Abernethy and Buckman have
    blended classrooms in different grade bands. The Academics team will work with educators to plan
    curriculum for students who are in blends during the 2026-27 school year and may not be during
    the 2027-28 school year. Students will receive PE, Arts, and Library/Media instruction each week.
    Based on enrollment, the school will be supported by a full-time counselor.
●   Special Education: Currently, Buckman has one Learning Center and two SES classrooms.
    Abernethy has one Learning Center classroom. Sunnyside has three Learning Center classrooms.
    With rightsizing, the Buckman SES classrooms will move with much of the Buckman student
    population to Sunnyside. Sunnyside will now have a projected 3.5 Learning Center FTE and two
    SES classrooms. Abernethy will maintain a one Learning Center classroom model.
●   Student and Family Supports: Currently, both Abernethy and Buckman offer before and after
    school programming. Our RESJ team is exploring an expansion of childcare capacity at Abernethy.
    Currently, there is SUN programming at Buckman, but not at Abernethy. Continued SUN
    programming will depend on County, PPS and City budget processes. Currently, there are no RESJ
    partners at Buckman or Abernethy. Continuation or expansion of existing partnerships will be
    dependent on the PPS budget process. Currently, there are no contracted mental health
    partnerships at Abernethy. Our Student Services team is working with existing mental health
    partners at the merging sites to ensure continuity of care.

REINVESTMENTS FOR RECEIVING SCHOOLS
Schools that receive additional students through consolidation will be allocated a proportion of the total programmatic
savings that result from Rightsizing actions. This allocation will be distributed as discretionary FTE that can be used for
instructional coaching, academic intervention, school climate support, social work, or duty support. School principals, with
consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.


  School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
  includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
  districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
  others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
  December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
