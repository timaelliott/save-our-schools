    WHAT’S CHANGING?

     ●   Beach School would close as a school building.
     ●   Students who live in the Beach neighborhood would go to Chief Joseph.
     ●   Special Education students receiving Learning Center support would continue to receive
         support at Chief Joseph.
     ●   The Spanish Dual Language Immersion (DLI) program would move to César Chávez E.S.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   The Beach building needs major repairs. The roof and building structure require urgent, expensive
    fixes to make it safe and modern.
●   We want stronger programs in safer buildings. Moving students out of Beach addresses safety
    concerns while bringing students together into stronger, fully supported schools.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Chief Joseph is easier to reach: Chief Joseph is closer to where most families in the new school
    area live.
●   Chief Joseph has plenty of space: Even though Beach is a larger building, Chief Joseph has more
    than enough room for all students.
●   Chief Joseph is already updated: Chief Joseph recently received major improvements funded by
    local school bonds, including roof repairs and updated classrooms for general and special
    education.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Better, safer classrooms: Students would move into buildings with upgraded spaces, learn
    alongside more classmates, and have access to more teachers and staff.
●   A stronger Spanish DLI program: Bringing all Spanish DLI students together at César Chávez
    creates a full, rich language learning community from kindergarten through 5th grade. It also lets
    Spanish-speaking teachers work together and plan as a team.

School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
