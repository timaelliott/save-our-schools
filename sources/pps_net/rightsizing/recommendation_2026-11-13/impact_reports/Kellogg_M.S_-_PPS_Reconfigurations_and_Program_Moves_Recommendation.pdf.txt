    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Kellogg Middle School would expand its boundary to welcome neighborhood students
         from Atkinson and Creston beginning in 2027-28.
     ●   All Spanish Immersion in the southeast remains at Kellogg.
     ●   Lent neighborhood students who do not enroll in Spanish Immersion would shift from
         Kellogg to Brentwood Middle School beginning in 2028-29.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Connecting elementary and middle schools: Ensures Atkinson and Creston students move
    together into Kellogg while continuing on to Franklin High School.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Maximizing modern space: Uses available building space at newly built Kellogg efficiently while
    building a strong student body.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   State-of-the-art building: More students get to learn in Kellogg's modern, recently rebuilt middle
    school building.
●   Single middle and high school path: All neighborhood students within Kellogg’s new boundary
    would feed directly to Franklin High School.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●     Academic Programs: Based on current enrollment projections, there will be around six sections at
      each grade level. Kellogg will also continue to offer multi-year pathways in Art, Band, Choir, and
      STEM, as well as a menu of other electives. Based on enrollment, the school will be supported by a
      full-time counselor.
●     Special Education: Currently, Kellogg has two Learning Center classes, one ISC class, and one SES
      class. With rightsizing, Kellogg will maintain the same level of programming.
●     Student and Family Supports: Multnomah County School-Based Mental Health and Portal Wellness
      are identified as Kellogg's school-based mental health partners. Student Services will coordinate
      with these partners as needed to review service continuity and capacity under any approved school
      changes.


    School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
    includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
    districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
    guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
    others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
    onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
    and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
    December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
    working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
