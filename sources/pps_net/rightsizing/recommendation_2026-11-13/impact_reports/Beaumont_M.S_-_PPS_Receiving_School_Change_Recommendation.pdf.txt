    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Beaumont would remain open as a neighborhood middle school (grades 6-8).
     ●   Beaumont would welcome 6th-8th grade students from Beverly Cleary, including students
         from the former Irvington boundary.
     ●   The Spanish Dual Language Immersion (DLI) program at Beaumont would move to
         Roseway Heights M.S.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Beverly Cleary is changing to K-5: Beverly Cleary is changing from a K-8 to a K-5 model, so middle
    grade students need a comprehensive middle school location.
●   Avoids temporary building moves: Welcoming Beverly Cleary middle schoolers to Beaumont
    keeps students close to home and prevents them from having to move to temporary buildings
    during Beverly Cleary's planned summer 2027 seismic construction.
●   Logical school pathways: Adding the former Irvington boundary creates clear, connected middle
    school pathways for all students in the area.
●   Bringing DLI programs together: Moving the Spanish DLI from Beaumont to Roseway Heights
    builds a middle school serving the area for the Spanish DLI program that then moves to McDaniel
    for High School.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Full middle school options: Middle school students gain access to broader electives, athletics,
    middle school sports, and learning options.
●   Concentrated DLI program: Moving Spanish DLI to Roseway Heights builds a strong, focused
    language program location where teachers can plan and work together.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●   Academic Programs: Based on current enrollment projections, there will be six to seven sections at
    each grade level. All classrooms will be neighborhood English Scholar classrooms. Beaumont will
    continue to offer three-year pathways in Art, Band, and Choir, as well as a menu of other electives.
    Based on enrollment, the school will be supported by a full-time counselor.
●   Special Education: Currently, Beaumont has two Learning Center classes, two ISC classes, and one
    SES classroom. With rightsizing, students from Beverly Cleary Middle School ISC who are in the
    Grant HS pathway, will move to Beaumont ISC and join the current programming. Beaumont will
    have two Learning Center classes, two ISC classes, and one SES class.
●   Student and Family Supports: Currently, there is SUN programming at Beaumont. Continued SUN
    programming will depend on County, PPS and City budget processes. Currently, there are two
    RESJ partners at Beaumont. Continuation or expansion of existing partnerships will be dependent
    on the PPS budget process. Currently, Cascadia is identified as the School-Based Mental Health
    Partner at Beaumont. Student Services is working with mental health partners to review capacity
    and plan for continuity of support as additional middle-grade students transition to Beaumont.

REINVESTMENTS FOR RECEIVING SCHOOLS
Schools that receive additional students through consolidation will be allocated a proportion of the total programmatic
savings that result from Rightsizing actions. This allocation will be distributed as discretionary FTE that can be used for
instructional coaching, academic intervention, school climate support, social work, or duty support. School principals, with
consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.


  School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
  includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
  districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
  others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
  December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
