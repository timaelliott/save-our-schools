    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Roseway Heights would remain open as a neighborhood middle school.
     ●   Roseway Heights would receive Spanish Dual Language Immersion (DLI) middle grade
         (6th-8th) students from Beaumont Middle School.
     ●   The Vietnamese DLI program would remain at Roseway Heights.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Middle school DLI school serving the area: Moving Spanish DLI middle grade students from
    Beaumont to Roseway Heights brings together middle and high school Spanish language
    pathways and matches middle school changes across the region.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Program strength: Keeping both DLI programs together was chosen because both language tracks
    continue to show student growth.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Dual language program location: Houses both Spanish and Vietnamese DLI middle school
    pathways on one school.
●   Teacher teamwork: Concentrating dual language programs allows DLI teachers to work together,
    plan together, and grow alongside fellow language educators.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●   Academic Programs: Roseway Heights will maintain its English Scholar and Vietnamese Dual
    Language Immersion programs, while expanding its Spanish Dual Language Immersion model.
    Based on current enrollment projections, there will be seven to eight sections at each grade level.
    Roseway Heights will continue to offer a range of electives including multi-year pathways in Art,
    Band, Mariachi, STEM, and a host of other electives. Based on enrollment, the school will be
    supported by a full-time counselor.
●   Special Education: Currently, Roseway Heights has 2.5 Learning Center classes and one SES class.
    With rightsizing, Roseway will continue to have one SES and the Learning Center classes.
●   Student and Family Supports: iPhilosophy and NARA Northwest are identified as Roseway Heights'
    school-based mental health partners. Student Services is reviewing capacity and continuity of
    services for students arriving with the Spanish immersion program from Beaumont.

REINVESTMENTS FOR RECEIVING SCHOOLS
Schools that receive additional students through consolidation will be allocated a proportion of the total programmatic
savings that result from Rightsizing actions. This allocation will be distributed as discretionary FTE that can be used for
instructional coaching, academic intervention, school climate support, social work, or duty support. School principals, with
consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.

School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
