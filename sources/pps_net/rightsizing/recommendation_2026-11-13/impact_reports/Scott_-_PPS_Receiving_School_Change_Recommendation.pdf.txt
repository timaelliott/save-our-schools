    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Scott would remain open as a neighborhood K-5 school and its boundary would expand to
         welcome neighborhood students from the closing Rose City Park K-5 school beginning in
         2028-29.
     ●   The Scott Spanish Dual Language Immersion (DLI) program would move to the Rigler K-5
         Spanish DLI regional hub beginning in 2027-28.
     ●   Rigler neighborhood students who opt-out of Spanish DLI will continue to attend
         Scott K-5.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   DLI program serving the area: Moving Spanish DLI to Rigler establishes Rigler as a focused
    regional Spanish language center.
●   Neighborhood program focus: Welcoming students from Rose City Park would result in a larger,
    more focused program.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Space balance: Moving Spanish DLI to Rigler created the space needed to welcome Rose City Park
    families.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Room to welcome new families: Moving Spanish DLI creates the internal building space needed to
    house Rose City Park's neighborhood student population.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●   Academic Programs: School year 2027-28: Based on current enrollment projections, there will be
    two classrooms at each grade-level, with blends not required. All classrooms will be neighborhood
    English Scholar classrooms. Students will receive PE, Arts, and Library/Media instruction each
    week. Based on enrollment, the school will be supported by a part-time counselor. School year
    2028-29: Scott will receive additional elementary students from Rose City Park, boosting total
    enrollment at each grade level.
●   Special Education: Currently, Scott has two Learning Center classes and one ISC class. With
    rightsizing, Scott's programming will not change.
●   Student and Family Supports: Happy Valley is identified as Scott's school-based mental health
    partner. Student Services is coordinating with the agency to review continuity of services for
    students transitioning with the Spanish immersion program to Rigler. Depending on country and
    district budgets, the existing SUN program and other culturally-specific partnerships will remain
    intact.

REINVESTMENTS FOR RECEIVING SCHOOLS
Schools that receive additional students through consolidation will be allocated a proportion of the total programmatic
savings that result from Rightsizing actions. This allocation will be distributed as discretionary FTE that can be used for
instructional coaching, academic intervention, school climate support, social work, or duty support. School principals, with
consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.


  School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
  includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
  districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
  others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
  December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
