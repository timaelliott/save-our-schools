    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   César Chávez would change from a K-8 to a focused K-5 Spanish DLI school serving the
         Jefferson/Roosevelt region.
     ●   It would welcome Spanish DLI students from Beach, Sitton, and James John.
     ●   Neighborhood (non-DLI) K-5 students would move to Rosa Parks K-5.
     ●   Middle grade (6th-8th) students would move to George Middle School.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Complex school setup: Hosting multiple complex programs (DLI, neighborhood K-8, special
    education) on one school created unbalanced class sizes and operational issues.
●   Focused language programming: Moving to a single-focus K-5 DLI school creates a regional
    Spanish language program location.
●   Vibrant language culture: Creates a school-wide focus on reading and writing in Spanish and
    English where DLI teachers plan and work together in grade-level teams.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Full middle school options: 6th-8th graders move to George Middle School for broader electives,
    athletics, and middle school buildings.
●   Building Upgrades: K-5 neighborhood students moving to Rosa Parks will enjoy an updated
    building.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●   Academic Programs: All students will be in Spanish DLI (SDLI) classrooms. SDLI instruction will
    continue to follow the 90-10 model. Based on current enrollment projections, there will be three to
    four classrooms at each grade-level, with blends not required. Students will receive PE, Arts, and
    Library/Media instruction each week. The majority of staff in the building will be Spanish-bilingual.
    Based on enrollment, the school will be supported by a full-time counselor.
●   Special Education: Currently, César Chávez is an Inclusive School Community and has 2.5 Learning
    center classes. With Rightsizing, César Chávez will remain an Inclusive School Community and is
    projected to have three Learning Center Classes. César Chávez will continue to use an inclusive
    school community special education service model. Students served in the 6-8 program will
    transfer with their general education peers and will be served in the Learning Center program at
    the middle school. Students in the K-5 English Scholars program who receive special education
    services will transfer with their general education peers to Rosa Parks. Rosa Parks also implements
    the inclusive special education service model and will continue to do so with students from César
    Chávez and Peninsula.
●   Student and Family Supports: Currently, Multnomah County School-Based Mental Health Partner is
    at César Chávez. Student Services is working with mental health partners to review capacity and
    continuity of services for students joining the expanded Spanish immersion program. As district
    and county budgets allow, the Latino Network SUN program and other culturally-specific
    partnerships will continue to support the school community. Families already enrolled in St. John's

REINVESTMENTS FOR RECEIVING SCHOOLS
Schools that receive additional students through consolidation will be allocated a proportion of the total programmatic
savings that result from Rightsizing actions. This allocation will be distributed as discretionary FTE that can be used for
instructional coaching, academic intervention, school climate support, social work, or duty support. School principals, with
consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.


  School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
  includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
  districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
  others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
  December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
