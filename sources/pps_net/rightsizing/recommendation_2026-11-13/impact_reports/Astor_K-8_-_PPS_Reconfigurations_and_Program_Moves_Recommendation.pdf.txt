Astor K-8                                                                      Changes beginning in

    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Astor would remain open as a neighborhood K-8 school. Astor would receive students
         from the Bybee Lake Shelter through a minor boundary adjustment with Faubion.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Room for more students: Astor has space in its building to welcome additional students in the
    region.
●   Serving local shelter needs: Including the Bybee Lake Shelter boundary makes sure those students
    go to a school as close as possible to where they live.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Keeping K-8 open: Astor remains one of the few K-8 neighborhood schools because nearby
    middle schools do not currently have room to take Astor's middle grade students.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Shorter commutes: Students residing near Bybee Lake Shelter would gain direct access to a
    neighborhood school much closer to home.
●   Keep K-8 model: Families keep their established K-8 school structure.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●     Academic Programs: Based on current enrollment projections, there will be up to two sections at
      each grade level. Blends may be required. Students will receive PE, Arts, and Library/Media
      instruction each week. Based on enrollment, the school will be supported by a full-time counselor.
●     Special Education: Currently Astor is an Inclusive School Community with four Learning Spaces.
      This model will remain the same for the 2027-2028 school year.
●     Student and Family Supports: Currently, Astor offers before and after school programming.
      Our RESJ team is exploring an expansion of childcare capacity at Astor. Currently, there is no SUN
      programming at Astor. Continued or expanding SUN programming will depend on County, PPS,
      and City budget process. Currently, there are two partners at Astor. RESJ partnerships continuing
      or expanding will be dependent on the PPS budget process. Currently, Cascadia Health is
      identified as Astor's school-based mental health partner for 2026–27.


    School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
    includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
    districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
    guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
    others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
    onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
    and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
    December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
    working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
