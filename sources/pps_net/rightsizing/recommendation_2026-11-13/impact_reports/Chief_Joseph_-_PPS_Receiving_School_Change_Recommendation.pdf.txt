    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Chief Joseph would remain open as a neighborhood K-5 school.
     ●   Chief Joseph would expand its school boundary to welcome 100% of the closing Beach
         neighborhood school area.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Central location and updated school: Chief Joseph sits in a central location to serve both
    communities and features better building conditions than Beach.
●   Better use of space: Merging Chief Joseph and Beach student communities helps keep student
    enrollment numbers sustainable for both communities.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Chief Joseph chosen over Beach: Chief Joseph was selected as the receiving school over Beach
    because it is closer to where students live, even though Beach had a slightly more room for
    students.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Upgraded learning environments: Students learn in improved classrooms equipped with focused
    special education spaces and roof improvements funded by recent bonds.
●   Central community gathering place: Families gain a central school location that keeps travel times
    reasonable across the combined boundary.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●   Academic Programs: Based on current enrollment projections, there will be two to four classrooms
    at each grade-level, with blends not required. Chief Joseph currently has blended classrooms and
    Beach does not. The Academics team will work with educators to plan curriculum for students who
    are in blends during the 2026-27 school year and may not be during the 2027-28 school year.
    Students will receive PE, Arts, and Library/Media instruction each week. Based on enrollment, the
    school will be supported by a full-time counselor.
●   Special Education: Currently, Chief Joseph has one Learning Center class. With Rightsizing, Chief
    Joseph will continue to have one Learning Center class.
●   Student and Family Supports: Currently, Happy Valley Counseling is the School-Based Mental
    Health Partner at Chief Joseph. Student Services is reviewing how existing partnership services will
    support students as district school-change plans are finalized. Our childcare team is exploring an
    expansion of childcare capacity at Chief Joseph.

REINVESTMENTS FOR RECEIVING SCHOOLS
Schools that receive additional students through consolidation will be allocated a proportion of the total programmatic
savings that result from Rightsizing actions. This allocation will be distributed as discretionary FTE that can be used for
instructional coaching, academic intervention, school climate support, social work, or duty support. School principals, with

consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.


  School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
  includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
  districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
  others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
  December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
