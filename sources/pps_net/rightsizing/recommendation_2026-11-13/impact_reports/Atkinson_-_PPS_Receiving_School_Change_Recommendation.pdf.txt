    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Atkinson would remain open as a neighborhood K-5 school.
     ●   Atkinson would welcome neighborhood K-5 students from Creston.
     ●   The Spanish Dual Language Immersion (DLI) program would move to Lent K-5.
     ●   Middle school pathway: All students in the new Atkinson boundary would go together to
         Kellogg for middle school and continue to Franklin for high school.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Creating space for neighborhood students: Moving Spanish DLI to Lent creates the physical space
    needed at Atkinson for the Atkinson and Creston student communities.
●   DLI Schools serving each area: Spanish DLI is moved into schools serving each area of the district.
    For southeast Portland, this is Lent.
●   Closer to Kellogg: Creston students north of Powell live closer to Atkinson and Kellogg Middle
    School.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Atkinson's central location: Atkinson was chosen to stay open over Creston because it is larger and
    more central to where families live in the new boundary.
●   Lent DLI school serving the area: Lent was chosen as the DLI school serving the area in the
    Southeast because it already functions as a full immersion school and families accessing Spanish
    DLI in this part of town live closer to Lent than Atkinson.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Safer, modern classrooms: Atkinson provides upgraded learning spaces.
●   Concentrated DLI program: Moving Spanish DLI to Lent builds a focused language center with full
    teacher collaboration.
●   Smooth middle and high school moves: Clear pathways keep friend groups together from
    elementary all the way through Franklin High School.

ENROLLMENT AND TRANSFER

●   Neighborhood students: K-4th grade students and incoming K students residing in the Creston
    E.S. attendance area will enroll at Atkinson E.S. 5th grade students who live in the Atkinson
    attendance area will enroll at Kellogg M.S. for 6th grade.
●   PPS transfer students: K-4th grade students attending Atkinson E.S. Spanish Immersion will enroll
    at Lent E.S. Spanish Immersion or may transfer to their neighborhood school by home address.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●   Academic Programs: Based on current enrollment projections, there will be two to three
    classrooms at each grade-level. Blends may be required at the 4th-5th grade band. Currently,
    Atkinson and Creston have blended classrooms in different grade bands. The Academics team will
    work with educators to plan curriculum for students who are in blends during the 2026-27 school
    year and may not be during the 2027-28 school year. All classrooms will be neighborhood English
    Scholar classrooms. Students will receive PE, Arts, and Library/Media instruction each week. Based
    on enrollment, the school will be supported by a full-time counselor. PPS Pre-K located at Creston
    Annex will remain unchanged.
●   Special Education: Currently, Atkinson has one Learning Center and two ISC Classrooms.
    With Rightsizing, Atkinson will maintain two ISC classrooms and one to two Learning Center
    Spaces, totaling four Special Education spaces.
●   Student and Family Supports: Currently, Atkinson offers before and after school programming.
    Our RESJ team is exploring an expansion of childcare capacity at Atkinson. Currently, there is no
    SUN programming at Atkinson. Continued or expanding SUN programming will depend on
    County, PPS and City budget process. Currently, there is one partner at Atkinson. RESJ
    partnerships continuing or expanding will be dependent on the PPS budget process. Currently,
    Atkinson and Creston maintain school-based mental health partnerships with Happy Valley
    Counseling.

REINVESTMENTS FOR RECEIVING SCHOOLS
Schools that receive additional students through consolidation will be allocated a proportion of the total programmatic
savings that result from Rightsizing actions. This allocation will be distributed as discretionary FTE that can be used for
instructional coaching, academic intervention, school climate support, social work, or duty support. School principals, with
consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.


  School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
  includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
  districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
  others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
  December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
