    WHAT’S CHANGING?

     ●   Sabin School would close as a school building.
     ●   Its full neighborhood K-5 school boundary would move to Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.
         Elementary.
     ●   Special Education students receiving Learning Center support would continue to receive
         support at Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Elementary in their learning center programming.
         Students in Sabin’s Social Emotional Skills program would also receive services in K–5 SES
         classrooms at Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., with individual placements determined by the
         Special Education Department in collaboration with school teams and families.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Low enrollment and building risks: Sabin's enrollment is expected to remain low (235 students),
    and the building faces major plumbing and heating system replacement needs, alongside
    dependencies on leased spaces for early learning.
●   Upgraded school features: Bringing together Sabin and Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Elementary
    moves students to a building with a newly upgraded roof, larger grounds (4.9 acres), and other
    strengthened school features.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Building capacity at Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.: Space capacity was evaluated to ensure the
    Mandarin Dual Language Immersion program could remain and also welcome Sabin families.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Better school grounds: Sabin students gain access to modern turfed fields, large outdoor spaces,
    and an updated roof.
●   Unified services: Elementary programs and early childhood services exist together under one roof.

ENROLLMENT AND TRANSFER

 ●   Neighborhood students: K-4th grade students and incoming K students residing in the Sabin
     attendance area will enroll at Dr. Martin Luther King Jr E.S.
 ●   PPS transfer students: K-4th grade students attending Sabin but not residing in the attendance
     area will enroll at Dr. Martin Luther King Jr E.S. or may transfer to their neighborhood school by
     home address.

School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
