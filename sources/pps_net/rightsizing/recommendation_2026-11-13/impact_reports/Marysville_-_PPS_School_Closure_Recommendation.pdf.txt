Marysville E.S.                                                                  Changes beginning in
                                                                                      2028-29




    WHAT’S CHANGING?

     ●   Marysville School would close as a K-5 school.
     ●   The neighborhood K-5 school boundary would move to Arleta K-5.
     ●   Marysville’s Inclusive School Community with three Learning Center Classes would move
         to Arleta K-5.
     ●   Lent neighborhood students who do not attend Lent Spanish Immersion would enroll at
         Kelly E.S.
     ●   The PPS Pre-K program at Marysville would move to Kelly.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Building size advantages: Arleta has room for 653 students (with 28 main classrooms) compared to
    Marysville's space of 410, allowing Arleta to comfortably house both communities.
●   Options for future building use: Future use of the Marysville facility would be considered through
    the Building Repurposing process and in alignment with the Oregon Seismic Rehabilitation Grant
    Program (SRGP) grant.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Avoiding multiple moves: Delaying full implementation ensures students previously impacted by
    Southeast Guiding Coalition changes do not experience multiple school moves during elementary
    school.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Updated Arleta school: Students gain new HVAC heating and cooling systems (Summer 2027) and
    full elevator access at Arleta.
●   Kept early learning: Moving Pre-K to Kelly preserves focused early childhood care in the area.

ENROLLMENT AND TRANSFER

 ●   Neighborhood students: K-4th grade students and incoming K students residing in the Marysville
     attendance area will enroll at Arleta E.S.
 ●   PPS transfer students: K-4th grade students attending Marysville but not residing in the
     attendance area will be enrolled at Arleta E.S. or may transfer to their neighborhood school by
     home address.

School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
