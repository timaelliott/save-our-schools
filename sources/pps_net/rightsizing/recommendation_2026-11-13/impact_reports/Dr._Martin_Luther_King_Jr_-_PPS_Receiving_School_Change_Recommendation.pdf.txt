    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Elementary would remain open as a neighborhood K-5 school.
     ●   The neighborhood boundary would expand to welcome 100% of the closing Sabin school
         area.
     ●   The Mandarin Dual Language Immersion (DLI) program remains at Dr. Martin Luther King
         Jr. Elementary.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Elementary features a large 4.9-acre site, turfed field, large building (618
    students), and a newly upgraded roof.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Fixing low enrollment: Combining two Sabin and Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. student communities
    build long-term sustainable student enrollment numbers for the school community.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Upgraded school buildings: Incoming Sabin students gain access to modern turfed fields, large
    outdoor spaces, and an updated roof.
●   Supporting the school over time: Expanding Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.'s historically small boundary
    increases student enrollment to sustainable levels.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●   Academic Programs: Based on current enrollment projections, there will be three to four
    classrooms at each grade-level. Blends may be required. Sabin currently has blended classrooms
    and Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. does not. The Academics team will work with educators to plan
    curriculum for students who are in blends during the 2026-27 school year and may not be during
    the 2027-28 school year. The co-located Mandarin DLI program currently at Dr. MLK Jr. will remain
    intact. Students will receive PE, Arts, and Library/Media instruction each week. Based on
    enrollment, the school will be supported by a full-time counselor. Two of the 3 PPS Pre-K
    classrooms currently located at Dr. MLK Jr., including the Black Excellence Pre-K classroom, will
    remain at this site. The third PPS Pre-K classroom will be relocated to another NE site that already
    has an established Pre-K foundation. The exact relocation site will be determined through a
    community needs and space analysis.
●   Special Education: Currently, Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. has one Learning Center class. With
    rightsizing, one of the SES classrooms currently housed at Sabin will move with the Sabin program
    to Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. The special education program will expand to include one SES
    classroom and one Learning Center.
●   Student and Family Supports: NARA Northwest and Happy Valley Counseling are identified as the
    School-Based Mental Health Partners at Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Student Services is reviewing
    continuity of support for incoming Sabin students. As district and county budgets allow, the SEI
    SUN program and other culturally-specific partnerships will continue to support the school
    community. Our childcare team is exploring an expansion of childcare capacity at Dr. Martin Luther
    King Jr.

REINVESTMENTS FOR RECEIVING SCHOOLS
Schools that receive additional students through consolidation will be allocated a proportion of the total programmatic
savings that result from Rightsizing actions. This allocation will be distributed as discretionary FTE that can be used for



                                                    PPS.NET/RIGHTSIZE                           Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. E.S.
instructional coaching, academic intervention, school climate support, social work, or duty support. School principals, with
consultation from their school communities and approval from their Senior Directors of Instructional Leadership, will
determine how to use these additional investments.


  School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
  includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
  districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
  guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
  others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
  onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
  and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
  December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
  working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
