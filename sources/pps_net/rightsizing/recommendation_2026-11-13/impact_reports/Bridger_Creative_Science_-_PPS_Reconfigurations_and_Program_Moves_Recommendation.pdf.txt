    WHAT’S HAPPENING?

     ●   Bridger Creative Science would change from a K-8 to a K-5 school.
     ●   The school would keep both its neighborhood school area and its Creative Science
         special-focus choice program.
     ●   Current middle grade students would be reassigned to Harrison Park Middle School or
         could choose to return to their neighborhood middle school.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●   Steady student numbers: Changing to K-5 boosts middle school enrollment in neighborhood
    schools while protecting the Creative Science unique learning program.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●   Preserving student enrollment: Keeping both the neighborhood area and the special-focus choice
    program protects student enrollment and honors the school's unique community history.
●   Southeast Guiding Coalition: Because of many recent changes, the decision was made to preserve
    the special-focus choice portion of Bridger Creative Science.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●   Focused programming: Elementary students benefit from a school and playground designed for
    younger children.
●   Richer middle school options: 6th-8th grade students gain access to broader middle school
    electives and learning options at a comprehensive middle school.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●     Academic Programs: School year 2028-29: Bridger Creative Science will house only students in
      grades K-5. Based on current enrollment projections, there will be 2 classrooms at each grade
      level. Students will receive PE, Arts, and Library/Media instruction each week. Based on
      enrollment, the school will be supported by a part-time counselor.
●     Special Education: Currently, Bridger has 2.5 Learning Center classes. There are no changes to this
      model for the 2027-2028 school year.
●     Student and Family Supports: Currently, Morrison Child and Family Services is the Bridger Creative
      Science's School-Based Mental Health Partner.


    School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
    includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
    districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
    guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
    others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
    onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
    and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
    December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
    working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
