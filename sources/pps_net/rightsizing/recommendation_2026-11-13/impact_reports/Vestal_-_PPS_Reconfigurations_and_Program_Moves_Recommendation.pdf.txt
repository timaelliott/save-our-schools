     WHAT’S HAPPENING?

      ●​   Vestal would remain open as a neighborhood K-5 school and receive the Vietnamese Dual
           Language Immersion (VDLI) program from Rose City Park.




WHY IS PPS CONSIDERING THIS?

●​   Reaching the district’s school size goal: Vestal is below the district enrollment goal if there are no
     changes.
●​   Proximity for VDLI families: Vietnamese DLI community lives close to Vestal, and adding the
     program brings Vestal above the district enrollment goal.



WHAT ELSE WAS CONSIDERED?

●​   Building conditions and location: Building conditions and proximity were major considerations in
     this decision.



WHAT WOULD STUDENTS GAIN?

●​   Upgraded school building: Vestal has seen recent building investments and this option keeps the
     same middle and high school pathway.

ACADEMIC PROGRAM TRANSITIONS

●​   Academic Programs: School year 2028-29: Vestal will maintain its neighborhood English Scholar
     program and welcome the Vietnamese Dual Language Immersion program from Rose City Park.
     Based on current enrollment projections, this program relocation will result in three to four
     classrooms at each grade level, with some number of blends likely. Students will receive PE, Arts,
     and Library/Media instruction each week. Based on enrollment, the school will be supported by a
     full-time counselor.
●​   Special Education: Currently, Vestal has 1.5 Learning Center classes and two ISC classes. ​
     With rightsizing, Vestal's programming does not make any changes.
●​   Student and Family Supports: Morrison Child and Family Services is identified as Vestal's
     school-based mental health partner. Student Services is reviewing how existing partnership services
     can support students as district school-change plans are finalized. Our childcare team is exploring
     an expansion of childcare capacity at Vestal.


 School transition supports: Teams from Central Office have developed a framework for implementing Rightsizing. This
 includes a menu of transition supports for all impacted school communities. This work is informed by lessons learned by
 districts across the country that have had successful school consolidations. The implementation framework includes
 guidance on merging academic programs and school-based teams, (such as Instructional Leadership, Climate, PTAs and
 others) tools, resources, and timelines. The framework includes timelines for merging school communities and
 onboarding staff, families and students to their new campuses. It also includes staffing timelines and plans for packing
 and moving furniture and instructional resources. This comprehensive framework will be shared with the community in
 December. If the Board of Education votes to proceed with Rightsizing for fall 2027, Central Office teams will begin
 working with school communities in January to implement transition supports appropriate to each community.
