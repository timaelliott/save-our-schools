RIGHTSIZING

Superintendent’s
Recommendation Summary
Projected declines in student enrollment at Portland Public Schools, coupled with a looming $65
million budget deficit, require immediate action. The final recommendation proposes closing and
consolidating 10 elementary schools, phased over two years. The proposal also includes
changing several K-8 schools to K-5, moving Spanish Dual Language Immersion programs into
four “hubs,” and changing school boundaries to reflect the new configurations. These steps will
provide more stability to the district and ensure all students receive the educational resources
and supports they need to succeed.

The proposal contains notable changes from prior Scenarios A and B. It will close fewer schools,
relocate less programs, and spread closures over two years. These changes were made after
hearing concerns raised by students, families and other PPS communities. The proposal will now
go to the Board of Education, which will continue gathering public input before voting on
December 15.


  CHANGES IN RESPONSE TO COMMUNITY FEEDBACK
      Sellwood Middle School, James John, Maplewood, and Stephenson will remain open.
      Sunnyside remains an environmental focus school, but converts to K-5.
      Skyline students will continue on to Lincoln High School rather than to Roosevelt.
      Odyssey K-8 choice program moves to Forest Park instead of Metropolitan Learning
      Center.
      The Mandarin DLI program currently housed at Dr. MLK Jr. remains at its current location.
      Lewis, Marysville, Woodmere, and Rose City Park will close in Fall 2028 instead of Fall
      2027.


THE FOLLOWING CLOSURES AND CONSOLIDATIONS HELP ENSURE EACH SCHOOL IS
FULLY ENROLLED WITH STABLE AND ADEQUATE RESOURCES.

This includes:

       Stable staffing, with qualified educators and specialists and fewer educators split across
       multiple buildings or roles.
       Rigorous, culturally responsive core academics and elective offerings, including arts, world
       language, physical education, athletics, interventions and acceleration.
       Safe, healthy, and comfortable facilities.

Closures and consolidations
FALL 2027

   Beach closes and students move to Chief Joseph with a projected enrollment of 441. Beach students who wish to
   continue in Spanish DLI move to Cesar Chavez.
   Peninsula closes and students move to Rosa Parks with a projected enrollment of 481. Cesar Chavez English
   Scholar students also move to Rosa Parks.
   Sabin closes and students move to Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. with a projected enrollment of 424.
   Creston closes and students move to Atkinson with a projected enrollment of 474. Atkinson students who wish to
   continue in Spanish DLI move to Lent.
   Creston’s Deaf and Hard of Hearing students move to Glencoe with a projected enrollment of 312.
   Buckman closes and students move to Abernethy and Sunnyside K-5 with projected enrollments of 312 and 480.
   Beverly Cleary K-5 merges with Irvington and remains at Irvington for one year while Beverly Cleary undergoes
   seismic reinforcement. The merger’s projected enrollment is 502.
   Scott students who wish to continue in Spanish DLI move to Rigler.
   Sitton students who wish to continue in Spanish DLI move to Cesar Chavez.
   Metropolitan Learning Center becomes a K-8 and grades 9-12 move to Alliance or their neighborhood high
   school.
Additionally, six K-8 schools (Beverly Cleary; Cesar Chavez; Faubion; Laurelhurst; Sunnyside; and Vernon) convert to K-
5, with grades 6-8 moving to nearby middle schools.


FALL 2028

   Rose City Park closes and English Scholar students move to Scott with a projected enrollment of 489. Students
   who wish to continue in the Vietnamese DLI program move to Vestal.
   Lewis closes and students move to Duniway with a projected enrollment of 533.
   Marysville closes and students move to Arleta with a projected enrollment of 415.
   Woodmere closes and students move to Whitman with a projected enrollment of 444.
   Students enrolled in Mandarin DLI at Clark who wish to continue in this program move to Woodstock.
   Beverly Cleary reopens and the merged Beverly Cleary-Irvington relocates to Beverly Cleary with their classmates.

Additionally, one K-8 school (Bridger) converts to K-5, with grades 6-8 moving to nearby middle schools.
