The PPS Board of Education determines whether schools will be closed. Board
determination on school closures is based on the Superintendent's recommendation.
The School Initiation & Closure policy (6.10.030-P) requires that the Superintendent's
recommendation be accompanied by a School Closure Report.

1a. School Description
Creston is a neighborhood K–5 school serving the Creston-Kenilworth neighborhood of
Southeast Portland, adjacent to Creston Park and Pool. The school partners with Columbia
Regional Inclusive Services to provide programming for students who are deaf and hard of
hearing and shares space with PPS’s Head Start program in the Creston Annex. Approximately
240 students are enrolled at Creston for the 2026-27 school year.

1b. Financial Impact
The primary financial benefit of consolidating elementary schools and programs is to align
school capacity and operating costs with enrollment, allowing PPS to concentrate limited
resources in fewer, more efficiently resourced schools. Combining school communities can
reduce duplicative school-level administrative and operating expenses, improve building
utilization, and support more efficient staffing and program delivery. These consolidations are
intended to generate ongoing savings while supporting strong core instructional programs and
student services.

If the school remains open, the projected cost per student for the 2027/2028 school year is:
$31,194.

If the school is closed in 2027, the projected overall savings is $1,085,010. This includes savings
from administrative, custodial, and office staffing, 80% of utilities and includes the one-time cost
of teacher moves. We anticipate ongoing expenses, including 20% of current utility costs and an
average annual security cost, if the school were closed and unoccupied.

Under the proposed change, 41% of students would live more than one mile from Atkinson and
would be offered school bus transportation. To accommodate these students, PPS anticipates
increasing service to Atkinson from two to four buses. The Transportation Department
estimates an additional annual cost of approximately $17,000.

Transportation and Safe Routes to School planning will identify needed supports for safe arrival
and dismissal, including walking and biking improvements, crossing supports, or traffic
circulation changes.

History of Requested and Actual Transfers
During 2024-2025, 2025-2026, and 2026-2027 school years, 43 students requested transfers
into Creston and 19 enrolled through an approved transfer. During the same period, 131
students residing within the school’s attendance area requested transfers to other schools, and
49 enrolled elsewhere through an approved transfer.

Projected declining enrollment will make it difficult to maintain stable staffing, full programming,
and essential student support services. Under enrollment-based staffing allocations, low
enrollment can result in year-to-year staffing changes, blended-grade classrooms or combined
programs, and reduced access to enrichment and student support offerings. Staff may serve
multiple roles or split time across schools, limiting continuity and availability. While educators
would continue working to meet students’ needs, projected enrollment would constrain the
school’s ability to provide consistent access to a well-rounded educational experience without
ongoing supplemental district resources.

Physical Condition of Building, Capital and Environmental Needs
Creston is a single-story International style school with a separate Creston Annex. The HVAC
equipment and much of the electrical system are beyond their typical design lives. Creston has
no fire-suppression system, weathered exterior wood trim, deteriorating mortar and separation

Building Use
Currently, the campus houses a K–5 neighborhood school, regional Deaf and Hard of Hearing
programming through Columbia Regional Inclusive Services, and preschool in the Creston
Annex, which would remain open as a preschool center.

Analysis of current and potential use of the facility, including recommended disposition
Following closure, potential uses of the Creston Elementary School facility will be evaluated
through the district’s Building Repurposing process, informed by community engagement,
district needs, facility conditions, and financial considerations. This process will consider
opportunities to support students and the surrounding community and identify a recommended
future use or disposition for Board consideration.
2b. Student Reassignment
The proposed closure of Creston as a K–5 school would reassign neighborhood students,
including Special Education Learning Center students, to Atkinson and relocate the regional
Deaf and Hard of Hearing program to Glencoe. The neighborhood pathway would continue
through Kellogg Middle School and Franklin High School, while Glencoe would connect the DHH
program to its Mt. Tabor Middle School pathway. The Creston Annex would remain open as a
preschool center.

Transportation options and barriers
Transportation options include walking, biking, family transportation, and school bus service for
elementary students who live more than one mile from their neighborhood school; additional
students may qualify based on Oregon Department of Education guidelines or individual needs.
We do not anticipate any barriers to students getting to school; however, additional bussing
may be provided, as needed, for hazard areas within the school attendance boundary.

How will this school location accommodate the reassigned students?
Atkinson has 25 regular classrooms and a functional capacity of 519 students. After
consolidation with Creston, Atkinson is projected to hold 474 students for the 2027-2028
school year.

3. Community/Cluster and District Impact Analysis

3a. Cluster Analysis

How will the proposed closure maintain or improve equity of access to schools offering high
quality educational choices?

In the 2026-2027 school year, PPS staffs and supports 56 elementary programs. Eleven of these
programs are situated within K-8 campuses. The enrollment of these programs ranges from 160
students to over 550 students. Using the QEM and our system definition of “well-resourced
schools” as a guide, we have established an elementary enrollment of 360 as the minimum
threshold to be able to staff and support a comprehensive and stable elementary program.
Maximizing the number of sites at or above this minimum threshold allows the district to
maximize the number of students who experience grade-level classrooms, comprehensive core
enrichments, and targeted academic supports. It also maximizes the number of educators who
work full-time on one campus and who benefit from regular collaboration with grade- and
content-alike colleagues.

By operating fewer elementary schools and spreading current enrollment more evenly across
remaining sites, the district can offer a more comprehensive and sustainable program at each
elementary site.

In recent years, we have used resources from other parts of our system to subsidize elementary
programs with low enrollment. With the revenue shortfall we have recently experienced and
anticipate continuing to experience, our capacity to subsidize underenrollment has diminished
and elementary schools have already experienced the following reductions and programmatic
constraints:

    ●​ Over half of our elementary programs have been forced to place students in multi-grade
       classrooms;
    ●​ Staffing for instructional coaching, academic coaching, and school climate support
       positions have been reduced;
    ●​ Core enrichment teachers that support Arts, PE, and Library/Media instruction have been
       split across multiple sites, reducing the capacity to have full-school enrichment
       programs;
    ●​ Support staff that help monitor lunch and recess have been reduced, forcing principals
       to spend much of their time monitoring duty;

3b. Effects on Other Schools, Enrollment, and Community Access
The proposed merger would affect Atkinson and Glencoe within the Franklin cluster. Enrollment
at Atkinson is projected to change from 366 to 474 in 2027. Enrollment at Glencoe is projected
to change from 339 to 312 in 2027. Atkinson is ready to receive the new students, with minor
upgrades in the kitchen, including a new warming cabinet and milk cooler. Glencoe will need an
updated double oven and warming cabinet in the kitchen, as well as some accessibility
upgrades, such as digital readerboard upgrades to support the incoming Deaf and Hard of
Hearing program.

Creston is located approximately 1–1.5 miles from Atkinson, depending on the route. Nearby
schools and community resources include Franklin High School, Clinton Park, and Champions
childcare at Atkinson. The proposed change would shift students’ daily school activities toward
Atkinson and Clinton Park, while potentially increasing travel to existing childcare arrangements
and resources near Creston. Transportation options include walking, biking, family
transportation, school bus service for eligible students, and public transit along the Powell and
Division corridors. Potential barriers include longer walking distances and crossings of SE
Powell Boulevard and other busy streets. PPS would support school bus service for students
who live more than one mile from the school and work with the school community and the
district Safe Routes to School coordinator to identify appropriate crossing, pedestrian, and
bicycle safety measures.

3c. Community Partnerships and Potential Community Impact
Creston currently partners with Grow Portland, which teaches a gardening class during the
school day. This program is not funded through the Racial Equity & Social Justice (RESJ)
department; schools contribute to program costs through a sliding fee scale based on their
percentage of students eligible for free and reduced-price meals. Closure could affect student
access to this programming. Opportunities for the partnership to continue include Atkinson,
which already offers Grow Portland, and the program is expected to expand to serve Creston
students projected to transition there. Services requiring further coordination include working
with Grow Portland and Atkinson to plan for the program’s expansion. Additional community
impacts are expected to be limited for this partnership, with proposed responses including
coordinating with Atkinson’s building leaders and Grow Portland to support a smooth transition.
Future community use of the facility would be considered through the Building Repurposing
process.

Creston currently partners with Champions to provide child care for approximately 50 students.
Relocating the program to Atkinson would allow families to maintain their existing provider
relationship, as Champions already serves approximately 45 students at Atkinson. However,
combining the programs would increase enrollment to approximately 95 students, requiring
additional appropriately licensed space, staffing, and operational resources. We ask that

Champions and Atkinson administration be included in early transition planning to identify
sufficient space, evaluate facility modifications and licensing requirements, and develop a
sustainable plan that accommodates both school communities. Clear communication and
adequate planning time will be essential to minimize disruptions for families and protect
existing child care services. Preserving this partnership requires ensuring Champions has the
space and support necessary to continue serving all 95 students without compromising the
quality or availability of care.

Creston currently partners with Impact NW to operate its SUN Community School, providing
wraparound services and family support through collaboration among PPS, Multnomah County
and the City of Portland. Impact NW provides free extended-day enrichment opportunities,
culturally relevant programming supported by staff who reflect and understand the student
population, and access to basic-needs resources, including a food pantry. Additional SUN
services specific to Creston include Big Group Games recess support, Creston Community
Mutual Aid (CCMA), Urban Gleaners food distributions, winter gift shop programming, resource
navigation and utility assistance, summer programming, and community-building events such
as BIPOC Family Night, STEAM Night, and community yoga classes. SUN serves as an
important connection point for students and families to access enrichment, individualized
support, community resources, and basic-needs assistance. Closure could significantly disrupt
student and family access to these established services, trusted relationships, and community
partnerships, while also affecting Impact NW's ability to provide consistent, school-based
support to the Creston community. Opportunities for partnerships to continue would require
further coordination, as students would transition to Atkinson, which does not currently operate
a SUN Community School. Unlike transitions to schools with an existing SUN program, there is
no established SUN service infrastructure at Atkinson to absorb Creston's existing
programming, staffing, partnerships, and family support services.

Services requiring alternatives or further coordination include identifying how students and
families will continue accessing wraparound services, food distributions, resource navigation,
enrichment opportunities, summer programming, and community-based supports currently
provided through Impact NW and its partners. Additional community impacts include the
potential loss of an established access point for families experiencing barriers to basic needs,
increased challenges navigating community resources, disruption of trusted relationships with
SUN staff, and reduced access to enrichment and engagement opportunities that support
student well-being and academic success. Proposed responses include a coordinated district
effort with Impact NW, Atkinson school leadership, and community partners to identify students
and families currently accessing Creston SUN services, establish warm handoffs to alternative
resources, assess opportunities to maintain existing partnerships and programming at other
locations, and identify potential service gaps requiring additional support or funding. Particular
consideration should be given to ensuring families do not lose access to essential basic-needs
resources and that students maintain connections to meaningful enrichment and community
supports following the transition.

Any future community use of the facility would be considered through the Building Repurposing
process.
