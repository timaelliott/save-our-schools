School Closure Process
The PPS Board of Education determines whether schools will be closed. Board
determination on school closures is based on the Superintendent's recommendation.
The School Initiation & Closure policy (6.10.030-P) requires that the Superintendent's
recommendation be accompanied by a School Closure Report.

1a. School Description
Marysville is a K–5 neighborhood school in the Foster-Powell neighborhood of Southeast
Portland. Marysville is an Inclusive School Community, serving students with disabilities in their
neighborhood school and hosts a PPS Pre-K program. Built in 1921, Marysville is a single story
building with 25 classrooms. Approximately 285 students are enrolled at Marysville for the
2026-2027 school year.

1b. Financial Impact
The primary financial benefit of consolidating elementary schools and programs is to align
school capacity and operating costs with enrollment, allowing PPS to concentrate limited
resources in fewer, more efficiently resourced schools. Combining school communities can
reduce duplicative school-level administrative and operating expenses, improve building
utilization, and support more efficient staffing and program delivery. These consolidations are
intended to generate ongoing savings while supporting strong core instructional programs and
student services.

If the school remains open, the projected cost per student for the 2028-2029 school year is:
$20,401. (This is calculated using projected FY28 compensation levels and current staffing
practices, which could change in the near future due to budget uncertainty.)

If the school is closed in 2028, the projected overall savings is $1,180,128. This includes savings
from Administrative, Custodial, and Office staffing, 80% of utilities, and includes the one-time
cost of teacher moves. We anticipate ongoing expenses, including 20% of current utility costs
and an average annual security cost, if the school were closed and unoccupied.

To accommodate the students who live more than one mile from Arleta, PPS anticipates
increasing service to Arleta from one to three buses/routes. The Transportation Department
estimates an additional savings of approximately $128,000. The proposed change would reduce
the total number of buses serving the closing and receiving schools, resulting in transportation
cost savings.

Transportation and Safe Routes to School planning will identify needed supports for safe arrival
and dismissal, including walking and biking improvements, crossing supports, or traffic
circulation changes.

History of Requested and Actual Transfers
During the 2024-2025, 2025-2026, and 2026-2027 school years, 33 students requested transfers
into Marysville and 14 enrolled through an approved transfer. During the same period, 132
students residing within the school’s attendance area requested transfers to other schools, and
51 enrolled elsewhere through an approved transfer.

Projected declining enrollment will make it difficult to maintain stable staffing, full programming,
and essential student support services. Under enrollment-based staffing allocations, low
enrollment can result in year-to-year staffing changes, blended-grade classrooms or combined
programs, and reduced access to enrichment and student support offerings. Staff may serve
multiple roles or split time across schools, limiting continuity and availability. While educators
would continue working to meet students’ needs, projected enrollment would constrain the
school’s ability to provide consistent access to a well-rounded educational experience without
ongoing supplemental district resources.

Gross capacity is the estimated maximum number of students a school can accommodate,
calculated by assigning a student capacity to each classroom based on its square footage and
summing those capacities:
    ●​ Less than 800 square feet: 24 students
    ●​ 800–1,000 square feet: 27 students
    ●​ More than 1,000 square feet: 30 students

Functional capacity is a practical estimate of the number of students a school can
accommodate, calculated by multiplying its gross capacity by the applicable utilization rate. It
does not represent maximum occupancy under building code requirements or determine
staffing levels.

Physical Condition of Building, Capital and Environmental Needs
Marysville is a single-story courtyard campus with Colonial Revival architectural features.
Marysville is over 100 years old, and in poor condition with a facility condition index (FCI) score
of 0.19. Assessments conducted in 2019–2020 identified water heaters beyond typical design
life, an older boiler, and 10 electrical panels beyond typical design life despite substantial
electrical replacement in 2013. Exterior concrete steps were cracked or crumbling, wood
windows and doors were aged, and finishes in the older portion of the school were beyond
design life. More recent upgrades to Marysville include: exterior-door accessibility upgrades in
2017, radon mitigation in 2018–2019, and a seismic retrofit in 2025-2026.

ADA Accessibility
Marysville is a single-story building with an accessible entrance.

Building Use
Marysville houses a K–5 neighborhood Inclusive School Community and PPS Pre-K.

Analysis of current and potential use of the facility, including recommended disposition
Following closure, potential uses of the Marysville facility will be evaluated through the district’s
Building Repurposing process, informed by community engagement, district needs, facility
conditions, and financial considerations. This process will consider opportunities to support
students and the surrounding community and identify a recommended future use or disposition
for Board consideration. It is important to note that Marysville received an Oregon Seismic
Rehabilitation Grant Program (SRGP) grant in 2024 which requires that the building continue to
be used for a public educational program or school until ten years after the project completion
date.

2b. Student Reassignment
The proposed closure of Marysville as a K–5 school would assign students from the
pre-Southeast Guiding Coalition (SEGC) Marysville neighborhood boundary to Arleta and
students from the pre-SEGC Lent non-DLI neighborhood boundary to Kelly. PPS Pre-K would
move to Kelly. Arleta would be an Inclusive School Community and have a grades 3-5 Social
Emotional Skills program.

3. Community/Cluster and District Impact Analysis

3a. Cluster Analysis

How will the proposed closure maintain or improve equity of access to schools offering high
quality educational choices?
In the 2026-27 school year, PPS staffs and supports 56 elementary programs across the district.
Eleven of these programs are situated within K-8 campuses. The enrollment of these programs
ranges from 160 students to over 550 students. Using the QEM and our system definition of
“well-resourced schools” as a guide, we have established an elementary enrollment of 360 as
the minimum threshold to be able to staff and support a comprehensive and stable elementary
program. Maximizing the number of sites at or above this minimum threshold allows the district
to maximize the number of students who experience grade-level classrooms, comprehensive
core enrichments, and targeted academic supports. It also maximizes the number of educators
who work full-time on one campus and who benefit from regular collaboration with grade- and
content-alike colleagues.

By operating fewer elementary schools and spreading current enrollment more evenly across
remaining sites, the district can stabilize and offer a more comprehensive and sustainable
program at each elementary site.

In recent years, we have used resources from other parts of our system to subsidize elementary
programs with low enrollment. Given our current and projected budget shortfalls, we can no
longer sustain subsidies for underenrolled sites. As a result, elementary schools have already
faced several programmatic and operational constraints:
    ●​ Over half of our elementary programs have been forced to place students in multi-grade
        classrooms;
    ●​ Staffing for instructional coaching, academic coaching, and school climate support
        positions have been reduced;
    ●​ Core enrichment teachers that support Arts, PE, and Library/Media instruction have been
        split across multiple sites, reducing the capacity to have full-school enrichment
        programs;

3b. Effects on Other Schools, Enrollment, and Community Access
The proposed merger would affect Arleta within the Franklin cluster. Enrollment at Arleta is
projected to change from 230 students to 415 students in the 2028–2029 school year. Based on
current enrollment projections, there will be three to four classrooms at each grade level, with
blends not required. These changes would support a combined elementary school community,
with opportunities to stabilize staffing and sustain educational programs and student supports.
Implementation would require coordinated staffing, classroom assignments, and use of shared
spaces to accommodate incoming students while supporting students already enrolled. Arleta’s
instructional spaces are ready to accommodate incoming students. Moderate kitchen upgrades
are needed, including a double oven and warming cabinet. The school is also slated to receive
HVAC system upgrades funded through the Portland Clean Energy Community Benefits Fund
(PCEF).

Marysville is located approximately one mile from Arleta Elementary School. Nearby community
resources include Mt. Scott Park, Mt. Scott Community Center and Pool and Holgate Library.
The proposed change would shift students’ daily school activities toward Arleta. Transportation
options include walking, biking, family transportation, school bus service for eligible students,
and public transit. PPS would support school bus service for students who live more than one
mile from the school and work with the school community and the district Safe Routes to
School coordinator to identify appropriate crossing, pedestrian, and bicycle safety measures.

3c. Community Partnerships and Potential Community Impact
Marysville partners with Friends of the Children, Chess for Success, and POIC (Portland
Opportunities Industrialization Center). Closure could affect student access to these programs
as some students transition to Kelly. Opportunities for partnerships to continue include Friends
of the Children, which has a long-term commitment to each child regardless of school
placement; mentors (referred to as Friends) continue supporting students when they transition,
at no cost to the district. Services requiring alternatives or further coordination include Chess
for Success, which currently has strong student participation at Marysville, and POIC.
Continuation of both programs at Kelly will depend on available Racial Equity & Social Justice
(RESJ) funding and budget priorities. Additional community impacts include the possible loss of
programs students are actively engaged in, with proposed responses including working with
Kelly’s building leaders and school community to prioritize student needs and prepare leaders to
support these partnerships. Because consolidation concentrates partner supports in fewer
buildings, it may help lessen the impact of any budget reductions on students and families.

Marysville partners with the YMCA to provide child care for approximately 13 students, including
four families receiving Employment Related Day Care (ERDC) assistance. Relocating the
program to Arleta, which also partners with the YMCA and currently serves approximately 50

Marysville partners with Impact NW to operate its SUN Community School, providing
community-based services and wraparound family supports through collaboration among PPS,
Multnomah County, and the City of Portland. SUN provides free extended-day enrichment
opportunities, educational support and skill development, access to basic-needs resources,
family involvement and support, and connections to social, health, and mental health resources.

Additional programming includes recreational activities, enrichment opportunities, and family
and community events that strengthen relationships between students, families, the school, and
neighborhood organizations. Closure could affect student and family access to established SUN
services, disrupt trusted relationships with Impact NW staff, and require adjustments to existing
community partnerships and service delivery.

Opportunities for partnerships to continue include transitioning students to Arleta, which also
operates a SUN Community School through Portland Parks & Recreation. Although students
would continue to have access to an established SUN program, the transition would involve a
change in lead agency from Impact NW to Portland Parks & Recreation. This shift may affect
existing staff relationships, family-support coordination, community partnerships, enrichment
programming, and how families access wraparound resources.

Services requiring further coordination include assessing Arleta's capacity to accommodate
additional students and families, identifying which existing Impact NW partnerships and
enrichment opportunities can continue, coordinating the transfer of family referrals and
individualized support needs, and ensuring continued access to basic-needs resources and
community-based services. Additional community impacts include potential disruptions to
trusted relationships with SUN staff, changes in how families navigate and access community
resources, and the loss of established connections with Impact NW and its community partners.

Proposed responses include coordinating with Impact NW, Portland Parks & Recreation, and
Arleta school leadership to develop a transition plan, establish warm handoffs between
agencies, identify opportunities to maintain existing partnerships and programming, assess
staffing and service capacity, and communicate changes in service delivery and resource
access to affected families. Particular consideration should be given to maintaining continuity
of wraparound supports and ensuring that students and families experience a seamless
transition between lead agencies without losing access to essential community-based
resources.

All future community use of the facility would be considered through the Building Repurposing
process.
