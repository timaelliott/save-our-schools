1a. School Description
Irvington Elementary School is a K–5 neighborhood school, centered in the Irvington
neighborhood of Northeast Portland. Built in 1932, the two-story school has 30 classrooms and
has approximately 260 students enrolled in 2026-2027.

1b. Financial Impact
The primary financial benefit of consolidating elementary schools and programs is to align
school capacity and operating costs with enrollment, allowing PPS to concentrate limited
resources in fewer, more efficiently resourced schools. Combining school communities can
reduce duplicative school-level administrative and operating expenses, improve building
utilization, and support more efficient staffing and program delivery. These consolidations are
intended to generate ongoing savings while supporting strong core instructional programs and
student services.
If the school remains open, the projected cost per student for the 2027/2028 school year is:
$17,363.
If the school is closed in 2027, the projected overall savings is $899,830. This includes savings
from administrative, custodial, and office staffing, 80% of utilities, and includes the one-time
cost of teacher moves. We anticipate ongoing expenses, including 20% of current utility costs
and an average annual security cost, if the school were closed and unoccupied.
Under the proposed change, 30% of students would live more than one mile from Beverly Cleary
and would be offered school bus transportation. To accommodate these students, PPS
anticipates increasing service to Beverly Cleary from zero to two buses. The Transportation
Department estimates an additional annual cost of $50,000.

Transportation and Safe Routes to School planning will identify needed supports for safe arrival
and dismissal, including walking and biking improvements, crossing supports, or traffic
circulation changes. Transportation arrangements and costs for the temporary swing site will be
assessed separately and communicated to families before the transition.

History of Requested and Actual Transfers

During 2024-25, 2025-26, and 2026-27 school years, 44 students requested transfers into
Irvington, and 17 enrolled through an approved transfer. During the same period, 95 students
residing within the school’s attendance area requested transfers to other schools, and 31
enrolled elsewhere through an approved transfer.

Projected declining enrollment will make it difficult to maintain stable staffing, full programming,
and essential student support services. Under enrollment-based staffing allocations, low
enrollment can result in year-to-year staffing changes, blended-grade classrooms or combined
programs, and reduced access to enrichment and student support offerings. Staff may serve
multiple roles or split time across schools, limiting continuity and availability. While educators
would continue working to meet students’ needs, projected enrollment would constrain the
school’s ability to provide consistent access to a well-rounded educational experience without
ongoing supplemental district resources.

Gross capacity is the estimated maximum number of students a school can accommodate,
calculated by assigning a student capacity to each classroom based on its square footage and
summing those capacities:
    ●​ Less than 800 square feet: 24 students
    ●​ 800–1,000 square feet: 27 students
    ●​ More than 1,000 square feet: 30 students

Functional capacity is a practical estimate of the number of students a school can
accommodate, calculated by multiplying its gross capacity by the applicable utilization rate. It
does not represent maximum occupancy under building code requirements or determine
staffing levels.

The cost of school operations includes projected FY28 school-based costs in funds 100 and
200 plus Facilities, Nutrition Services, and Special Education, at the current service level.

Analysis of current and potential use of the facility, including recommended disposition
Following closure, potential uses of the Irvington facility will be evaluated through the district’s
Building Repurposing process, informed by community engagement, district needs, facility
conditions, and financial considerations. This process will consider opportunities to support
students and the surrounding community and identify a recommended future use or disposition
for Board consideration. If in-process feasibility assessments confirm, Irvington would likely be
used as a temporary swing-site during the 2027-2028 Beverly Cleary seismic work.

2b. Student Reassignment
The proposed closure of Irvington would reassign neighborhood K–5 students to Beverly Cleary.
The attendance area’s grades 6–8 pathway would shift from Harriet Tubman to Beaumont
Middle School. Special Education students receiving Learning Center support would continue to
receive support at Beverly Cleary K–5.

Transportation options and barriers
Transportation options include walking, biking, family transportation, and school bus service for
elementary students who live more than one mile from their neighborhood school; additional
students may qualify based on Oregon Department of Education guidelines or individual needs.
We do not anticipate any barriers to students getting to school; however, additional busing may
be provided, as needed, for hazard areas within the school attendance boundary.

How will this school location accommodate the reassigned students?
Beverly Cleary has 26 classrooms and a functional capacity of 531 students. After the
consolidation with Irvington, Beverly Cleary is projected to hold 502 students in the 2027-2028
school year.



 Recommendation
 PPS recommends closing Irvington as a neighborhood K–5 school for the 2027–2028 school
 year and assigning neighborhood K–5 students to Beverly Cleary. The attendance area’s
 middle school pathway would change from Harriet Tubman to Beaumont.

3. Community/Cluster and District Impact Analysis

3a. Cluster Analysis

How will the proposed closure maintain or improve equity of access to schools offering high
quality educational choices?
In the 2026-2027 school year, PPS staffs and supports 56 elementary programs. Eleven of these
programs are situated within K-8 campuses. Enrollment ranges from 160 students to over 550
students. Using the QEM and our system definition of “well-resourced schools” as a guide, we
have established an elementary enrollment of 360 as the minimum threshold to be able to staff
and support a comprehensive and stable elementary program. Maximizing the number of sites
at or above this minimum threshold allows the district to maximize the number of students who
experience grade-level classrooms, comprehensive core enrichments, and targeted academic
supports. It also maximizes the number of educators who work full-time on one campus and
who benefit from regular collaboration with grade- and content-alike colleagues.

By operating fewer elementary schools and spreading current enrollment more evenly across
remaining sites, the district can stabilize and offer a more comprehensive and sustainable
program at each elementary site.

In recent years, we have used resources from other parts of our system to subsidize elementary
programs with low enrollment. Given our current and projected budget shortfalls, we can no
longer sustain subsidies for underenrolled sites. As a result, elementary schools have already
faced several programmatic and operational constraints:
    ●​ Over half of our elementary programs have been forced to place students in multi-grade
        classrooms;
    ●​ Staffing for instructional coaching, academic coaching, and school climate support
        positions have been reduced;
    ●​ Core enrichment teachers that support Arts, PE, and Library/Media instruction have been
        split across multiple sites, reducing the capacity to have full-school enrichment
        programs;
    ●​ Support staff that help monitor lunch and recess have been reduced, forcing principals
        to spend much of their time monitoring duty;
    ●​ Opportunities for strong teacher collaboration and professional learning have been
        compromised by multi-grade classrooms and single-strand programs.

If we continue with no rightsizing action, all of these constraints will be compounded in school
year 27-28.

3b. Effects on Other Schools, Enrollment, and Community Access
The proposed merger would affect Beverly Cleary within the Grant cluster. Enrollment at Beverly
Cleary is projected to change from 480 (as a K-8) to 502 (as a K-5) in the 2027–2028 school
year. Based on current enrollment projections, there will be three to four classrooms at each
grade-level, with blends not required. These changes would support a combined elementary
school community, with opportunities to stabilize staffing and sustain educational programs

and student supports. Implementation would require coordinated staffing, classroom
assignments, and use of shared spaces to accommodate incoming students while supporting
students already enrolled. In 2027, students from Irvington would join Beverly Cleary’s K–5

proposed change would shift students’ daily school activities toward Beverly Cleary and Grant
Park. Transportation options include walking, biking, family transportation, and school bus
service for eligible students. PPS would support school bus service for students who live more
than one mile from the school and work with the school community and the district Safe Routes
to School coordinator to identify appropriate crossing, pedestrian, and bicycle safety measures.

3c. Community Partnerships and Potential Community Impact
Irvington currently partners with Grow Portland, which teaches a gardening class during the
school day. Closure could affect student access to this programming. Irvington students are
projected to transition to Beverly Cleary, which does not currently offer Grow Portland.
Opportunities for the partnership to continue, relocate, or adapt include bringing the program to
Beverly Cleary. Additional community impacts include a possible gap in gardening instruction
for transitioning students, with proposed responses including working with Beverly Cleary’s
building leaders and school community to determine what students need and whether the
school is ready to support this partnership.

Irvington Extended Day Child Care is an independent, single-site provider serving approximately
80 students and families through essential after-school care. Closing Irvington without
relocating the program to Beverly Cleary could force the business to close, eliminate staff jobs,
and disrupt care for the families who depend on it. Although Beverly Cleary currently hosts a
YMCA child care program serving approximately 55 students, Irvington Extended Day should be
included in space-planning discussions to identify sufficient, appropriately licensed space for
both programs to continue operating. This is especially important because five Irvington
Extended Day families receive ERDC assistance and could experience funding delays or
interruptions if required to change providers. Preserving this partnership would protect an
established community resource, maintain employment, and provide approximately 80 students
with familiar caregivers and consistent routines during a significant school transition. We ask
that Irvington Extended Day be included in early transition planning and provide a viable
opportunity to continue serving its families at Beverly Cleary.

Any future community use of the facility would be considered through the Building Repurposing
process.
