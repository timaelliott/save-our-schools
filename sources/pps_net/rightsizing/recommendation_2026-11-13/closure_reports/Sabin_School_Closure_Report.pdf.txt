1a. School Description
Sabin Elementary School is a Northeast Portland neighborhood K–5 school and is currently part
of the Grant cluster. It hosts a Social Emotional Skills classroom and on-site, after school
childcare. Approximately 250 students are enrolled at Sabin for the 2026-27 school year.

1b. Financial Impact
The primary financial benefit of consolidating elementary schools and programs is to align
school capacity and operating costs with enrollment, allowing PPS to concentrate limited
resources in fewer, more efficiently resourced schools. Combining school communities can
reduce duplicative school-level administrative and operating expenses, improve building
utilization, and support more efficient staffing and program delivery. These consolidations are
intended to generate ongoing savings while supporting strong core instructional programs and
student services.

If the school remains open, the projected cost per student for the 2027/2028 school year is:
$19,741.

If the school is closed in 2027, the projected overall savings is $898,472. This includes savings
from administrative, custodial, and office staffing, 80% of utilities, and includes the one-time
cost of teacher moves. We anticipate ongoing expenses, including 20% of current utility costs
and an average annual security cost, if the school were closed and unoccupied.

Under the proposed change, 32% of students would live more than one mile from Dr. Martin
Luther King Jr. and would be offered school bus transportation. To accommodate these
students, PPS anticipates Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. will continue to have two buses. The
Transportation Department anticipates no cost because the number of buses remains the
same.

Transportation and Safe Routes to School planning will identify needed supports for safe arrival
and dismissal, including walking and biking improvements, crossing supports, or traffic
circulation changes.

History of Requested and Actual Transfers
During 2024-25, 2025-26, and 2026-27 school years, 54 students requested transfers into Sabin,
and 19 enrolled through an approved transfer. During the same period, 152 students residing
within the school’s attendance area requested transfers to other schools, and 47 enrolled
elsewhere through an approved transfer.

Projected declining enrollment will make it difficult to maintain stable staffing, full programming,
and essential student support services. Under enrollment-based staffing allocations, low
enrollment can result in year-to-year staffing changes, blended-grade classrooms or combined
programs, and reduced access to enrichment and student support offerings. Staff may serve
multiple roles or split time across schools, limiting continuity and availability. While educators
would continue working to meet students’ needs, projected enrollment would constrain the
school’s ability to provide consistent access to a well-rounded educational experience without
ongoing supplemental district resources.

Gross capacity is the estimated maximum number of students a school can accommodate,
calculated by assigning a student capacity to each classroom based on its square footage and
summing those capacities:
    ●​ Less than 800 square feet: 24 students
    ●​ 800–1,000 square feet: 27 students
    ●​ More than 1,000 square feet: 30 students

Functional capacity is a practical estimate of the number of students a school can
accommodate, calculated by multiplying its gross capacity by the applicable utilization rate. It
does not represent maximum occupancy under building code requirements or determine
staffing levels.

Physical Condition of Building, Capital and Environmental Needs
Sabin is a three-level, brick-faced reinforced-concrete school with portable facilities and a
covered play structure. Assessments conducted in 2019–2020 identified much HVAC
equipment beyond its typical design life, corroded and leaking boiler circulation pumps, aging
electrical distribution and interior lighting, and some flooring and casework beyond design life;
there are also plumbing and heating replacement needs. The assessment reported recently
replaced fire-alarm equipment in good condition and a wet-pipe sprinkler system protecting
corridors, stairwells, and classroom entrances without major observed deficiencies.

ADA Accessibility
The first floor is accessible through an entrance on the side of the building and there is an
elevator that provides access to the basement and second-floor classrooms.

Building Use
Currently, Sabin houses a K–5 neighborhood school with general education classrooms, a
Social Emotional Skills classroom, enrichment instruction, and student support services.

Analysis of current and potential use of the facility, including recommended
disposition
Following closure, potential uses of the Sabin Elementary School facility will be evaluated
through the district’s Building Repurposing process, informed by community engagement,
district needs, facility conditions, and financial considerations. This process will consider
opportunities to support students and the surrounding community and identify a recommended
future use or disposition for Board consideration.

2b. Student Reassignment
The proposed closure of Sabin Elementary School would move its full neighborhood K–5
boundary to Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Elementary. Benefits include MLK Jr.’s updated roof, larger
grounds, and outdoor facilities. Students in Sabin’s Social Emotional Skills program would also
receive services in K–5 SES classrooms at Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., with individual placements
determined by the Special Education Department in collaboration with school teams and
families.

3. Community/Cluster and District Impact Analysis

3a. Cluster Analysis
How will the proposed closure maintain or improve equity of access to schools
offering high quality educational choices?
In the school year 2026-27, Portland Public Schools staffs and supports 56 elementary
programs across the district. Eleven of these programs are situated within K-8 campuses.
Enrollment ranges from 160 students to over 550 students. Using the QEM and our system
definition of “well-resourced schools” as a guide, we have established an elementary enrollment
of 360 as the minimum threshold to be able to staff and support a comprehensive and stable
elementary program. Maximizing the number of sites at or above this minimum threshold allows
the district to maximize the number of students who experience grade-level classrooms,
comprehensive core enrichments, and targeted academic supports. It also maximizes the
number of educators who work full-time on one campus and who benefit from regular
collaboration with grade- and content-alike colleagues.

By operating fewer elementary schools and spreading current enrollment more evenly across
remaining sites, the district can offer a more comprehensive and sustainable program at each
elementary site.

In recent years, we have used resources from other parts of our system to subsidize elementary
programs with low enrollment. Given our current and projected budget shortfalls, we can no
longer sustain subsidies for underenrolled sites. As a result, elementary schools have already
faced several programmatic and operational constraints:
    ●​ Over half of our elementary programs have been forced to place students in multi-grade
        classrooms;
    ●​ Instructional coaches, academic coaches, and school climate support positions have
        been cut;

3b. Effects on Other Schools, Enrollment, and Community Access
The proposed merger would affect Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Elementary within the Jefferson
cluster. Enrollment at Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Elementary is projected to change from 319 to
572 in the 2027–2028 school year. Based on these projections, there will be two to three
classrooms at each grade-level. These changes would support a combined elementary school
community, with opportunities to stabilize staffing and sustain educational programs and
student supports. Implementation will require coordinated staffing, classroom assignments, and
use of shared spaces to accommodate incoming students while supporting students already
enrolled. Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. is ready to receive students and would need a very minimal
kitchen upgrade (a warming cabinet). There already are sufficient instructional and student
support spaces, and we would continue to coordinate any additional supports needed to
accommodate the combined school community.

Sabin Elementary School is located approximately one mile from Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.
Elementary School. Nearby community resources include Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. School Park.
The proposed change would shift students’ daily school activities toward Dr. Martin Luther King
Jr. Elementary and its adjacent park, while potentially increasing travel to existing childcare
arrangements and resources near Sabin. Transportation options include walking, biking, family
transportation, and school bus service for eligible students. PPS would support school bus
service for students who live more than one mile from the school and work with the school
community and the district Safe Routes to School coordinator to identify appropriate crossing,
pedestrian, and bicycle safety measures.

3c. Community Partnerships and Potential Community Impact
Sabin currently partners with Chess for Success and the Black Parent Initiative to provide family
and student programming. Sabin's closure risks interrupting these services. While both
organizations previously operated at Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. School, those partnerships ended
prior to the 2026–27 school year. PPS will coordinate with both organizations and school
leadership to explore resuming services at Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., where Sabin students are
projected to transition, or to identify alternative community partnerships.

Sabin currently partners with Neighborhood House, an established child care provider serving
four PPS communities. The Sabin program serves 58 students, with an additional 10 on its
waitlist, while Neighborhood House at Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. school currently serves 44

students. Relocating the Sabin program to Martin Luther King Jr. would allow families to keep
their same provider, but would require appropriately licensed space to accommodate up to 102
enrolled students. Child care spaces must meet state licensing requirements, which involve
additional time and costs, making early planning essential. District leadership, Dr. Martin Luther
King Jr. administration, and Neighborhood House will collaborate early to establish a clear
transition plan, ensure reliable access to appropriate space, and protect the needs of both
school communities. Preserving this partnership would maintain continuity for families, support
an established community provider, and create opportunities to address the existing demand for
accessible child care.

Sabin currently partners with Self Enhancement, Inc. (SEI) to operate its SUN Community
School, providing wraparound services and family support through a collaboration between PPS,
Multnomah County and the City of Portland. SEI’s partner organizations include Chess for
Success, Tiny Dance Life, Soo Bahk Do, Sabin PTA, Motion Magic, NW Children's Theater, Ethos,
and Adventures in Spanish provides free extended-day enrichment opportunities, access to a
food pantry, and culturally relevant programming supported by staff who reflect and understand
the student population. SUN also serves as an important connection point for students and
families to access individualized support, community resources, enrichment opportunities, and
basic-needs assistance. Closure could affect student and family access to established
relationships with SUN staff, enrichment providers, and community resources, as well as require
adjustments to programming locations, partner operations, and access to existing supports.
Opportunities for partnerships to continue include transitioning students and services to Dr.
Martin Luther King Jr., which also operates a SEI-led SUN Community School. Having the same
community-based organization operating SUN at both schools creates an opportunity to
maintain service continuity, preserve established relationships, and coordinate student and
family transitions within an existing service system.

Services requiring further coordination include assessing Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.’s capacity to
accommodate additional students and service needs, coordinating SUN staffing and program
resources, maintaining access to enrichment partners and basic-needs supports, and ensuring
students can continue receiving individualized and wraparound services without interruption.
Additional community impacts include the potential disruption of trusted relationships,
enrichment opportunities, and established points of connection for families accessing
community resources, with proposed responses including coordinating with SEI and Dr. Martin
Luther King Jr. school leadership to develop a transition plan, identify staffing and resource
needs, communicate with families, and prioritize continuity of services and student participation
in existing programs.

Any future community use of the facility would be considered through the Building Repurposing
process.
