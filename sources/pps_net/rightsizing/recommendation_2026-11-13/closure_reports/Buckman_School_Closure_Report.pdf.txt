School Closure Process
The PPS Board of Education determines whether schools will be closed. Board
determination on school closures is based on the Superintendent's recommendation.
The School Initiation & Closure policy (6.10.030-P) requires that the Superintendent's
recommendation be accompanied by a School Closure Report.

1a. School Description
Buckman Elementary School is a K–5 neighborhood and arts focus-option school in inner
Southeast Portland, serving neighborhood students and students admitted through the PPS
lottery. Its arts-integrated program provides visual arts, music, and dance instruction for all
students. The school also houses regional Social Emotional Skills classrooms serving grades
K–2 and 3–5. The Buckman campus has 28 classrooms and approximately 240 students are
enrolled for the 2026-27 school year.

1b. Financial Impact
The primary financial benefit of consolidating elementary schools and programs is to align
school capacity and operating costs with enrollment, allowing PPS to concentrate limited
resources in fewer, more efficiently resourced schools. Combining school communities can
reduce duplicative school-level administrative and operating expenses, improve building
utilization, and support more efficient staffing and program delivery. These consolidations are
intended to generate ongoing savings while supporting strong core instructional programs and
student services.

If the school remains open, the projected cost per student for the 2027/2028 school year is:
$21,616.
If the school is closed in 2027, the projected overall savings is $934,719. This includes savings
from administrative, custodial, and office staffing, 80% of utilities, and includes the one-time
cost of teacher moves. We anticipate ongoing expenses, including 20% of current utility costs
and an average annual security cost, if the school were closed and unoccupied.

Under the proposed change, 36% of students would live more than one mile from Sunnyside and
would be offered school bus transportation. To accommodate these students, PPS anticipates
increasing service to Sunnyside Elementary from zero to three buses.

Under the proposed change, 44% of students would live more than one mile from Abernethy and
would be offered school bus transportation. To accommodate these students, PPS anticipates
maintaining service to Abernethy with four buses. The Transportation Department estimates an
additional cost of approximately $50,000.

Transportation and Safe Routes to School planning will identify needed supports for safe arrival
and dismissal, including walking and biking improvements, crossing supports, or traffic
circulation changes.

History of Requested and Actual Transfers
During 2024-25, 2025-26, and 2026-27 school years, 334 students requested transfers into
Buckman and 92 enrolled through an approved transfer. During the same period, 151 students
residing within the school’s attendance area requested transfers to other schools, and 61
enrolled elsewhere through an approved transfer.

Projected declining enrollment will make it difficult to maintain stable staffing, full programming,
and essential student support services. Under enrollment-based staffing allocations, low
enrollment can result in year-to-year staffing changes, blended-grade classrooms or combined
programs, and reduced access to enrichment and student support offerings. Staff may serve
multiple roles or split time across schools, limiting continuity and availability. While educators
would continue working to meet students’ needs, projected enrollment would constrain the
school’s ability to provide consistent access to a well-rounded educational experience without
ongoing supplemental district resources.

Physical Condition of Building, Capital and Environmental Needs
Situated in the Buckman neighborhood of Southeast Portland, the 1921 Buckman Elementary
School is over 100 years old, and in poor condition with a facility condition index (FCI) score of
0.19. Facility assessments identified cracking in the south exterior concrete wall and southwest
retaining wall; cracked or deteriorating concrete stairs; roof-hatch deterioration; loose and
cracked cafeteria flooring; and delaminating wood floors in the cafeteria and gymnasium. The
2021 Long Range Facilities Plan also identified classroom temperature-control problems, aging
mechanical equipment, and undersized classrooms and shared spaces. Furthermore, there are
structural issues that would need to be addressed if the school continued to be open.

ADA Accessibility
The first floor is accessible through a ramp near the main entrance and there is an elevator that
provides access to the basement and second-floor classrooms.

Building Use
Currently, Buckman houses a K–5 neighborhood school and an arts focus-option program,
including visual arts, music, and dance instruction, as well as regional K–2 and 3–5 Social
Emotional Skills classrooms.

Analysis of current and potential use of the facility, including recommended disposition
Following closure, potential uses of the Buckman Elementary School facility will be evaluated
through the district’s Building Repurposing Plan, informed by community engagement, district
needs, facility conditions, and financial considerations. This process will consider opportunities
to support students and the surrounding community and identify a recommended future use or
disposition for Board consideration.


2b. Student Reassignment
The proposed closure of Buckman Elementary School would reassign neighborhood K–5
students to Abernethy and Sunnyside and discontinue the arts focus-option program. The
neighborhood boundary would be divided approximately in half: the area north of Hawthorne
and south of Burnside would be assigned to Abernethy, and the area north of Burnside would be
assigned to Sunnyside. Students currently served in Buckman’s K–2 and 3–5 Social Emotional
Skills (SES) classrooms would receive services in dedicated SES classrooms at Sunnyside.

3. Community/Cluster and District Impact Analysis

3a. Cluster Analysis

How will the proposed closure maintain or improve equity of access to schools offering high
quality educational choices?
In the 2026-2027 school year, PPS staffs and supports 56 elementary programs. Eleven of these
programs are situated within K-8 campuses. The enrollment of these programs ranges from 160
students to over 550 students. Using the QEM and our system definition of “well-resourced
schools” as a guide, we have established an elementary enrollment of 360 as the minimum
threshold to be able to staff and support a comprehensive and stable elementary program.
Maximizing the number of sites at or above this minimum threshold allows the district to
maximize the number of students who experience grade-level classrooms, comprehensive core
enrichments, and targeted academic supports. It also maximizes the number of educators who
work full-time on one campus and who benefit from regular collaboration with grade- and
content-alike colleagues.
By operating fewer elementary schools and spreading current enrollment more evenly across
remaining sites, the district can stabilize and offer a more comprehensive and sustainable
program at each elementary site.

In recent years, we have used resources from other parts of our system to subsidize elementary
programs with low enrollment. Given our current and projected budget shortfalls, we can no
longer sustain subsidies for underenrolled sites. As a result, elementary schools have already
faced several programmatic and operational constraints:

3b. Effects on Other Schools, Enrollment, and Community Access
The proposed merger would affect Sunnyside School and Abernethy Elementary School.
Enrollment at Sunnyside is projected to change from 485 (as a K-8) to 480 (as a K-5) in the
2027–2028 school year. Based on current enrollment projections, Sunnyside will have two to
three classrooms at each grade-level, with blends not required. Enrollment at Abernethy is
projected to change from 269 to 312 in the same year. At Abernethy, based on current
enrollment projections, there will be two to three classrooms at each grade-level, with blends not
required. These changes would support combined elementary school communities, with
opportunities to stabilize staffing and sustain educational programs and student supports.
Implementation would require coordinated staffing, classroom assignments, and use of shared
spaces, along with support for students transitioning from Buckman’s arts-integrated program
into another program with an environmental focus. Receiving-school readiness needs at
Sunnyside include minor kitchen upgrades, including a warming cabinet, and a recommended
steam pipe assessment. Abernethy requires moderate kitchen upgrades, including a double
oven, double-door freezer, and warming oven, as well as further evaluation and remediation of
groundwater conditions in the playground area affecting the additional classrooms northeast of
the main building, where the former portables were located. These improvements would
support incoming students while maintaining services for students already enrolled.

Buckman Elementary School is located approximately 1.4 miles from Sunnyside and 1.4 miles
from Abernethy. Nearby community resources include Belmont Library in the Sunnyside area
and Piccolo Park near Abernethy. The proposed change would shift students’ daily school
activities toward their assigned receiving school. Transportation options include walking, biking,
family transportation, and school bus service for eligible students. PPS would evaluate needs
through transportation eligibility and route reviews, coordination with school-based childcare
providers, and Safe Routes to School planning to identify appropriate crossing, pedestrian, and
bicycle safety measures.

3c. Community Partnerships and Potential Community Impact
Buckman currently partners with Friends of the Children to provide long-term mentoring for
students. Closure is not expected to interrupt this service. Friends of the Children has a
long-term commitment to each child regardless of school placement, and mentors (referred to
as Friends) continue supporting students when they transition to another school. These
services are provided at no cost to the District. No Racial Equity & Social Justice (RESJ) partner
services at Buckman have been identified as requiring alternatives. Additional community
impacts include the adjustment for students and mentors as they reconnect in a new school
setting, with proposed responses including coordinating with receiving schools so Friends can
continue working with students without interruption.

Buckman currently partners with Champions to provide before- and after-school care for
approximately 45 students. The program is expected to relocate to Sunnyside, which partners
with the YMCA serving 48 students, and Abernethy, which partners with Art 4 Life serving 87
students. Adding Buckman's students would bring the combined enrollment across these
programs to approximately 180 students, requiring careful consideration of available space,
licensing, staffing, and operational capacity. We ask that Champions, the YMCA, Art 4 Life, and
both receiving school administrations be included in early transition planning to determine how
students will be divided, ensure sufficient appropriately licensed space, and minimize
disruptions to existing programs and families. Clear communication about enrollment, program
locations, transportation, and timelines will also be essential. A successful transition requires
collaboration and adequate resources to accommodate Buckman families while preserving the
services and partnerships already established at Sunnyside and Abernethy.

Buckman currently partners with Impact NW to operate its SUN Community School, providing
community-based services and wraparound family supports through collaboration among PPS,
Multnomah County, and the City of Portland. SUN provides free extended-day enrichment
opportunities, access to a food pantry, individualized family support, and culturally relevant
programming supported by staff who reflect and understand the student population. Buckman's
enrichment offerings include arts and cultural programming such as Ballet the Black Way,
Spanish language instruction, theater through Northwest Children's Theater, martial arts, sports,
chess, music, creative writing, and STEM activities such as LEGO Mechanical Engineering.
These opportunities provide students with access to experiences that support creativity,
academic enrichment, physical activity, cultural learning, and social-emotional development.
Closure could significantly disrupt student and family access to established SUN services,
enrichment opportunities, trusted staff relationships, and community partnerships.
Opportunities for partnerships to continue would require further coordination, as students would
transition to Sunnyside and Abernethy, neither of which currently operates a SUN Community
School. Unlike transitions to schools with an existing SUN program, there is no established SUN
infrastructure at either receiving school to absorb Buckman's existing programming, staffing,
partnerships, and family support services. Services requiring alternatives or further coordination
include identifying how students and families will continue accessing wraparound supports,
food pantry resources, individualized family assistance, free enrichment opportunities, and
summer programming currently coordinated through Impact NW. Additional community impacts
include the potential loss of an established access point for families navigating basic-needs

resources, disruption of community partnerships and enrichment providers, and reduced access
to free developmental and academic opportunities that support student engagement and

Proposed responses include a coordinated district effort with Impact NW, Sunnyside and
Abernethy school leadership, and community partners to identify students and families currently
accessing Buckman SUN services, establish warm handoffs to alternative resources, assess
opportunities to maintain existing enrichment partnerships and programming at other locations,
and identify potential service gaps requiring additional staffing, funding, or alternative service
delivery arrangements. Particular consideration should be given to preserving access to SUN
services for families transitioning to both receiving schools and ensuring that students do not
experience an interruption in access to essential resources and enrichment opportunities.

Any future community use of the facility would be considered through the Building Repurposing
