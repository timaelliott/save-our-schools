1a. School Description
Meriwether Lewis Elementary School is a K–5 neighborhood school in Southeast Portland’s
Woodstock neighborhood. It hosts a focus on literacy and mathematics, additional reading and
mathematics support, visual arts, gardens, physical education, and library services. The school
has 19 classrooms in a single-story campus with gardens supporting environmental education.
Approximately 240 students are enrolled at Lewis for the 2026-2027 school year.

1b. Financial Impact
The primary financial benefit of consolidating elementary schools and programs is to align
school capacity and operating costs with enrollment, allowing PPS to concentrate limited
resources in fewer, more efficiently resourced schools. Combining school communities can
reduce duplicative school-level administrative and operating expenses, improve building
utilization, and support more efficient staffing and program delivery. These consolidations are
intended to generate ongoing savings while supporting strong core instructional programs and
student services.

If the school remains open, the projected cost per student for the 2028-2029 school year is:
$19,094. (This is calculated using projected FY28 compensation levels and current staffing
practices, which could change in the near future due to budget uncertainty.)

If the school is closed in 2028, the projected overall savings is $860,366. This includes savings
from administrative, custodial, and office staffing, 80% of utilities, and includes the one-time
cost of teacher moves. We anticipate ongoing expenses, including 20% of current utility costs
and an average annual security cost, if the school were closed and unoccupied.

To accommodate students who live more than one mile from Duniway, PPS anticipates
maintaining service to Duniway with two buses. The Transportation Department estimates an
additional savings of approximately $122,000. The proposed change would reduce the total
number of buses serving the closing and receiving schools, resulting in transportation cost
savings.

Transportation and Safe Routes to School planning will identify needed supports for safe arrival
and dismissal, including walking and biking improvements, crossing supports, or traffic
circulation changes.

History of Requested and Actual Transfers
During 2024-2025, 2025-2026, and 2026-2027 school years, 67 students requested transfers
into Lewis and 22 enrolled through an approved transfer. During the same period, 104 students
residing within the school’s attendance area requested transfers to other schools, and 26
enrolled elsewhere through an approved transfer.

Projected declining enrollment will make it difficult to maintain stable staffing, full programming,
and essential student support services. Under enrollment-based staffing allocations, low
enrollment can result in year-to-year staffing changes, blended-grade classrooms or combined
programs, and reduced access to enrichment and student support offerings. Staff may serve
multiple roles or split time across schools, limiting continuity and availability. While educators
would continue working to meet students’ needs, projected enrollment would constrain the
school’s ability to provide consistent access to a well-rounded educational experience without
ongoing supplemental district resources.

Physical Condition of Building, Capital and Environmental Needs
Lewis a single-story, L-shaped wood-frame school with brick, wood, and concrete-block
cladding and interconnected wings under shed and gable roofs. Previous facility assessments
conducted in 2019–2020 identified temperature-control problems in the north addition, original
boilers with reported piping leaks, cracking around the gym’s exterior brick wall, weathered
window frames and cracked panes, and damaged exterior metal doors. In addition, it identified
undersized kindergarten, special education, library, gymnasium, and cafeteria spaces. Lewis
received a seismic retrofit in 2018.

ADA Accessibility

Building Use
Lewis houses a K–5 neighborhood school and Communication Behavior classrooms, with

Analysis of current and potential use of the facility, including recommended disposition
Following closure, potential uses of the Lewis facility will be evaluated through the district’s
Building Repurposing process, informed by community engagement, district needs, facility
conditions, and financial considerations. This process will consider opportunities to support
students and the surrounding community and identify a recommended future use or disposition
for Board consideration. The 2018 SRGP seismic grant requires Lewis to be used for
educational purposes for 10 years. Because the school isn't set to close until 2028, that 10-year
requirement will be fulfilled.

2b. Student Reassignment
The proposed closure of Lewis Elementary School would reassign neighborhood K–5 students
to Duniway. Students served in Lewis Communication Behavior classrooms would also receive
services at Duniway.

3. Community/Cluster and District Impact Analysis

3a. Cluster Analysis

How will the proposed closure maintain or improve equity of access to schools offering high
quality educational choices?
In the 2026-2027 school year Portland Public Schools staffs and supports 56 elementary
programs across the district. Eleven of these programs are situated within K-8 campuses. The
enrollment of these programs ranges from 160 students to over 550 students. Using the QEM
and our system definition of “well-resourced schools” as a guide, we have established an
elementary enrollment of 360 as the minimum threshold to be able to staff and support a
comprehensive and stable elementary program. Maximizing the number of sites at or above this
minimum threshold allows the district to maximize the number of students who experience
grade-level classrooms, comprehensive core enrichments, and targeted academic supports. It
also maximizes the number of educators who work full-time on one campus and who benefit
from regular collaboration with grade- and content-alike colleagues.

By operating fewer elementary schools and spreading current enrollment more evenly across
remaining sites, the district can stabilize and offer a more comprehensive and sustainable
program at each elementary site.

In recent years, we have used resources from other parts of our system to subsidize elementary
programs with low enrollment. Given our current and projected budget shortfalls, we can no
longer sustain subsidies for underenrolled sites. As a result, elementary schools have already
faced several programmatic and operational constraints:

    ●​ Over half of our elementary programs have been forced to place students in multi-grade
       classrooms;
    ●​ Staffing for instructional coaching, academic coaching, and school climate support
       positions have been reduced;
    ●​ Core enrichment teachers that support Arts, PE, and Library/Media instruction have been
       split across multiple sites, reducing the capacity to have full-school enrichment
       programs;
    ●​ Support staff that help monitor lunch and recess have been reduced, forcing principals
       to spend much of their time monitoring duty;
    ●​ Opportunities for strong teacher collaboration and professional learning have been
       compromised by multi-grade classrooms and single-strand programs.

3b. Effects on Other Schools, Enrollment, and Community Access
The proposed merger would affect Duniway within the Cleveland cluster. Enrollment at Duniway
is projected to change from 362 to 533 in the 2028–2029 school year. Based on current
enrollment projections, there will be three to four classrooms at each grade level, with blends
not required. The combined school community would provide opportunities to stabilize staffing
and sustain educational programs and student supports. Duniway would need moderate kitchen
upgrades (additional double ovens, dishwasher, warming cooler, and milk cooler), and
classroom assignments and student support spaces coordinated to accommodate incoming
students while supporting students already enrolled.

Lewis is located approximately 1.2 miles from Duniway. The proposed change would shift
students’ daily school activities toward Duniway and the Eastmoreland neighborhood.
Transportation options include walking, biking, family transportation, and school bus service for
eligible students. Potential barriers include longer walking distances and crossings of
Campesinos Boulevard and other busy streets, depending on students’ home addresses. PPS
would support school bus service for students who live more than one mile from the school and
work with the school community and the district Safe Routes to School coordinator to identify
appropriate crossing, pedestrian, and bicycle safety measures.

3c. Community Partnerships and Potential Community Impact
Lewis does not have Racial Equity & Social Justice (RESJ) partner programming. Closure is not
expected to affect RESJ partner services. PPS will continue working with building leaders and
school communities at receiving schools so that partnerships reflect what students and
families need. Future community use of the facility would be considered through the Building
Repurposing process.

Lewis partners with the YMCA to provide child care for approximately 32 students. Relocating
Lewis students to Duniway would require families to transition to a different provider, as
Duniway partners with Duniway All Stars, which serves approximately 167 students through its
school-age and preschool programs. Adding Lewis students could increase enrollment to
approximately 199 students, creating additional space and staffing needs. A significant concern
is protecting the dedicated space Duniway All Stars uses, as growing classroom needs could
result in that space being reassigned for instructional purposes, potentially disrupting services
for existing families. We ask that the YMCA, Duniway All Stars, and district leadership be
included in early planning discussions to identify child care options for Lewis families, protect
existing program space, and determine whether additional appropriately licensed space is
available. Any transition plan should consider the needs of both school communities, minimize
disruptions for families, and ensure that existing child care services are not compromised by
increased enrollment or changing classroom needs.
