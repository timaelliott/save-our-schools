1a. School Description
Peninsula is a North Portland neighborhood K–5 school. It’s a collaborative community
committed to authentic relationships, culturally responsive teaching, and removing barriers to
student success. Its enrichment program connects classroom learning with art, music, physical
education, and library instruction. Peninsula is an Inclusive school community serving students
with disabilities in their neighborhood school. There are 28 classrooms on a single-story
campus and approximately 225 students are enrolled for the 2026-27 school year.

1b. Financial Impact
The primary financial benefit of consolidating elementary schools and programs is to align
school capacity and operating costs with enrollment, allowing PPS to concentrate limited
resources in fewer, more efficiently resourced schools. Combining school communities can
reduce duplicative school-level administrative and operating expenses, improve building
utilization, and support more efficient staffing and program delivery. These consolidations are
intended to generate ongoing savings while supporting strong core instructional programs and
student services.

If the school remains open, the projected cost per student for the 2027/2028 school year is:
$20,099.
If the school is closed in 2027, the projected overall savings is $961,959. This includes savings
from administrative, custodial, and office staffing, 80% of utilities, and includes the one-time
cost of teacher moves. We anticipate ongoing expenses, including 20% of current utility costs
and an average annual security cost, if the school were closed and unoccupied.

Under the proposed change, 45% of students would live more than one mile from Rosa Parks
and would be offered school bus transportation. To accommodate these students, PPS
anticipates increasing service to Rosa Parks from zero to four buses. The Transportation
Department estimates an additional annual cost of approximately $170,000.

Transportation and Safe Routes to School planning will identify needed supports for safe arrival
and dismissal, including walking and biking improvements, crossing supports, or traffic
circulation changes.

History of Requested and Actual Transfers

During 2024-2025, 2025-2026, and 2026-2027 school years, 40 students requested transfers
into Peninsula and 14 enrolled through an approved transfer. During the same period, 143
students residing within the school’s attendance area requested transfers to other schools and
44 enrolled elsewhere through an approved transfer.

Projected declining enrollment will make it difficult to maintain stable staffing, full programming,
and essential student support services. Under enrollment-based staffing allocations, low
enrollment can result in year-to-year staffing changes, blended-grade classrooms or combined
programs, and reduced access to enrichment and student support offerings. Staff may serve
multiple roles or split time across schools, limiting continuity and availability. While educators
would continue working to meet students’ needs, projected enrollment would constrain the
school’s ability to provide consistent access to a well-rounded educational experience without
ongoing supplemental district resources.

Gross capacity is the estimated maximum number of students a school can accommodate,
calculated by assigning a student capacity to each classroom based on its square footage and
summing those capacities:
    ●​ Less than 800 square feet: 24 students
    ●​ 800–1,000 square feet: 27 students
    ●​ More than 1,000 square feet: 30 students

Functional capacity is a practical estimate of the number of students a school can
accommodate, calculated by multiplying its gross capacity by the applicable utilization rate. It
does not represent maximum occupancy under building code requirements or determine
staffing levels.

Analysis of current and potential use of the facility, including recommended disposition
Following closure, potential uses of the Peninsula facility will be evaluated through the district’s
Building Repurposing process, informed by community engagement, district needs, facility
conditions, and financial considerations. This process will consider opportunities to support
students and the surrounding community and identify a recommended future use or disposition
for Board consideration.
2b. Student Reassignment
The proposed closure of Peninsula would move its full neighborhood K–5 boundary to Rosa
Parks. Rosa Parks was built in 2006 and has updated facilities. Peninsula’s Inclusive School
Community would merge with Rosa Parks K-5’s Inclusive School Community. The combined
community would maintain the continuum of IEP-determined programming and supports.

Transportation options and barriers
Transportation options include walking, biking, family transportation, and school bus service for
elementary students who live more than one mile from their neighborhood school; additional
students may qualify based on Oregon Department of Education guidelines or individual needs.
We do not anticipate any barriers to students getting to school; however, additional bussing
may be provided, as needed, for hazard areas within the school attendance boundary.

How will this school location accommodate the reassigned students?
Rosa Parks was constructed in 2006, has 26 classrooms and a functional capacity of 499
students. With a combined enrollment of 481 in 2027/2028, Rosa Parks will be able to
accommodate the reassigned students. Rosa Parks also has air conditioning, more modern
learning spaces, and an elevator to access second-floor classrooms.

3. Community/Cluster and District Impact Analysis

3a. Cluster Analysis

How will the proposed closure maintain or improve equity of access to schools offering high
quality educational choices?
In the 2026-2027 school year, PPS staffs and supports 56 elementary programs. Eleven of these
programs are situated within K-8 campuses. The enrollment of these programs ranges from 160
students to over 550 students. Using the QEM and our system definition of “well-resourced
schools” as a guide, we have established an elementary enrollment of 360 as the minimum
threshold to be able to staff and support a comprehensive and stable elementary program.
Maximizing the number of sites at or above this minimum threshold allows the district to
maximize the number of students who experience grade-level classrooms, comprehensive core
enrichments, and targeted academic supports. It also maximizes the number of educators who
work full-time on one campus and who benefit from regular collaboration with grade- and
content-alike colleagues.
By operating fewer elementary schools and spreading current enrollment more evenly across
remaining sites, the district can stabilize and offer a more comprehensive and sustainable
program at each elementary site.

In recent years, we have used resources from other parts of our system to subsidize elementary
programs with low enrollment. Given our current and projected budget shortfalls, we can no
longer sustain subsidies for underenrolled sites. As a result, elementary schools have already
faced several programmatic and operational constraints:

    ●​ Over half of our elementary programs have been forced to place students in multi-grade
       classrooms;
    ●​ Staffing for instructional coaching, academic coaching, and school climate support
       positions have been reduced;
    ●​ Core enrichment teachers that support Arts, PE, and Library/Media instruction have been
       split across multiple sites, reducing the capacity to have full-school enrichment
       programs;
    ●​ Support staff that help monitor lunch and recess have been reduced, forcing principals
       to spend much of their time monitoring duty;
    ●​ Opportunities for strong teacher collaboration and professional learning have been
       compromised by multi-grade classrooms and single-strand programs.

3b. Effects on Other Schools, Enrollment, and Community Access
The proposed merger would affect Peninsula and Rosa Parks within the Roosevelt cluster.
Enrollment at Rosa Parks is projected to change from 160 to 481 in 2027-2028. Based on
current enrollment projections, there will be three to four classrooms at each grade-level, with
blends not required. Rosa Parks is ready to receive students throughout instructional spaces but
would need minimal kitchen upgrades (warming cabinet) to adequately accommodate the new
student population.

Peninsula is located approximately one mile from Rosa Parks. Rosa Parks is next door to the
Charles Jordan Community Center. Transportation options include walking, biking, family
transportation, and school bus service for eligible students. Potential barriers include longer
walking distances from the Peninsula neighborhood to Rosa Parks. PPS would evaluate these
needs through transportation eligibility and route reviews, and Safe Routes to School planning to
identify appropriate crossing, pedestrian, and bicycle safety measures.

3c. Community Partnerships and Potential Community Impact
Peninsula partners with Friends of the Children to provide long-term mentoring for students.
Closure is not expected to interrupt this service. Friends of the Children has a long-term
commitment to each child regardless of school placement, and mentors (referred to as Friends)
continue supporting students when they transition to another school. These services are
provided at no cost to the District. No Racial Equity & Social Justice (RESJ) partner services at
Peninsula have been identified as requiring alternatives. Additional community impacts include
the adjustment for students and mentors as they reconnect in a new school setting, with
proposed responses including coordinating with receiving schools so Friends can continue
working with students without interruption.

Peninsula partners with the YMCA to provide child care for approximately 46 students.
Relocating the program to Rosa Parks, which does not currently have an on-site child care
provider, creates an opportunity to maintain care for Peninsula families while expanding access
to Rosa Parks families. We ask that the YMCA and Rosa Parks administration be included in
early planning discussions to identify sufficient, stable, appropriately licensed space, staffing,
and operational resources to support a sustainable program. The designated space should
accommodate the existing 46 Peninsula students while allowing opportunities for future
enrollment growth. This transition could do more than preserve an existing partnership; it could
create a valuable new community resource that supports working families and expands access
to reliable, on-site child care at Rosa Parks.

Peninsula partners with Self Enhancement, Inc. (SEI) to operate its SUN Community School,
providing community-based services and wraparound family supports through collaboration
among PPS, Multnomah County, and the City of Portland. SEI provides free extended-day
enrichment opportunities, access to a food pantry and other basic-needs resources, educational
and recreational activities, and culturally responsive programming supported by staff who

enrichment opportunities and basic-needs supports, and ensuring students can continue
receiving individualized and wraparound services without interruption. Additional community
impacts include the potential disruption of trusted relationships, neighborhood-based
enrichment opportunities, and established points of connection for families accessing
community resources, with proposed responses including coordinating with SEI and Rosa Parks
school leadership to develop a transition plan, identify staffing and resource needs,
communicate with families, and prioritize continuity of services and student participation in
existing programs.

Any future community use of the facility would be considered through the Building Repurposing
