determination on school closures is based on the Superintendent's recommendation.
The School Initiation & Closure policy (6.10.030-P) requires that the Superintendent's
recommendation be accompanied by a School Closure Report.

1a. School Description
Rose City Park Elementary School is a Northeast Portland K–5 school offering neighborhood
instruction and Vietnamese Dual Language Immersion, where students spend half the day
learning with a Vietnamese-speaking teacher and half with an English-speaking teacher. To
continue the language pathway, students then graduate to Roseway Heights Middle School and
onto McDaniel High School. Built in 1912, Rose City Park has three levels and 27 classrooms.
Approximately 390 students are enrolled at Rose City Park for the 2026-2027 school year.

1b. Financial Impact
The primary financial benefit of consolidating elementary schools and programs is to align
school capacity and operating costs with enrollment, allowing PPS to concentrate limited
resources in fewer, more efficiently resourced schools. Combining school communities can
reduce duplicative school-level administrative and operating expenses, improve building
utilization, and support more efficient staffing and program delivery. These consolidations are
intended to generate ongoing savings while supporting strong core instructional programs and
student services.
If the school remains open, the projected cost per student for the 2028/2029 school year is:
$19,741. (This is calculated using projected FY28 compensation levels and current staffing
practices, which could change in the near future due to budget uncertainty.)
If the school is closed in 2028, the projected overall savings is $898,472. This includes savings
from Administrative, Custodial, and Office staffing, 80% of utilities and includes one-time cost of
teacher moves. We anticipate ongoing expenses, including 20% of current utility costs and an
average annual security cost, if the school were closed and unoccupied.
To accommodate the students who live more than one mile from Scott, PPS anticipates
increasing service to Scott from three to four buses/routes. The Transportation Department
estimates an additional savings of approximately $126,000. The proposed change would
reduce the total number of buses serving the closing and receiving schools, resulting in
transportation cost savings. Students in the Vietnamese Dual Language Immersion (DLI)
program would move to Vestal K-5. Students in the VDLI program would be offered
transportation with their DLI peers.

Transportation and Safe Routes to School planning will identify needed supports for safe arrival
and dismissal, including walking and biking improvements, crossing supports, or traffic
circulation changes.

History of Requested and Actual Transfers
During the 2024-2025, 2025-2026, and 2026-2027 school years, 132 students requested
transfers into Rose City Park, and 85 enrolled through an approved transfer. During the same
period, 136 students residing within the school’s attendance area requested transfers to other
schools, and 31 enrolled elsewhere through an approved transfer.

Projected enrollment remains above the district’s 360 student threshold and does not, on its
own, indicate that the school would be unable to maintain stable staffing, programming, or
essential student supports. The closure was considered within the broader district context,
including opportunities to improve facility utilization and condition, align resources with student
needs, and sustain well-resourced schools across the entire district.

Gross capacity is the estimated maximum number of students a school can accommodate,
calculated by assigning a student capacity to each classroom based on its square footage and
summing those capacities:
    ●​ Less than 800 square feet: 24 students
    ●​ 800–1,000 square feet: 27 students
    ●​ More than 1,000 square feet: 30 students

Functional capacity is a practical estimate of the number of students a school can
accommodate, calculated by multiplying its gross capacity by the applicable utilization rate. It
does not represent maximum occupancy under building code requirements or determine
staffing levels.

The cost of school operations includes projected FY28 school-based costs in funds 100 and
200 plus Facilities, Nutrition Services, and Special Education, at the current service level.

Physical Condition of Building, Capital and Environmental Needs
Rose City Park is a multi-story school built in 1912. Facility assessments conducted in
2019–2020 identified plumbing fixtures, most heating and cooling equipment, exhaust fans, the
main electrical switchboard, and many distribution panels beyond their typical design lives. The
roof hatch and original skylight were also aged and deteriorated. The assessment recorded
fire-alarm installation in 2019 and a partially sprinklered building. In addition, Rose City Park has
undersized classrooms and a lack of purposely designed special education space.

ADA Accessibility
Rose City Park has an accessible entrance and an elevator.

Building Use
Rose City Park houses K–5 neighborhood instruction and Vietnamese Dual Language
Immersion, with student support services and enrichment instruction.

Analysis of current and potential use of the facility, including recommended disposition
Following closure, potential uses of the Rose City Park facility will be evaluated through the
district’s Building Repurposing process, informed by community engagement, district needs,
facility conditions, and financial considerations. This process will consider opportunities to
support students and the surrounding community and identify a recommended future use or
disposition for Board consideration. Rose City Park was set to receive a full seismic retrofit in
28-29. Closure would provide the opportunity to redistribute those funds to address other high
risk building parts.

2b. Student Reassignment
The proposed closure of Rose City Park would move its full neighborhood K–5 boundary to
Scott and its Vietnamese Dual Language Immersion program to Vestal in 2028-2029.
Vietnamese DLI students would retain the Roseway Heights Middle School pathway. Special
Education students receiving Learning Center support would continue to receive support at
Scott K–5.

Transportation options and barriers
Transportation options include walking, biking, family transportation, and school bus service for
elementary students who live more than one mile from their neighborhood school; additional
students may qualify based on Oregon Department of Education guidelines or individual needs.
We do not anticipate any barriers to students getting to school; however, additional bussing
may be provided, as needed, for hazard areas within the school attendance boundary.

How will this school location accommodate the reassigned students?​
Scott has 27 regular classrooms (and two modular classrooms) and a functional capacity of
633 students. With a combined enrollment of 489, Scott will be able to accommodate the
reassigned students. Vestal has 23 regular classrooms (and three modular classrooms) and a
functional capacity of 516 students. With a combined enrollment of 444, Vestal will be able to
accommodate the reassigned students.

3. Community/Cluster and District Impact Analysis

3a. Cluster Analysis

How will the proposed closure maintain or improve equity of access to schools offering high
quality educational choices?
In the 2026-27 school year PPS staffs and supports 56 elementary programs across the district.
Eleven of these programs are situated within K-8 campuses. The enrollment of these programs
ranges from 160 students to over 550 students. Using the QEM and our system definition of
“well-resourced schools” as a guide, we have established an elementary enrollment of 360 as
the minimum threshold to be able to staff and support a comprehensive and stable elementary
program. Maximizing the number of sites at or above this minimum threshold allows the district
to maximize the number of students who experience grade-level classrooms, comprehensive
core enrichments, and targeted academic supports. It also maximizes the number of educators
who work full-time on one campus and who benefit from regular collaboration with grade- and
content-alike colleagues.

By operating fewer elementary schools and spreading current enrollment more evenly across
remaining sites, the district can stabilize and offer a more comprehensive and sustainable
program at each elementary site.

In recent years, we have used resources from other parts of our system to subsidize elementary
programs with low enrollment. Given our current and projected budget shortfalls, we can no
longer sustain subsidies for underenrolled sites. As a result, elementary schools have already
faced several programmatic and operational constraints:
    ●​ Over half of our elementary programs have been forced to place students in multi-grade
        classrooms;
    ●​ Staffing for instructional coaching, academic coaching, and school climate support
        positions have been reduced;
    ●​ Core enrichment teachers that support Arts, PE, and Library/Media instruction have been
        split across multiple sites, reducing the capacity to have full-school enrichment
        programs;
    ●​ Support staff that help monitor lunch and recess have been reduced, forcing principals
        to spend much of their time monitoring duty;
    ●​ Opportunities for strong teacher collaboration and professional learning have been
        compromised by multi-grade classrooms and single-strand programs.

If we continue with no rightsizing action, all of these constraints will be compounded in the
2027-2028 school year.

3b. Effects on Other Schools, Enrollment, and Community Access
The proposed merger would affect Scott and Vestal within the Leodis V. McDaniel cluster. Rose
City Park’s neighborhood students would attend Scott, and its Vietnamese Dual Language
Immersion (DLI) program would relocate to Vestal. Enrollment at Scott is projected to change
from approximately 500 in 2026-2027 to 324 in 2027-2028 then to 489 in the 2028–2029 school
year. In 2027, based on current enrollment projections, there will be two classrooms at each
grade level, with blends not required at Scott. All classrooms will be neighborhood English
Scholar classrooms. In 2028, enrollment will continue to increase as students from Rose City
Park join the Scott community.

Enrollment at Vestal is projected to change from 283 to 444 in 2028-2029. Based on current
enrollment projections, this program relocation will result in three to four classrooms at each
grade level, with some number of blends likely. These changes would support combined school
communities, with opportunities to stabilize staffing and sustain educational programs and
student supports while preserving Vietnamese language instruction. Implementation will require
coordinated staffing, classroom assignments, and use of shared spaces.

Scott is ready to receive students, as its 2028-2029 projected enrollment is actually less than its
current enrollment, with no specific facility upgrades required before their arrival. Vestal requires
moderate kitchen upgrades, including double ovens estimated at $27,000, along with
appropriate classrooms and instructional resources for the incoming Vietnamese DLI program.

Rose City Park is located approximately 1.5 miles from Scott and 2.3 miles from Vestal. Nearby
community resources include Wellington Park near Scott and Montavilla Park and Montavilla
Community Center in the broader Vestal area. The proposed change would shift students’ daily
school activities toward their receiving schools. Transportation options include walking, biking,
family transportation, and school bus service for eligible students. Travel distances and
potential barriers would vary by home address and receiving-school assignment, with
transportation provided for Vietnamese DLI students at Vestal via a hub model for students who
attend from multiple neighborhoods. PPS would support school bus service for eligible students
who live more than one mile from Scott. Both schools would be able to work with the district
Safe Routes to School coordinator to identify appropriate crossing, pedestrian, and bicycle
safety measures.

3c. Community Partnerships and Potential Community Impact
Rose City Park partners with Grow Portland, which teaches a gardening class during the school
day. Closure could affect student access to this programming. Rose City Park students are
projected to transition to Vestal, which does not offer Grow Portland. Opportunities for the
partnership to continue, relocate, or adapt include bringing the program to Vestal if the school
has a garden that can support it. Services requiring alternatives or further coordination include
confirming whether Vestal has a school garden; if it does not, the program may be relocated to
another school that does. Additional community impacts include a possible gap in gardening
instruction for transitioning students, with proposed responses including working with Vestal’s
building leaders and school community to determine student needs and readiness to support
this partnership.

Rose City partners with a child care provider serving approximately 60 students and families.
Relocating these students to Scott, which currently serves approximately 50 students, and

Vestal, which serves approximately 52, could bring combined enrollment across the three

Additionally, 13 families across the three schools currently receive ERDC (Employment Related

Rose City Park partners with Immigrant and Refugee Community Organization (IRCO) to operate
its SUN Community School, providing community-based services and wraparound family
supports through braided funding among Portland Public Schools, Multnomah County, and the
City of Portland. IRCO provides free extended-day enrichment opportunities, including art, chess
club, tennis, Vietnamese tutoring, ukulele, student leadership, and LEGO activities, as well as
free summer day camps, access to a food pantry and other basic-needs resources, and
culturally and linguistically responsive programming supported by staff who reflect and
understand the student population.

SUN also serves as an important connection point for students and families to access
individualized support, community resources, enrichment opportunities, and connections to
housing, utility assistance, and other social services. Closure could affect student and family
access to established relationships with SUN staff, enrichment providers, and community
resources, as well as require adjustments to programming locations, partner operations, and
access to existing supports.

Opportunities for partnerships to continue include transitioning students and services to Vestal
and Scott, both of which operate SUN Community Schools. Vestal is operated by IRCO, creating
an opportunity to maintain greater organizational continuity, preserve existing service
connections, and coordinate student and family transitions within an established service
system.

Scott is operated by Latino Network, meaning students and families transitioning there would
experience a change in lead agency. Although SUN services would remain available, this
transition may require families to establish new relationships with SUN staff and navigate
different community partnerships, programming, and family-support coordination.

Services requiring further coordination include assessing Vestal and Scott's capacity to
accommodate additional students and service needs, coordinating SUN staffing and program
resources, maintaining access to existing enrichment partners and basic-needs supports,
transferring family referrals and individualized support needs, and ensuring students can
continue receiving wraparound services without interruption.

Additional community impacts include the potential disruption of trusted relationships with
IRCO staff, changes in how families access culturally and linguistically responsive community
resources, and the loss of established neighborhood connections, with proposed responses
including coordinating with IRCO, Latino Network, and receiving-school leadership to develop a
transition plan, establish warm handoffs between agencies, identify staffing and resource
