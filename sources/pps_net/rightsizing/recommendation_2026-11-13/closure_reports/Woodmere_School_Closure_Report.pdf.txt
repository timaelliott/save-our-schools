1a. School Description
Woodmere Elementary School is a K–5 neighborhood school in Southeast Portland’s
Brentwood-Darlington neighborhood. The school employs varied teaching approaches,
scheduled early reading and mathematics intervention, and support through special education,
English language instruction, and Talented and Gifted services, as well as Communication
Behavior classrooms. Woodmere is a single-story campus, built in 1954, with 19 regular
classrooms and four modular classrooms. Approximately 220 students are enrolled at
Woodmere for the 2026-2027 school year.

1b. Financial Impact
The primary financial benefit of consolidating elementary schools and programs is to align
school capacity and operating costs with enrollment, allowing PPS to concentrate limited
resources in fewer, more efficiently resourced schools. Combining school communities can
reduce duplicative school-level administrative and operating expenses, improve building
utilization, and support more efficient staffing and program delivery. These consolidations are
intended to generate ongoing savings while supporting strong core instructional programs and
student services.

If the school remains open, the projected cost per student for the 2028-2029 school year is:
$23,201. (This is calculated using projected FY28 compensation levels and current staffing
practices, which could change in the near future due to budget uncertainty.)

If the school is closed in 2028, the projected overall savings is $1,100,925. This includes
savings from administrative, custodial, and office staffing, 80% of utilities, and includes the
one-time cost of teacher moves. We anticipate ongoing expenses, including 20% of current
utility costs and an average annual security cost, if the school were closed and unoccupied.

To accommodate the students who live more than one mile from Whitman, PPS anticipates
increasing service to Whitman from one bus to two buses. The Transportation Department
estimates an additional annual savings of approximately $110,000. The proposed change would
reduce the total number of buses serving the closing and receiving schools, resulting in
transportation cost savings.

Transportation and Safe Routes to School planning will identify needed supports for safe arrival
and dismissal, including walking and biking improvements, crossing supports, or traffic
circulation changes.

History of Requested and Actual Transfers
During the 2024-2025, 2025-2026, and 2026-2027 school years, 44 students requested transfers
into Woodmere and 26 enrolled through an approved transfer. During the same period, 162
students residing within the school’s attendance area requested transfers to other schools, and
53 enrolled elsewhere through an approved transfer.

Projected declining enrollment will make it difficult to maintain stable staffing, full programming,
and essential student support services. Under enrollment-based staffing allocations, low
enrollment can result in year-to-year staffing changes, blended-grade classrooms or combined
programs, and reduced access to enrichment and student support offerings. Staff may serve
multiple roles or split time across schools, limiting continuity and availability. While educators
would continue working to meet students’ needs, projected enrollment would constrain the
school’s ability to provide consistent access to a well-rounded educational experience without
ongoing supplemental district resources.

Gross capacity is the estimated maximum number of students a school can accommodate,
calculated by assigning a student capacity to each classroom based on its square footage and
summing those capacities:
    ●​ Less than 800 square feet: 24 students
    ●​ 800–1,000 square feet: 27 students
    ●​ More than 1,000 square feet: 30 students

Functional capacity is a practical estimate of the number of students a school can
accommodate, calculated by multiplying its gross capacity by the applicable utilization rate. It
does not represent maximum occupancy under building code requirements or determine
staffing levels.

Physical Condition of Building, Capital and Environmental Needs
Woodmere is a single-story, H-shaped wood-frame school with red brick cladding, gable roofs,
and an interior garden. While a recent roof replacement is near completion through the 2025
bond program, other facility assessments have identified aging mechanical and plumbing
systems; signage identifying asbestos-containing pipe and equipment insulation; and
approximately 85% of electrical distribution panels beyond design life.

ADA Accessibility
Woodmere is a single story building with accessible entrances.

Building Use
Woodmere houses a K–5 neighborhood school and Communication Behavior classrooms, with
academic intervention, language learning, special education, and student support services.

Analysis of current and potential use of the facility, including recommended disposition
Following closure, potential uses of the Woodmere facility will be evaluated through the
district’s Building Repurposing process, informed by community engagement, district needs,
facility conditions, and financial considerations. This process will consider opportunities to
support students and the surrounding community and identify a recommended future use or
disposition for Board consideration.

2b. Student Reassignment
The proposed closure of Woodmere would shift its full neighborhood K–5 boundary to Whitman.
Special Education students receiving Learning Center support would move to Whitman K-5 and
join their Inclusive School Community. Special Education CB students would either move to a CB
class at Kelly K-5 or move with their general education peers to Whitman K-5 and join their
Inclusive School Community - this will be determined on a case-by-case basis.

Transportation options and barriers
Transportation options include walking, biking, family transportation, and school bus service for
elementary students who live more than one mile from their neighborhood school; additional
students may qualify based on Oregon Department of Education guidelines or individual needs.
We do not anticipate any barriers to students getting to school; however, additional bussing
may be provided, as needed, for hazard areas within the school attendance boundary.

How will this school location accommodate the reassigned students?
Whitman has 24 classrooms and a functional capacity of 416 students. With a combined
enrollment of 444, Whitman will likely need to re-evaluate the three classrooms dedicated to
early childhood to be able to accommodate the reassigned students and provide designated
flexible learning spaces for students with disabilities.

3. Community/Cluster and District Impact Analysis

3a. Cluster Analysis

How will the proposed closure maintain or improve equity of access to schools offering high
quality educational choices?
In the 2026-2027 school year PPS staffs and supports 56 elementary programs across the
district. Eleven of these programs are situated within K-8 campuses. The enrollment of these
programs ranges from 160 students to over 550 students. Using the QEM and our system
definition of “well-resourced schools” as a guide, we have established an elementary enrollment
of 360 as the minimum threshold to be able to staff and support a comprehensive and stable
elementary program. Maximizing the number of sites at or above this minimum threshold allows
the district to maximize the number of students who experience grade-level classrooms,
comprehensive core enrichments, and targeted academic supports. It also maximizes the
number of educators who work full-time on one campus and who benefit from regular
collaboration with grade- and content-alike colleagues.

By operating fewer elementary schools and spreading current enrollment more evenly across
remaining sites, the district can stabilize and offer a more comprehensive and sustainable
program at each elementary site.

In recent years, we have used resources from other parts of our system to subsidize elementary
programs with low enrollment. Given our current and projected budget shortfalls, we can no
longer sustain subsidies for underenrolled sites. As a result, elementary schools have already
faced several programmatic and operational constraints:
    ●​ Over half of our elementary programs have been forced to place students in multi-grade
        classrooms;
    ●​ Staffing for instructional coaching, academic coaching, and school climate support
        positions have been reduced;
    ●​ Core enrichment teachers that support Arts, PE, and Library/Media instruction have been
        split across multiple sites, reducing the capacity to have full-school enrichment
        programs;
    ●​ Support staff that help monitor lunch and recess have been reduced, forcing principals
        to spend much of their time monitoring duty;
    ●​ Opportunities for strong teacher collaboration and professional learning have been
        compromised by multi-grade classrooms and single-strand programs.

3b. Effects on Other Schools, Enrollment, and Community Access
The proposed merger would affect Whitman within the Franklin cluster. Enrollment at Whitman
is projected to change from 192 to 444 in 2028. Based on projections, there will be two to three
classrooms at each grade level. Whitman would require minimal upgrades to the kitchen (a
warming cabinet). Classroom assignments, student support spaces, and use of shared areas
would be coordinated to accommodate incoming students while supporting students already
enrolled. As noted above, Whitman and the Early Learners team will likely need to re-evaluate
the three classroom dedicated to the PPS Pre-K program to be able to accommodate the
reassigned students

Woodmere is located approximately 0.7 mile from Whitman Elementary School. Nearby
community resources include Flavel Park and Brentwood-Darlington Community Center, with
Holgate Library serving the broader area. The proposed change would shift students’ daily
school activities toward Whitman and Flavel Park. Transportation options include walking,
biking, family transportation, and school bus service for eligible students. Potential barriers
include longer walking distances and crossings of SE Flavel Street and other busy streets,
depending on students’ home addresses. PPS would support school bus service for students
who live more than one mile from the school and work with the school community and the
district Safe Routes to School coordinator to identify appropriate crossing, pedestrian, and
bicycle safety measures.

3c. Community Partnerships and Potential Community Impact
Woodmere does not have Racial Equity & Social Justice (RESJ) partner programming. Closure is
not expected to affect RESJ partner services. PPS will continue working with building leaders
and school communities at receiving schools so that partnerships reflect what students and
families need. Future community use of the facility would be considered through the Building
Repurposing process.

Woodmere does not partner with a child care provider, as there has not been an identified need
for on-site child care within its school community. Therefore, closure would not directly affect
an existing child care partnership or require the relocation of a program. However, community
needs may change over time, and we remain open to reassessing family demand and exploring
partnerships with qualified child care providers if a need emerges. Any future program would
require consideration of available space, licensing, staffing, and operational resources. We
encourage the district to remain open to future opportunities to establish on-site child care if
demand increases, ensuring that students and families have access to this important resource
when needed.

Woodmere partners with Impact NW to operate its SUN Community School, providing
community-based services and wraparound family supports through collaboration among PPS,
Multnomah County, and the City of Portland. SUN provides free extended-day enrichment
opportunities, access to food resources, educational support, family engagement opportunities,

and connections to social, health, and mental health resources. Additional services specific to
Woodmere include a weekly family food backpack program providing groceries for entire
households, parent and family cafés connecting families with school staff and social work
supports, community events and resource fairs, and assistance navigating basic-needs
resources. These services create an established connection point for students and families to
access educational enrichment, food assistance, family support, and community resources.
Closure could affect student and family access to these services, disrupt established
relationships with SUN staff and community partners, and require adjustments to programming
locations and service delivery.

Opportunities for partnerships to continue include transitioning students and services to
Whitman, which also operates an Impact NW-led SUN Community School. Having the same
community-based organization operating SUN at both schools creates an opportunity to
maintain service continuity, preserve established relationships, coordinate family transitions,
and potentially integrate existing programming and partnerships into Whitman's service delivery
model.

Services requiring further coordination include assessing Whitman's capacity to accommodate
additional students and families, maintaining access to food assistance and the family
backpack program, coordinating family engagement and resource navigation supports, and
identifying opportunities to continue existing enrichment programming and community
partnerships.

Additional community impacts include potential disruptions to food access, family engagement,
and trusted relationships with school-based support staff, as well as increased demand on
Whitman's existing SUN resources. Proposed responses include coordinating with Impact NW
and Whitman school leadership to develop a transition plan, assess staffing and program
capacity, identify opportunities to maintain Woodmere's existing services and partnerships,
transfer family referrals and support needs, and communicate alternative access points for
families relying on SUN resources. Particular consideration should be given to maintaining
access to food assistance and ensuring families experience a seamless transition into
Whitman's existing community-based service system. Future community use of the facility
would be considered through the Building Repurposing process.
