Frequently Asked Questions

Part of the Rightsize. Right Support. Right for Students. process.

This page is your central resource for answers to common questions about the rightsizing process, including what to expect, how decisions are made, and how this work may impact students and communities. The FAQ section is updated weekly as new questions emerge through community engagement, click the “+” icons to expand and view responses.

For feedback or inquiries, please fill out the Rightsize Feedback Survey. You can stay connected by sharing your email address to receive updates, engagement opportunities, and key milestones throughout the process.

How Did We Get Here?

  • *NEW* How reliable are the district’s enrollment projections, and how do they account for local housing developments, construction permits, post-pandemic trends, Preschool for All, and potential student flight?
  • What are the key factors that led to the district considering merging schools/programs?
  • What main problems is the district trying to solve with this process?
  • Why did the district spend money moving programs around if enrollment was already dropping?
  • Is there a plan to ask state leaders for better, more reliable school funding?
  • How will school mergers lead to greater sustainability and stability?

*NEW* How reliable are the district’s enrollment projections, and how do they account for local housing developments, construction permits, post-pandemic trends, Preschool for All, and potential student flight?

PPS relies on projections by the Population Research Center at  Portland State University. These projections have proven reliable in the past and are used by many government agencies for planning purposes. 

What are the key factors that led to the district considering merging schools/programs?

Birth rates in Portland dropped by 30% over the last decade (Population Research Center, PSU), leaving many school buildings underenrolled. Combining smaller programs can help create larger, more stable schools with more comparable programming and staffing across the city. 

What main problems is the district trying to solve with this process?

Fewer children are being enrolled in Portland Public Schools due to a number of factors. When enrollment drops, staffing and resources can become spread too thin, which can lead to fewer programs, more part-time staff, and different student experiences from school to school.Through Rightsizing, the district is working to increase stability in staffing, balance resources and improve the student learning experience across the city, especially for historically underserved students. 

Why did the district spend money moving programs around if enrollment was already dropping?

Past changes were made to balance student enrollment across local neighborhoods and make better use of building spaces and resources. Our current Rightsizing process takes a broader, long-term look across the entire city to create lasting stability, balance and sustainability. 

Is there a plan to ask state leaders for better, more reliable school funding?

Yes, district leaders regularly work with state lawmakers to advocate for stronger public school funding. At the same time, PPS must balance its current budget with available state funding.

How will school mergers lead to greater sustainability and stability?

Bringing students together can create stronger, more consistent school experiences. Schools with more stable enrollment may be better able to offer a wider range of classes, programs, activities, and student supports, while creating more stable staffing over time. This can give students more consistent access to the opportunities and relationships that support learning and belonging.

*NEW* How can PPS plan responsibly for fewer students today while also working to attract and retain more students in the future?

Declining enrollment is largely due to factors beyond PPS control, such as falling birth rates, and is a growing concern for school districts across Oregon and around the U.S. Nonetheless, PPS remains committed to its outreach efforts, such as Connect to Kindergarten, to attract more students to the district.

*NEW* If our school merges, DLI program moves, or our boundary changes, what options do families have to keep siblings together?

For boundary changes, there are district policies and directives that inform the enrollment and transfer process and we continue to consult them as we finalize the guidelines for Rightsizing. You can review them here:

  • 4.10.045-P Student Assignment to Neighborhood Schools Provides a guarantee for younger siblings living in a neighborhood approved for a boundary change to attend the former neighborhood school if an older sibling will attend
  • 4.10.049-AD Student Assignment Review & School Boundary Changes Allows guaranteed space to the former neighborhood school for younger siblings in a neighborhood approved for boundary change if an application is submitted and older sibling will attend

More information on the options and process for siblings to remain enrolled together will be included in the Superintendent's recommendation.
Enrolling in a DLI program will continue to require a lottery application and language assessment. If a student meets eligibility requirements, and they have a sibling already enrolled in the DLI program, they will also have a sibling preference in the lottery.
 

*NEW* How can community members advocate for broader systemic solutions or education funding directly with local, state, and federal representatives?

You can advocate by sharing your perspective with elected officials about the importance of stable, adequate funding for PPS. This may include contacting your legislators, providing public testimony, or participating in advocacy opportunities. Learn more and find resources on how to engage using our PPS Advocacy Toolkit. 

*NEW* What lottery preferences or guarantees will be included for students impacted by Rightsizing?

 Through our extensive engagement process, we will be listening to community members to determine the inclusion or exclusion of lottery preferences or guaranteed rights for students impacted by Rightsizing, that may be needed to address specific community or student needs.

When would changes happen?

Implementation of Board approved changes will begin no later than the 2027-28 school year and will be completed within three years. PPS will use the time before implementation to support students, staff, and school communities through the transition. A full implementation timeline will be shared publicly before the Board makes a final decision. 

What research or best practices are being used to guide school closures and boundary changes?

PPS is drawing from national research, state guidance, and lessons learned from districts including Denver, Seattle, and Austin. In addition, PPS also commissioned an Equity Audit of its most recent district-led balancing initiative, Southeast Enrollment and Program Balancing. Those findings can be found on the SEGC Documents page.  

What is the step-by-step timeline, and when will the school Board share a final plan?

The Superintendent will provide a formal recommendation to the board in November. The board will then vote in December after community input, and school changes will begin taking effect in fall 2027.

Through what specific channels will closure lists, meeting notices, and district updates be distributed to the public?

All relevant materials and information can be found on our Rightsizing web page.

Who makes the final decision?

The Superintendent will make a recommendation after reviewing data and community feedback. The school board will make the final decision in a public meeting in December. 

How will new school boundaries and attendance maps be drawn?

If boundaries need to change, PPS will look at where students live, school size and space, programs, transportation, and how students move through schools from elementary to high school. PPS will also review the equity impacts of proposed boundaries and gather community feedback before final decisions are made.

Factors Impacting Decisions

  • *NEW* How are physical facility conditions, recent or planned bond-funded seismic retrofits, ADA accessibility, and historical capital investments weighted in closure determinations?
  • What are the core rules (guiding principles) being used to decide which schools to consolidate or close?
  • How will budget data, building safety, and earthquake risks factor into which schools close?
  • How does closing neighborhood schools fit with the district's equity goals?

*NEW* How are physical facility conditions, recent or planned bond-funded seismic retrofits, ADA accessibility, and historical capital investments weighted in closure determinations?

The conditions of school buildings, including seismic stability and ADA accessibility were all important factors in determining which schools were proposed for closure and which were proposed as receiving facilities.

What are the core rules (guiding principles) being used to decide which schools to consolidate or close?

On May 26, 2026, the Board adopted four guiding principles for Rightsizing: create sustainable and well-resourced schools; support safe and healthy learning environments; maintain a strong network of neighborhood schools while balancing educational options; and use the PPS Equity Lens to center students most impacted by inequities. No single factor will determine whether a school is changed or closed.

How will budget data, building safety, and earthquake risks factor into which schools close?

PPS will consider many types of information, including but not limited to enrollment, building use, operating costs, building condition, and earthquake risk. Building condition and safety information will help PPS understand the benefits, challenges, and investments connected to each scenario, but no single measure will determine whether a school closes.

How does closing neighborhood schools fit with the district's equity goals?

Every plan is reviewed using the PPS Racial Equity Lens.

School Experience

  • What extra help and support will be provided for families and students impacted by closures or boundary changes?
  • What is PPS’s specific operational definition of a "well-resourced school" regarding staffing ratios, specialized programming, support services, and learning environments?
  • How will specialized programs—like Dual Language Immersion (DLI), Special Education, and TAG—be impacted and protected?
  • Could School Improvement Bond funds be redirected to cover operational expenses and prevent school closures and boundary changes?

What extra help and support will be provided for families and students impacted by closures or boundary changes?

PPS will provide additional support to help students, families, and staff through any changes. This will include transition planning with affected school communities, with a focus on helping students feel welcomed, connected, and ready for their new school. Specific supports will be based on the needs of each community and will be developed in collaboration with affected students, families, and staff.

What is PPS’s specific operational definition of a "well-resourced school" regarding staffing ratios, specialized programming, support services, and learning environments?

A well-resourced school includes:

  • Increasingly stable staffing that allows for grade-level classrooms, teacher team collaboration, and less educators split across multiple buildings or roles.
  • Access to a full range of enrichment and elective offerings, athletics, and academic and intervention supports as needed.
  • Access to culturally specific partnerships, mental health and nursing supports, and family and community resources. 
  • Learning within a facility that supports core and enrichment programming. 
  • Safe, healthy, comfortable facilities that are in the best of condition.
     

How will specialized programs—like Dual Language Immersion (DLI), Special Education, and TAG—be impacted and protected?

We look at specialized programs as full learning experiences from PK-12, not just single classrooms. No matter which changes are recommended, PPS will make sure every student gets the required services and support they need.

Could School Improvement Bond funds be redirected to cover operational expenses and prevent school closures and boundary changes?

No. School Improvement Bond Program funding is dedicated to school improvement and modernization projects as described by voter-approved ballot language that Portland voters passed. Legally, this funding must be spent on projects that are covered by the ballot language.
 
Bond program funding is separate from the fund that supports PPS's operational expenses. Bond-funded projects do not 'compete for' or 'divert' funding that would otherwise contribute to PPS's other budget priorities. Learn more.

*NEW* How will community feedback actually be processed, analyzed, and integrated into decision-making beyond automated responses?

Members of the PPS Rightsizing team attend all listening sessions and other engagement meetings. They also read comments submitted through our Rightsizing Survey. All comments become part of the conversation as the team works toward a recommendation to the school board.

*NEW* Through what platforms (e.g., in-person listening sessions, online portals, hybrid meetings) can parents submit feedback and ask questions?

PPS has scheduled ongoing engagement sessions throughout November. You may learn more and sign up here. Additionally, you may submit questions or feedback by going to our Rightsizing web page. The specific feedback form can be found here. 

*NEW* How will PPS ensure accessible, multi-channel communication for non-English-speaking families and those without digital access (e.g., paper notices, text updates, translated flyers)?

PPS offers translations for all public facing materials in its five supported languages (Spanish, Mandarin, Somali, Russian and Vietnamese).

How will the district inform and gather input from the community about potential closures and boundary changes?

PPS will share scenarios, maps, data, and updates publicly and provide several ways for students, families, staff, and community members to give input. Feedback gathered through meetings, surveys, and other engagement will be reviewed as scenarios are studied and refined before the Superintendent makes a recommendation to the school board in November.

Where can parents find simple, clear updates on key dates and decision points?

Families can visit pps.net/rightsizing for regular updates, key dates, and important resources. News updates are also shared through school newsletters and emails from the district.

How are student voices and ideas being gathered and included in this process?

Student feedback is gathered through the District Student Council, the Student Voice Advisory Committee, and school listening sessions. Their input on school climate, student experience, and programs help guide school board decisions and implementation planning. Additionally, there is a student representative on the Portland Public School Board.
