Please note that this document is a draft framework developed by one school board
member, Christy Splitt, and offered in the spirit of compromise between those who’d
prefer to make even fewer changes and those who would prefer to make more. At this
point, it is a living document as feedback is incorporated and corrections are made. PPS
staff have not yet reviewed this document, but community members have requested to
see it and so it is being shared in draft, unvetted form. A list of changes from the original
posting of this document on 10/23/26 can be found at the end of the document. If you
have questions or comments, please email Christy Splitt at csplitt@pps.net.

The primary goal of Scenario D versus Scenarios A and B is to reduce the overall number of
changes to the district at one time while still moving the district toward more sustainable and
equitable schools. It also attempts to find middle ground between board members who would
prefer to see no or fewer mergers, with others who would prefer to see more. Some changes

are necessary now, especially given past inaction to address declining enrollment and the
achievement gap, and the $65 million budget deficit. Future additional changes can benefit from
more time, clarity, and conversation. Scenario D utilizes or makes modest adjustments to
Scenarios A and B.
         AF
To objectively narrow the list of proposed mergers, Scenario D combines schools with
the fewest students with neighboring schools. Why focus on the smallest schools? Schools
with the fewest students cost more per student, because of many costs that remain the same
per school despite size of population. At the same time, these smaller schools often have fewer
resources and support. There are 16 PPS buildings projected to have 250 or fewer students in
the fall of 2027. Scenario D reduces that number to four. Scenario D attempts to give all
communities the option to transition together, though does suggest phased-in boundary
changes to even out enrollment numbers over time in some cases.
        R
Scenario D also centers seismic safety by closing several buildings identified as needing
expensive seismic retrofits, including two unreinforced masonry schools - the most dangerous.
Information from the PPS Seismic & Seismic Risk Assessments is used below to demonstrate
how this was considered for each school merger.
D

Additionally, Scenario D in most cases continues to move the district away from K-8s and
toward comprehensive middle schools that can offer more courses and more support for
students who need it. It also includes some middle school shifts to ensure middle schools have
over 400 students.

Under Scenario D, approximately 1231 students (~37.5% experiencing poverty) would move
from a closed school and another approximately 1670) would change schools or attend a
different school than expected. This means that about 10% of students would experience a
change in school or change in planned school, versus the 27% and 24% of students moving
under Scenario A and B. An estimated 37 of the district’s 81 schools experience some change
as a result of Scenario D.
In addition to the closures and changes below, the introduction of Scenario D will be
accompanied by a set of policy proposals, which are summarized at the end of this document.
This includes information required for all scenarios prior to a board vote, suggested board policy
around buildings and building upgrades, and a variety of next steps to continue needed
conversations around future changes that can lead to stronger, more sustainable, and more
equitable schools.

Please note that this framework is based on information that is not granular enough to provide
metrics on underserved racial groups and multiple language learners or poverty rates for the
students affected by changes outside of the school mergers. Further analysis must be done for
this scenario to be fully compared to the others, which will require district collaboration.

Associated Policy Proposals

Proposed Closed Building Parameters:
Board policy should make it clear that closed schools cannot become charter or private K-12
schools. They must be well maintained for either future use (for example, as a swing school for
future school projects) or disposed of according to board policy as quickly as possible, and the
public must be made aware of which buildings are being kept for future use or which will be sold
within one year of a building closure.

Proposed Enrollment Pilot (does not require any PPS action):
Given limited capacity at PPS due to budget cuts, four Wells cluster school communities commit
to fund a three year pilot to test enrollment strategies in SW and for any school with a projected
enrollment of under 300 students, issuing a report to the board or relevant committee every
year, starting fall of 2027. The pilot will be led by a steering committee of representatives from
each participant school, whether or not they contribute to funding the effort.

Proposed Prioritization for Bond and Other Facilities Investments:
The board should resolve that any future facilities investments prioritize seismic retrofits and
HVAC upgrades for any schools that have recently accepted more than 100 students from a
D

closure, and direct that future investments consider how to pair future retrofits and
improvements with potential consolidations. For example, a future bond could call for projects
that allow for something like the Irvington-Beverly Cleary merger, where students will attend a
modernized building together.

Other Considerations:
The board should not vote on any Scenario without projected class size lists and number of
homerooms at the receiving schools, an equity analysis for each change rather than the total
impact and that includes information about special education students and programs, a fiscal
analysis for each change that includes transportation costs, a sample transition plan that clearly
illustrates what students can expect in return for changing schools and how staffing will be
handled - including special education and other student support positions - to ensure quality
change management. Additionally, each merger or change should be assigned a team that
includes a senior district staffer and a district-level special education administrator to serve as
the liaisons between the district, the school, and the community. Scenario D recommends that
PPS staff consider the recommendations in the PPS Rightsizing Analysis prepared by the
Special Education Parent Teacher Student Association in crafting these various plans and
documents.

The District will engage families throughout the 2027 legislative session, when once again PPS
staff and board will advocate for increased school funding, against unfunded mandates, and on
other measures. Generally speaking, the State School Fund is determined immediately
following the May Revenue Forecast, which could allow for additional resources to aid in the
various transitions around the district and avoid or reduce further cuts across all schools.
